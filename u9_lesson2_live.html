AP Statistics Unit 9 Inference for Quantitative Data: Slopes

Topic 9.2: Confidence Intervals for the Slope of a Regression Model

Checking Conditions, Choosing a t-Interval, and Calculating a Confidence Interval for Slope
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Identify the conditions the population regression model must satisfy for valid confidence intervals and significance tests for slope.
  • Describe the shape, center, and variability of the sampling distribution of the slope when those conditions are met.
  • Use sample graphs and data-collection details to check whether inference about slope is appropriate.
  • Explain why a t-interval is used for the slope and how sample statistics estimate unknown population quantities.
  • Calculate a confidence interval for the slope of a population regression line and describe what affects its width.
Essential Knowledge:
  • Inference for slope is based on the population regression model μy = α + βx.
  • Valid confidence intervals and significance tests for slope require a linear relationship, equal standard deviation of y-values across x, and approximate normality of responses for each x-value.
  • When the conditions are met, the sampling distribution of the sample slope b is approximately normal, centered at the true slope β.
  • Its variability is given by σb = σ / (σx√n) when the sample size is no more than 10% of the population size.
  • With only sample data, use s to estimate σ and sx to estimate σx.
  • The correct interval procedure is a t-interval for the slope, using b ± t*SEb with df = n - 2.
  • For Old Faithful in 2019, b = 7.79, SEb = 4.03, t* = 2.069, and the 95% confidence interval is -0.55 to 16.13.
  • Confidence intervals get narrower with larger random samples or lower confidence levels.

Key Vocabulary

Population Regression ModelThe model μy = α + βx describing the mean response for each value of x.
Conditions for InferenceRequirements that must be met before making a confidence interval or significance test for slope.
Linear RelationshipThe true relationship between x and y follows a straight-line pattern rather than a curve.
Equal VarianceThe spread of the y-values stays about the same for all x-values.
Normal ResponsesFor a given x-value, the y-values are approximately normally distributed around the regression line.
Sampling Distribution of bThe distribution of sample slope values from many repeated random samples of the same size.
Standard Deviation of Residuals (s)A sample-based measure of the typical prediction error around the sample regression line.
Sample Standard Deviation of x (sx)A measure of how much the sample x-values vary around their mean.
Standard Error of the SlopeAn estimate of the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of b.
t-Interval for the SlopeThe confidence interval procedure used for estimating the slope of a population regression line.
Critical Value (t*)The t-multiplier that matches the desired confidence level and degrees of freedom.
Margin of ErrorThe amount added and subtracted from the point estimate to form the interval.
10% ConditionWhen sampling without replacement, the sample size must be no more than 10% of the population size to treat observations as independent.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. Inference for slope depends on the population regression model and its conditions.
  2. When the conditions are met, the sampling distribution of b is approximately normal, centered at β.
  3. Sample graphs and collection methods are used to check conditions when the population is unknown.
  4. Because σ and σx are unknown, we estimate them with s and sx and use a t-interval for the slope.
  5. For Old Faithful in 2019, the 95% confidence interval for the slope is -0.55 to 16.13.
