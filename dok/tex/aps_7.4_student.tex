\begin{callout}{\IconBook\ PAIR THE DATA BEFORE YOU TEST (keep on the page)}{calloutgreen}
When the same individual is measured twice, define an order of subtraction and analyze the resulting
differences as one quantitative sample. For $d=\text{new}-\text{old}$, a reduction corresponds to
$H_a:\mu_d<0$; the null is $H_0:\mu_d=0$. With unknown population standard deviation, use a matched-pairs
one-sample $t$-test on the differences. Verify random sampling or random assignment, $n\leq0.10N$ when
sampling without replacement, and an approximately normal sampling distribution. If $n<30$, inspect the
differences themselves for strong skewness and outliers.
\end{callout}
