\focusbanner{TODAY'S PROBLEM --- annotated}

Suppose a company claims 10{,}000 mystery tokens are $50\%$ red, $30\%$ teal, and $20\%$ gold. An SRS of 80 has the shown counts.\par\smallskip \textbf{Leo:} ``Mix and redraw these same 80 tokens without replacement; recompute $\chi^2$ for 1,000 trials.''\par \textbf{Amara:} ``For each trial, generate 80 independent colors with probabilities 0.50, 0.30, and 0.20, then recompute $\chi^2$.'' In Amara's trials, 186 statistics are at least the observed value.\par
\begin{center}
\textbf{Token sample}\par\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Color} & \textbf{Claim} & \textbf{Observed} \\ \hline
\textbf{Red} & $0.50$ & 32 \\ \hline
\textbf{Teal} & $0.30$ & 30 \\ \hline
\textbf{Gold} & $0.20$ & 18 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{firsttakebox}{FIRST TAKE (5 min)}
Which simulation can reveal whether the sample's color discrepancies are unexpected? State what varies in one trial.\par
\answer{Not graded. Both plans use random actions; the key distinction is whether category totals may vary under the null model.}
\end{firsttakebox}

\textit{Rules callout ``BUILD DISCREPANCY FROM THE NULL MODEL (keep on the page)'' is printed on the student sheet.}\par\medskip

\dokbadge{1}~\textbf{(a)} Calculate the three expected counts under the company's claimed color model.\par
\answer{For $n=80$, expected counts are $40$ red, $24$ teal, and $16$ gold.}

\dokbadge{2}~\textbf{(b)} Compute the $\chi^2$ contributions and total, give $df$, estimate the upper tail from Amara's trials, and check 10 percent and expected counts.\par
\answer{The contributions are $1.60$, $1.50$, and $0.25$, so $\chi^2=3.35$ and $df=3-1=2$. The simulated upper tail is $186/1000=0.186$. Also, $80\leq0.10(10{,}000)=1{,}000$, and expected counts 40, 24, and 16 exceed 5.}

\dokbadge{3}~\textbf{(c)} Choose the simulation that answers the chance-variation question and judge surprise at 5 percent. Compare what each trial fixes or varies, use the simulated tail, explain the rejected output's false assurance, and avoid claiming the model is proved.\par
\answer{Amara is valid because each trial lets counts vary under the claimed probabilities while fixing $n=80$. Leo preserves 32 red, 30 teal, and 18 gold, so every trial returns $\chi^2=3.35$; order is irrelevant. Since 186 of 1,000 statistics are at least 3.35, $0.186>0.05$ and the discrepancy is not unexpected. The sample gives no convincing evidence against the mix but does not prove it. Leo's degenerate output would make every observed mix look automatically typical. The theoretical tail is about 0.1873.}
