\begin{callout}{\IconBook\ TABLES FOR A CATEGORICAL VARIABLE (keep on the page)}{calloutgreen}
A \textbf{frequency table} gives the \textbf{count} in each category.
A \textbf{relative-frequency table} gives the \textbf{proportion} (or percent) in each category:
$\text{relative frequency} = \dfrac{\text{count in the category}}{\text{total}}$, times 100 for a percent.
The percents in one table add to 100\%.\\
\textbf{A percent needs its base.} ``83\% said yes'' means nothing until you know 83\% \emph{of how many}.
Counts answer ``how many?''; percents answer ``what share?'' --- use the table that answers the question asked.
\end{callout}
