\begin{callout}{\IconBook\ SCALE EACH GAP, THEN BOUND THE CONCLUSION (keep on the page)}{calloutgreen}
For a chi-square test of independence, $H_0$ says the two categorical variables are not
associated in the population; $H_a$ says they are associated. Calculate
$E=(\text{row total})(\text{column total})/(\text{table total})$ and
$\chi^2=\sum (O-E)^2/E$ with $df=(r-1)(c-1)$. Every expected count should be greater than 5,
and the p-value is the upper-tail probability assuming $H_0$ is true. A cell's contribution
scales its squared gap by $E$, so the largest raw gap need not contribute most. Random sampling
supports population scope; without random assignment, association does not establish causation.
\end{callout}
