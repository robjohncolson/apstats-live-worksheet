\daybanner{Unit 3 \textperiodcentered\ Topic 3.7 \textperiodcentered\ TODAY'S PROBLEM}{Sorting Trays After New Labels}

\noindent\begin{minipage}[t]{0.57\linewidth}
\vspace{0pt}
A cafeteria served 5{,}000 September trays after new sorting-bin labels were installed. The director claims at least $60\%$ are sorted correctly. An SRS of 250 trays finds 136 correct; the club tests at $\alpha=0.05$.\par\smallskip \textbf{Imani:} ``The $p$-value is 0.0354, so reject $H_0$. The labels caused sorting below 60 percent for every month.''\par \textbf{Drew:} ``The 5.6-point gap may be too small to matter, so it is not significant. Accept the director's claim.''\par\smallskip The test conditions have been checked: random sample; $250<500$; null-expected counts 150 and 100.\par
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.40\linewidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\begin{center}
\textbf{September study}\par\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Source} & \textbf{Trays} & \textbf{Correct} & \textbf{$\alpha$} \\ \hline
\textbf{September} & 5,000 & claim: $\geq 60\%$ & -- \\ \hline
\textbf{SRS} & 250 & 136 & 0.05 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{minipage}

\begin{firsttakebox}{FIRST TAKE (5 min, on your sheet)}
Can these September trays tell us what caused the result in every month? Give one reason from the study.
\end{firsttakebox}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.15in, labelwidth=1in, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item[\dokbadge{1}~(a)] Define $p$ and state the hypotheses for checking whether correct sorting is below 60\%.
  \item[\dokbadge{2}~(b)] Calculate $\widehat p$, the one-proportion $z$ statistic, and the lower-tail $p$-value. Use the verified conditions above.
  \item[\dokbadge{3}~(c)] In 4--5 sentences, correct both reports. Compare the $p$-value with $\alpha$, separate statistical significance from practical importance, and limit the conclusion to the people or items studied and what the design can show about cause.
\end{description}
