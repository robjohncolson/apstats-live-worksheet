\begin{callout}{\IconBook\ BINS BEFORE BINOMIAL (keep on the page)}{calloutgreen}
A binomial random variable counts successes across trials that are \textbf{B}inary, plausibly \textbf{I}ndependent,
fixed in \textbf{N}umber, and have the \textbf{S}ame success probability. Define $X$, $n$, and $p$ before calculating.\\
$P(X=x)=\binom{n}{x}p^x(1-p)^{n-x}$. A cumulative event such as $X\leq3$ includes every value from
0 through 3. A small probability makes an outcome unusual under the model; it does not make it impossible or name a cause.
\end{callout}
