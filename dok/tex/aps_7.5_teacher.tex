\focusbanner{TODAY'S PROBLEM --- annotated}

Suppose a help desk closed 1,200 tickets in a quarter. To investigate whether their population mean resolution time was below 12 hours, an analyst took an SRS of 24 and used $\alpha=0.05$.\par\smallskip \textbf{Leah:} ``The lower-tail test gives $t=-1.5747$, $df=23$, and $p\approx0.0645$. I would state what evidence this does and does not provide.''\par \textbf{Damon:} ``Because $p>0.05$, accept $H_0$. The test proves the population mean was exactly 12 hours and the observed difference was only chance.''\par
\begin{center}
\textbf{Ticket sample}\par\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Summary} & \textbf{Value} \\ \hline
\textbf{Population} & 1200 \\ \hline
\textbf{$n$} & 24 \\ \hline
\textbf{Mean (h)} & 11.1 \\ \hline
\textbf{SD (h)} & 2.8 \\ \hline
\textbf{Shape} & Symmetric; no outliers \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{firsttakebox}{FIRST TAKE (5 min)}
Which account is more defensible? Cite the p-value comparison or a claim that a test can or cannot justify.\par
\answer{Not graded. Students should distinguish the formal decision from a claim about the null value.}
\end{firsttakebox}

\textit{Rules callout ``COMPARE EVIDENCE TO THE PRESET THRESHOLD (keep on the page)'' is printed on the student sheet.}\par\medskip

\dokbadge{1}~\textbf{(a)} Define the population mean and state hypotheses for the research question.\par
\answer{Let $\mu$ be the mean resolution time for all 1,200 tickets closed that quarter. $H_0:\mu=12$ hours and $H_a:\mu<12$ hours.}

\dokbadge{2}~\textbf{(b)} Verify random, 10 percent, and shape conditions. Compute $SE$, $t$, $df$, and the lower-tail p-value; interpret the p-value.\par
\answer{The SRS satisfies $24\leq0.10(1{,}200)=120$; the symmetric, outlier-free sample supports shape for $n=24$. $SE=2.8/\sqrt{24}=0.57155$, so $t=(11.1-12)/0.57155=-1.57467$, $df=23$, and $p=0.06449$. If $\mu=12$, the chance of an SRS result at least this low in the direction of $H_a$ is about 0.0645.}

\dokbadge{3}~\textbf{(c)} Adjudicate the accounts. Compare $p$ with $\alpha$, distinguish the formal decision from acceptance or proof, state the scoped population conclusion, and explain the two beliefs produced by the rejected account.\par
\answer{Leah is warranted. Since $0.0645>0.05$, fail to reject $H_0$. The sample does not provide convincing evidence that the mean for all 1,200 tickets was below 12 hours. The p-value assumes $\mu=12$; it is not the probability that assumption is true. Failing to reject says the data are not sufficiently incompatible with 12, not that $\mu=12$ was proved. Chance is plausible but not proved to be the only explanation. The rejected account would falsely report both equality and certainty about chance.}
