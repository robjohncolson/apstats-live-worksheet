Suppose a membership association has 5,000 new members and takes an SRS of 120. Each member is classified by orientation status and preferred support channel. The association may test whether the variables are associated.\par\smallskip \textbf{Nico:} ``Every observed cell is at least 6, and the data came from an SRS, so the usual chi-square setup is ready.''\par \textbf{Priya:} ``The margins may produce a different boundary check; I would calculate that table before deciding.''\par

\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Status} & \textbf{Chat} & \textbf{Email} & \textbf{Phone} & \textbf{Total} \\ \hline
\textbf{Done} & 42 & 34 & 24 & 100 \\ \hline
\textbf{Not done} & 8 & 6 & 6 & 20 \\ \hline
\textbf{Total} & 50 & 40 & 30 & 120 \\ \hline

\textit{Rules callout ``EXPECTED COUNTS UNDER NO ASSOCIATION (keep on the page)'' is printed on the student sheet.}\par\medskip

\dokbadge{1}~\textbf{(a)} Which row total, column total, and table total are needed for Not done/Phone?\par
\answer{The row total is 20, the Phone column total is 30, and the table total is 120.}

\dokbadge{2}~\textbf{(b)} Calculate the expected counts for Done/Chat and Not done/Phone. The other four expected counts are all greater than 5.\par
\answer{Done/Chat: $(100)(50)/120=41.6667$. Not done/Phone: $(20)(30)/120=5$.}

\dokbadge{3}~\textbf{(c)} In 3--4 sentences, decide whose claim follows the printed rule. Compare observed 6 with the expected Phone count, explain why the kind of count matters, and suggest one next step that keeps the same support-channel categories.\par
\answer{Priya is justified: the rule checks expected counts, and the Not-done/Phone expected count is 5, which is not greater than 5. The observed count of 6 does not satisfy a rule about what the no-association model predicts. Under this sheet's stated rule, do not proceed with the usual approximation yet. Collect a larger random sample and recheck the expected counts while retaining the same categories.}
