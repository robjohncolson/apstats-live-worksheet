\focusbanner{TODAY'S PROBLEM --- annotated}

Suppose a city library takes an SRS of 240 of its 6,000 active adult cardholders. It records each person's chosen notification method and whether the person returned an item late in the previous six months.\par\smallskip \textbf{Nia:} ``Email/Late has the largest raw gap, 13.75; I think that cell drives the statistic and the result shows email prevents late returns.''\par \textbf{Omar:} ``Expected counts differ, so raw gaps may not settle cell influence. I would calculate contributions and examine how notification methods were obtained before interpreting.''\par
\begin{center}
\textbf{Late returns}\par\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{Late} & \textbf{Not late} & \textbf{Total} \\ \hline
\textbf{Email} & 15 & 85 & 100 \\ \hline
\textbf{Text} & 24 & 56 & 80 \\ \hline
\textbf{None} & 30 & 30 & 60 \\ \hline
\textbf{Total} & 69 & 171 & 240 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{firsttakebox}{FIRST TAKE (5 min)}
Which account is better supported so far? Name one calculation and one design fact needed to decide.\par
\answer{Not graded. Students should identify both numerical and design evidence still needed.}
\end{firsttakebox}

\textit{Rules callout ``SCALE EACH GAP, THEN BOUND THE CONCLUSION (keep on the page)'' is printed on the student sheet.}\par\medskip

\dokbadge{1}~\textbf{(a)} Give the row totals, column totals, table total, and degrees of freedom.\par
\answer{Row totals are 100, 80, and 60; column totals are 69 and 171; $n=240$; and $df=(3-1)(2-1)=2$.}

\dokbadge{2}~\textbf{(b)} Verify random sampling, 10 percent, and expected counts. Calculate all expected counts, cell contributions, $\chi^2$, and the upper-tail p-value.\par
\answer{The SRS satisfies $240\leq0.10(6{,}000)=600$. Expected counts are Email $(28.75,71.25)$, Text $(23,57)$, and None $(17.25,42.75)$, all greater than 5.\par Contributions are $6.5761,2.6535,0.0435$ and $0.0175,9.4239,3.8026$ in table order. Thus $\chi^2=22.5172$, $df=2$, and $p\approx0.0000129$.}

\dokbadge{3}~\textbf{(c)} Adjudicate the accounts. Compare the Email/Late and None/Late raw gaps and contributions, use the p-value for a population conclusion, bound causation, and state the two consequences of the rejected account.\par
\answer{Omar is warranted. Email/Late has gap $13.75$ but contribution $13.75^2/28.75=6.5761$; None/Late has gap $12.75$ but contribution $12.75^2/17.25=9.4239$, the largest. Since $p\approx0.0000129$, reject no association: chosen method and late return are associated among the 6,000 active adult cardholders. The SRS supports that scope, but methods were not assigned, so prevention is not established. The rejected account both ranks cell influence by unscaled gaps and turns observational association into causation.}
