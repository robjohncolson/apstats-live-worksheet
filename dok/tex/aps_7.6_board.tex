\daybanner{Unit 4 \textperiodcentered\ Topic 4.7 \textperiodcentered\ TODAY'S PROBLEM}{Two Samples of Service Times}

\noindent\begin{minipage}[t]{0.57\linewidth}
\vspace{0pt}
Suppose two depots independently take SRSs of 36 weekday service-time records from monthly populations of 900 North and 1,200 South records. Technology gives $df=69.31$ and $t^*=1.995$ for a 95\% two-sample interval.\par\smallskip \textbf{Tariq:} ``I sorted both samples and paired equal ranks. The resulting differences have $\bar d=2.2$ and $s_d=3.0$, giving $(1.18,3.22)$. Pairing makes the estimate more precise.''\par \textbf{Mei:} ``The records were sampled separately, so I would use a two-sample interval. Sorting observed responses may change the uncertainty rather than reveal design-based pairs.''\par
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.40\linewidth}
\vspace{0pt}

\begin{firsttakebox}{FIRST TAKE (5 min, on your sheet)}
Does sorting the times make the North and South records real pairs? Explain in one sentence.
\end{firsttakebox}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.15in, labelwidth=1in, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item[\dokbadge{1}~(a)] Find the North-minus-South difference in sample mean service times.
  \item[\dokbadge{2}~(b)] The independent-sample standard error is 1.88797 minutes. Use $t^*=1.995$ to calculate the 95\% interval.
  \item[\dokbadge{3}~(c)] In 3--4 sentences, choose a procedure using the original sampling design. Compare the two intervals and explain how sorting the times could mislead a reader about whether the population means differ.
\end{description}
