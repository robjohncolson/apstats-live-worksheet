A library studies self-checkout before and after a redesign. Records give $p_B=0.42$ among 4{,}000 before visits and $p_A=0.54$ among 5{,}000 after visits.\par\smallskip \textbf{Plan I:} Draw an SRS of 120 before visits and a separate SRS of 150 after visits; do not track cardholders.\par \textbf{Plan P:} Measure the same 120 cardholders before and after.\par\smallskip \textbf{Sora:} Separate selection warrants Plan I's independent model; 0.0350 means a difference at most 0.01 is unusual.\par \textbf{Mateo:} Pairing controls person differences, so Plan P may help. \textit{Rules callout ``THE VARIANCE FORMULA REQUIRES INDEPENDENT SAMPLES (keep on the page)'' is printed on the student sheet.}\par\medskip

\dokbadge{1}~\textbf{(a)} For after minus before, identify the parameter and statistic and state Plan I's sampling-distribution mean.\par
\answer{The parameter is $p_A-p_B$, the difference between the after- and before-period population proportions of visits using self-checkout. The statistic is $\widehat p_A-\widehat p_B$. For Plan I, $\mu_{\widehat p_A-\widehat p_B}=p_A-p_B=0.54-0.42=0.12$.}

\dokbadge{2}~\textbf{(b)} For Plan I, check both 10 percent conditions and all four counts. Find the standard deviation and approximate $P(\widehat p_A-\widehat p_B\leq0.01)$; interpret it.\par
\answer{Both 10 percent checks hold: $150<500$ and $120<400$; the four counts are 81, 69, 50.4, and 69.6. The variance is $0.001656+0.002030=0.003686$, so $SD=\sqrt{0.003686}=0.0607124$. Then $z=-1.8118$ and $P(\widehat p_A-\widehat p_B\leq0.01)\approx0.0350$. About 3.5 percent of independent sample pairs would show a difference at most 0.01 when the population difference is 0.12.}

\dokbadge{3}~\textbf{(c)} Adjudicate the claims. Use design and conditions to place the independent model and assess Plan P. Explain why equal sample sizes are not independence, how transferring 0.0607 and 0.0350 could mislead, and what paired analysis must retain.\par
\answer{Sora is correct for Plan I: separate SRSs and passing conditions warrant 0.0607 and 0.0350. Mateo is right that pairing can control stable person differences. But Plan P links each person's outcomes; equal sample sizes do not imply independence. The independent formula omits within-person association and could distort spread and tail probability in either direction. A valid paired analysis keeps each pair or the discordant transitions. Plan I fits the independent model; Plan P can be useful with paired analysis.}
