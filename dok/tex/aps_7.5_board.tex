\daybanner{Unit 4 \textperiodcentered\ Topic 4.5 \textperiodcentered\ TODAY'S PROBLEM}{A Test of Mean Resolution Time}

\noindent\begin{minipage}[t]{0.57\linewidth}
\vspace{0pt}
Suppose a help desk closed 1,200 tickets in a quarter. To investigate whether their population mean resolution time was below 12 hours, an analyst took an SRS of 24 and used $\alpha=0.05$.\par\smallskip \textbf{Leah:} ``The lower-tail test gives $t=-1.5747$, $df=23$, and $p\approx0.0645$. I would state what evidence this does and does not provide.''\par \textbf{Damon:} ``Because $p>0.05$, accept $H_0$. The test proves the population mean was exactly 12 hours and the observed difference was only chance.''\par
\end{minipage}\hfill

\begin{firsttakebox}{FIRST TAKE (5 min, on your sheet)}
Which account is more defensible? Cite the p-value comparison or a claim that a test can or cannot justify.
\end{firsttakebox}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.15in, labelwidth=1in, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item[\dokbadge{1}~(a)] Define the population mean and state hypotheses for the research question.
  \item[\dokbadge{2}~(b)] Verify random, 10 percent, and shape conditions. Compute $SE$, $t$, $df$, and the lower-tail p-value; interpret the p-value.
  \item[\dokbadge{3}~(c)] Adjudicate the accounts. Compare $p$ with $\alpha$, distinguish the formal decision from acceptance or proof, state the scoped population conclusion, and explain the two beliefs produced by the rejected account.
\end{description}
