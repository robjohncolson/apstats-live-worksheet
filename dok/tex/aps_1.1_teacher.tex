\focusbanner{TODAY'S PROBLEM --- annotated}

Suppose a school newspaper records last night's sleep for 12 students in one class. All 12 values are valid. For this class, the school flags a sleep concern if more than $20\%$ slept fewer than 6 hours.\par\smallskip \textbf{Claim 1:} ``The full table shows a sleep concern in this class.''\par \textbf{Claim 2:} ``Leave out the two lowest sleep values and there is no sleep concern.''\par
\begin{center}
\textbf{Sleep last night}\par\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Student} & \textbf{Sleep (hours)} \\ \hline
\textbf{Aiden} & 5.0 \\ \hline
\textbf{Bea} & 7.0 \\ \hline
\textbf{Cruz} & 8.5 \\ \hline
\textbf{Dev} & 6.5 \\ \hline
\textbf{Elise} & 8.0 \\ \hline
\textbf{Fiona} & 7.5 \\ \hline
\textbf{Gabe} & 5.5 \\ \hline
\textbf{Hana} & 9.0 \\ \hline
\textbf{Imani} & 7.0 \\ \hline
\textbf{Jalen} & 6.0 \\ \hline
\textbf{Kira} & 8.0 \\ \hline
\textbf{Leo} & 4.5 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{firsttakebox}{FIRST TAKE (5 min)}
Which claim, if either, would you print? Name one detail behind your choice.\par
\answer{Not graded. Students can choose using the sleep values and the printed threshold before formal vocabulary.}
\end{firsttakebox}

\textit{Rules callout ``USE DATA IN CONTEXT (keep on the page)'' is printed on the student sheet.}\par\medskip

\dokbadge{1}~\textbf{(a)} Name the variable and its unit.\par
\answer{Hours of sleep last night for each student in this class.}

\dokbadge{2}~\textbf{(b)} Find the percent who slept fewer than 6 hours using all 12 students. Then leave out the two lowest values and find the new percent.\par
\answer{All data: $3/12=25\%$. Removing 4.5 and 5.0 hours leaves $1/10=10\%$ below 6 hours.}

\dokbadge{3}~\textbf{(c)} Which claim would you print? Use both percents and the school's rule. Explain why keeping or removing the two values changes what readers learn about this class.\par
\answer{Print Claim 1: $25\%$ exceeds the $20\%$ threshold. Claim 2's $10\%$ is arithmetically correct for the shortened table, but excluding two valid low values changes both the count and the total. It hides part of the sleep concern in the original class.}
