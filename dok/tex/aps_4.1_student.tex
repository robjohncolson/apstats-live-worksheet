\begin{callout}{\IconBook\ MODEL THE PATTERN, NOT THE STORY (keep on the page)}{calloutgreen}
A random process can produce streaks. A simulation uses chance to imitate a process; each trial must match the original number of independent flips and the stated chance of heads. Record the outcome of each trial and calculate $\text{successful trials}/\text{total trials}$. Many trials reduce chance variation in this estimate. A simulation result is evidence about a model, not proof that a real coin is fair.
\end{callout}
