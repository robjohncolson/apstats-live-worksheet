## Patterns across the set

The required gates were rerun before every unit commit: registry validation (70 rows / 66 lessons), both Vitest suites (271 tests), and the Python build suite (471 tests). Staged GitNexus reviews reported low risk and no affected application symbols or execution flows. Final PDF counts and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in [pdf-pages.json](audit-support/pdf-pages.json).

- Repeated calculations, full procedure checklists, and extra redesign questions crowded the central judgment. Supplied intermediate results and shorter parts preserve the evidence-and-justification work while reducing copying and calculator entry.
- Actual video order exposed premature methods, including resistance, expected value, z-scores, paired inference, and theoretical chi-square conditions. Most were removed or replaced with already-taught reasoning; the remaining short bridges are flagged below.
- First takes sometimes required the new vocabulary, while headings and callouts sometimes revealed the answer. Plain questions, neutral labels, complete rules, and usable sentence frames now support the task without making the decision for students.
- Keys needed arithmetic, interpretation, and scoring repairs. Revised E/P/I guides distinguish a complete argument, some substantive correct reasoning with omissions, and no substantive correct link. Raster inspection also caught small labels, overlapping dots, displaced bars, and graph-based questions requiring unavailable exact values.

## Things a human should look at

- **Timing and grading:** The roughly 8-10-minute finish estimates are planning judgments, not an observed classroom trial. Pilot the longest condition-check days (especially 6.2, 6.3, and 6.8), the English-learner frames, and the under-one-minute E/P/I scoring target with this class.
- **7.2 and 7.4 - Alignment FLAG:** The CED tether and printed callout supply paired-difference reasoning before the corresponding 7.5 video. Decide whether that bridge is sufficient on those days; the contexts and competing claims were retained and the long arithmetic was removed.
- **8.4 - Alignment and Callout FLAG:** The boundary judgment uses a printed expected-count rule ahead of the next video. The course also conflicts at the boundary: 8.5 uses strictly greater than 5, while 8.6 exit/cards use at least 5. The problem retains the strict rule pending a teacher decision; the curriculum source files were outside this task's edit scope.
- **1.2 - Rating assumptions:** The key accepts an ordered-category interpretation, or a quantitative interpretation with an explicit equal-step measured-score assumption. Confirm how students should state that assumption; the numerical appearance of stars alone is insufficient.
- **1.7 - Quartile convention:** The problem explicitly excludes the overall median from each half. Confirm that instruction against classroom/calculator practice; other conventions exist, so the key no longer calls one convention uniquely AP.
- **1.3 - Worksheet wording:** The source worksheet calls a majority both "more than half" and "at least 50%." Clarify the exact-50% boundary with students; the DOK task does not require that inconsistent definition, and the worksheet was not edited.
- **5.3, 5.7, and 5.8 - Normal approximation:** 5.3 now says the larger-sample model is more defensible, not guaranteed. The later tasks retain the worksheet's n >= 30 classroom convention; extreme skew can require more information to judge tail accuracy.
- **6.9 - Scope:** Random assignment supports a causal conclusion for the participants. The retained phrase "students like these volunteers" needs a substantive similarity judgment before extending the conclusion; it does not establish a district-wide result.
- **Final viewing:** Check board readability from the actual back row. Topic 4.7 has one teacher-only 3.02225pt overfull identifier line; all three teacher pages were inspected and the full identifier remains visible. No student or board clipping was found in that topic.
