Topic 5.3: The Central Limit Theorem

Sampling Distributions via Simulation & Randomization Distributions
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objective:
  • UNC-3.H: Estimate sampling distributions using simulation.
Essential Knowledge:
  • A sampling distribution of a statistic is the distribution of values for all possible samples of a given size.
  • The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) states that when the sample size is sufficiently large, the sampling distribution of the mean will be approximately normal.
  • A randomization distribution is generated by simulation, repeatedly reassigning response values to treatment groups.

Key Vocabulary

Sampling Distribution The distribution of values for a statistic from all possible samples of a given size from a population
Central Limit Theorem When sample size is sufficiently large, the sampling distribution of the mean will be approximately normal, regardless of population shape
Randomization Distribution A collection of statistics generated by simulation, reassigning response values to treatment groups to model chance alone
Simulation Using repeated random sampling to approximate a sampling distribution
Sample Mean (x) The average of values in a sample; varies from sample to sample

Randomization Distribution

A collection of statistics generated by repeatedly reassigning the response values to treatment groups, with total disregard for where they originally came from.
8. [1:28] Simulation Process: Take all 16 values, randomly assign 8 to each group, calculate means, find the difference. Repeat 1,000 times to build a distribution of differences that could occur by alone.
9. [3:22] The actual experiment's difference of 1.69 hours is located in the tail of the randomization distribution.

Exit Ticket

A researcher wants to test whether a new fertilizer increases plant growth. She randomly assigns 20 plants to receive either the new fertilizer (10 plants) or no fertilizer (10 plants). After 4 weeks, she measures the height increase for each plant.
  • (a) The fertilizer group had a mean growth of 8.5 cm and the control group had 6.2 cm. Calculate the observed difference in means.
  • (b) She creates a randomization distribution by reassigning plants to groups 1,000 times. Only 25 of those simulations produced a difference as large as or larger than her observed difference. Calculate the probability and explain what it means.
  • (c) Based on this probability, what conclusion should she draw about the effectiveness of the fertilizer?
