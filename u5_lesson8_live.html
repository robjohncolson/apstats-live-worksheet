AP Statistics Unit 5 Sampling Distributions

Topic 5.8: Sampling Distributions for Differences in Sample Means

From One Mean to Two — Comparing Populations with x̄1 − x̄2
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • UNC-3.T: Determine parameters of a sampling distribution for a difference in sample means.
  • UNC-3.U: Determine whether a sampling distribution of a difference in sample means can be described as approximately normal.
  • UNC-3.V: Interpret probabilities and parameters for a sampling distribution for a difference in sample means.
Essential Knowledge:
  • The sampling distribution of x̄1 − x̄2 has mean μ1 − μ2 and SD √(σ1²/n1 + σ2²/n2).
  • The sampling distribution is approximately normal when both populations are normal OR when both n1 ≥ 30 and n2 ≥ 30.

Key Vocabulary

Difference in Sample Means x̄1 − x̄2 — the statistic computed by subtracting the sample mean of one group from the sample mean of another
Sampling Distribution The distribution of x̄1 − x̄2 across all possible pairs of random samples of the given sizes from two populations
10% Condition Both sample sizes must be less than 10% of their respective population sizes for the SD formula to be valid
Independence of Samples The two samples must be selected independently of each other (knowing one sample tells you nothing about the other)

Part 2: Formulas & Conditions

[Video 1: 4:08 – 6:44]

Formulas (on your AP formula sheet)

Mean: μx̄1 − x̄2 = μ1 − μ2
SD: σx̄1 − x̄2 = √(σ1²/n1 + σ2²/n2)
4. [5:22] Apply the mean formula: μx̄L − x̄O = 4 − 3 = ounce
5. [5:48] Apply the SD formula: σx̄L − x̄O = √(0.5²/6 + 0.4²/6) = ounces
6. [5:06] Two conditions for the SD formula to be valid: both sample sizes must be less than of their population sizes, and the two samples must be of each other.

Pause & Think

7. Notice the SD formula adds the two fractions under the square root, even though we subtract the means. This is because variability always when you combine independent random variables — uncertainty from both samples contributes to the total spread.

Part 3: Shape — When Is the Distribution Approximately Normal?

[Video 1: 6:44 – 8:28]
Two Paths to Normality (only one needs to hold):
[6:44] Path 1: Both population distributions are approximately normal.
[7:06] Path 2: Both sample sizes are large enough (n1 ≥ 30 and n2 ≥ 30).
8. In the citrus example, both population distributions are approximately normal. This means the sampling distribution of x̄L − x̄O is approximately normal even though n = , which is less than 30.
9. If neither population were normal, we would need both sample sizes to be at least for the sampling distribution to be approximately normal.
