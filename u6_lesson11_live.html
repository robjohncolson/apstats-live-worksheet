AP Statistics Unit 6 Inference for Proportions

Topic 6.11: Carrying Out a Test for the Difference of Two Population Proportions

Calculating the Test Statistic, p-Value, and Conclusion
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • VAR-6.K: Calculate an appropriate test statistic for the difference of two population proportions.
  • DAT-3.C: Interpret the p-value of a significance test for a difference of two population proportions.
  • DAT-3.D: Justify a claim about populations using a decision from a significance test.
Essential Knowledge:
  • The test statistic for a difference in proportions uses the observed difference, the hypothesized difference of 0, and a pooled standard error.
  • The pooled proportion is p̂c = (X1 + X2) / (n1 + n2).
  • Use the standard normal distribution to find the p-value, with the tail(s) determined by the alternative hypothesis.
  • An interpretation of a p-value must assume the null hypothesis is true and describe chance variation from random sampling or random assignment.
  • Compare the p-value to the significance level to reject or fail to reject H0, then state the conclusion in context.

Key Vocabulary

Standardized Test Statistic The value z that compares the observed difference in sample proportions to what is expected under the null hypothesis.
Pooled Proportion The combined success proportion, written as p̂c, used in the standard error for a significance test.
p-Value The probability, assuming the null is true, of getting a result as extreme or more extreme than the observed result.
Right-Tail p-Value Used when the alternative is greater than, such as p1 > p2.
Two-Sided p-Value Used when the alternative is not equal to, so extreme results in both directions count.
Significance Level The cutoff, written as α, used to decide whether the evidence is strong enough to reject the null hypothesis.
Reject H0 A decision made when the p-value is less than or equal to α, supporting the alternative hypothesis.
Fail to Reject H0 A decision made when the p-value is greater than α, meaning the data do not provide convincing evidence for the alternative.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. For a significance test about two population proportions, compute the pooled proportion first and use it in the standard error.
  2. Use the standard normal distribution to turn the z test statistic into a p-value.
  3. The alternative hypothesis determines whether the p-value comes from the left tail, right tail, or both tails.
  4. Interpret the p-value by assuming the null hypothesis is true and describing chance variation.
  5. Compare the p-value to α to reject or fail to reject H0, then state the conclusion in context.
