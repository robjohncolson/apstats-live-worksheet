Binomial Settings
02:51
A binomial setting involves repeated trials of a random process, where the following conditions are met:

Binary: Two possible outcomes: success or failure.

Independent: Independent trials of the same random process.

Number: A fixed number of trials, n.

Success: Each trial has the same probability of success, p.

Binomial Distributions
04:30
In a binomial setting, the random variable X (the number of successes) is called a binomial random variable. And a distribution of that random variable is a binomial distribution.

04:44
How can we find probabilities involving a binomial random variable?

We could use simulation (shown in Topic 4.2 Video 2).

Or we could use the binomial formula.

04:59
The binomial formula for the probability that the random variable X equals a particular value (k) is:
The number of combinations of X out of n times the probability of success (p) to the power of x, times the probability of failure (1-p) to the power of n-x.

Calculating Binomial Probabilities
05:17
Let's solve the example: The probability a tropical storm becomes a hurricane is 0.53. If the weather service predicts 6 more tropical storms, what is the probability that exactly 5 of them become hurricanes?

05:32

Define the random variable: Let X = the number of hurricanes that develop from 6 tropical storms.

Identify the distribution: This is a binomial distribution with n = 6 and p = 0.53. We want to find P(X = 5).

Find the probability: Using the formula:

P(X = 5) = (6 choose 5) * (0.53)^5 * (1 - 0.53)^1

P(X = 5) = 0.118

06:06
The probability that exactly 5 of 6 tropical storms will become hurricanes is 0.118.
