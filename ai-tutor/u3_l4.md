[MCQ] U3-L4-Q01

A school nutritionist was interested in how students at a certain school
would feel after taking a nutritional supplement. The nutritionist selected
a random sample of twenty students from the school to participate in the
study. Participants were asked to keep a journal on how well they felt after
taking the supplement each day. What possible source of bias is present in
the method of data collection?

(A) Bias from a sampling method that only uses volunteers
(B) Undercoverage bias
(C) Nonresponse bias
(D) Response bias where responses are self-reported
(E) Response bias where the question wording is leading or confusing

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The item tests LO DAT-2.E
and EK DAT-2.E.5. The sampling method was random (eliminating A); there is
no mention of excluded groups (eliminating B) or non-returns (eliminating C);
the question wording is not described as leading (eliminating E). The bias
arises because participants self-record their own subjective feelings —
self-reported data is subject to response bias per EK DAT-2.E.5: individuals
may over- or under-report how they feel due to subjective interpretation,
social desirability, or personal perception. The top distractor is A; push
students to notice that random selection was used, so the sample is not
comprised of volunteers (EK DAT-2.E.2 does not apply here).

[MCQ] U3-L4-Q02

A city has designed a survey to collect information about residents' opinions
about city services. Which of the following describes a scenario in which
nonresponse bias is likely present?

(A) Surveys were mailed to 500 people, and 200 of the surveys were completed and returned.
(B) Surveys were mailed to one-half of the neighborhoods in the city and all surveys were returned
(C) Surveys were completed by the first 500 residents who listened to a radio show
(D) Surveys were completed by 500 residents selected at random
(E) Surveys were given to all residents in one neighborhood of the city and all surveys were returned

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The item tests LO DAT-2.E
and EK DAT-2.E.4. Nonresponse bias requires that individuals chosen for the
sample do not respond, and those non-respondents may systematically differ
from respondents. In (A), 300 of 500 selected people never returned the
survey — a 60% nonresponse rate. Those who did not respond may hold
systematically different opinions about city services (e.g., more
dissatisfied residents may be less likely to respond). Options B and E
describe full response (all returned); C and D involve different selection
issues (voluntary/radio listeners and random selection, respectively) but do
not show incomplete responses from a selected sample. The top distractor is
B; students may confuse surveying only half the neighborhoods (undercoverage
concern) with nonresponse — key distinction is that all surveys were
returned in B, so nonresponse bias is absent (EK DAT-2.E.4 requires
non-returns from the chosen sample).

[MCQ] U3-L4-Q03

Which of the following does not describe a sampling method that has a
potential source of voluntary response bias for the administration of a
survey about college athletics at a university?

(A) Giving the survey to 30 students selected at random from each of the eight dorms on campus
(B) Giving the survey to the first 50 students who call the university radio station after the broadcaster announces the survey on air
(C) Giving the survey to any student who stops at a table set up in the student union after the survey is advertised
(D) Giving the survey to all students who respond to an advertisement in the student newspaper
(E) Giving the survey to physical education majors who receive extra credit for completing the survey

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The item tests LO DAT-2.E
and EK DAT-2.E.2. The question asks which option does NOT involve voluntary
response bias. Voluntary response bias requires that participants
self-select — they choose to participate. In (A), students are randomly
selected by the researcher, not by their own choice; they are chosen, not
volunteers (EK DAT-2.E.2 and EK DAT-2.E.6). Options B, C, and D all involve
self-selection: callers respond on their own initiative, students stop by
the table by choice, students opt in to an advertisement. Option E is a
subtler distractor — PE majors receive extra credit, which may motivate
participation, but the key distinction is that those students still choose
to complete it (it is not mandatory). The critical point for the tutor: only
random selection by the researcher (A) breaks the self-selection mechanism
that defines voluntary response bias.
