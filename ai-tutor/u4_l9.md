[MCQ] U4-L9-Q01

At a large regional collegiate women's swim meet, an official records the time it takes each swimmer to swim 100 meters for all swimmers who compete in only one stroke category. The following table shows the mean times and corresponding standard deviations for the collegiate women at the swim meet for each of the four stroke categories. For each of the 4 stroke categories, consider a random variable representing the time of a randomly selected swimmer in that category. What is the standard deviation of the sum of the 4 random variables?

| Stroke Category | Mean 100 meter Time | Standard Deviation |
|---|---|---|
| Backstroke | 55.6 seconds | 0.70 seconds |
| Breaststroke | 63.3 seconds | 0.92 seconds |
| Butterfly | 54.4 seconds | 0.94 seconds |
| Freestyle | 50.2 seconds | 0.76 seconds |

(A) 0.83 seconds
(B) 1.67 seconds
(C) 2.80 seconds
(D) 3.32 seconds
(E) 3.76 seconds

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests EK VAR-5.E.3 — when summing independent random variables, variances add (not standard deviations). The correct method: sum the variances (0.70² + 0.92² + 0.94² + 0.76² = 0.49 + 0.8464 + 0.8836 + 0.5776 = 2.7976), then take the square root (√2.7976 ≈ 1.67). Choice A is the distractor for students who average the SDs; choice E is the distractor for students who sum the SDs directly; choice C is the raw sum of variances without taking the square root.

[MCQ] U4-L9-Q02

A player pays $15 to play a game in which a chip is randomly selected from a bag of chips. The bag contains 10 red chips, 4 blue chips, and 6 yellow chips. The player wins $5 if a red chip is selected, $10 if a blue chip is selected, and $20 if a yellow chip is selected. Let the random variable X represent the amount won from the selection of the chip, and let the random variable W represent the total amount won, where W = X − 15. What is the mean of W?

(A) $10.50
(B) $4.50
(C) −$4.50
(D) −$6.50
(E) −$10.50

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests EK VAR-5.F.1 (linear transformation of mean) and LO VAR-5.E. First compute E(X): (10/20)(5) + (4/20)(10) + (6/20)(20) = 2.50 + 2.00 + 6.00 = $10.50. Then apply W = X − 15: μ_W = μ_X − 15 = 10.50 − 15.00 = −$4.50. Choice A is the distractor for students who stop at E(X) without subtracting the $15 cost; choice B is the magnitude of the loss without the sign; choice D and E reflect arithmetic errors in computing the weighted average.

[MCQ] U4-L9-Q03

The distribution of random variable R has mean 10 and standard deviation 4. The distribution of random variable S has mean 7 and standard deviation 3. If R and S are independent, what are the mean and standard deviation of the distribution of R − S?

(A) Mean 3 and standard deviation 1
(B) Mean 3 and standard deviation 5
(C) Mean 3 and standard deviation 7
(D) Mean 17 and standard deviation 1
(E) Mean 17 and standard deviation 5

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests EK VAR-5.E.3 for the difference of two independent random variables. When subtracting independent RVs, means subtract (10 − 7 = 3) but variances add (4² + 3² = 16 + 9 = 25), so σ_{R−S} = √25 = 5. The most common misconception is subtracting standard deviations (4 − 3 = 1, choice A) — probe this explicitly. Choice C comes from adding SDs directly (4 + 3 = 7). Choices D and E reflect students who add the means instead of subtracting.

[FRQ] U4-L9-Q04

Show all your work. Indicate clearly the methods you use, because you will be scored on the correctness of your methods as well as on the accuracy and completeness of your results and explanations.

Company F sells fabrics known as fat quarters, which are rectangles of fabric created by cutting a yard of fabric into four pieces. Occasionally the manufacturing process results in a fabric defect. Let the random variable X represent the number of defects on a fat quarter created by Company F. The following table shows the probability distribution of X.

| X | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Probability | 0.58 | 0.23 | 0.11 | 0.05 | 0.03 |

If a fat quarter has more than 2 defects, it cannot be sold and is discarded. Let the random variable Y represent the number of defects on a fat quarter that can be sold by Company F.

(a) Construct the probability distribution of the random variable Y.

(b) Determine the mean and standard deviation of Y. Show your work.

Company G also sells fat quarters. The mean and standard deviation of the number of defects on a fat quarter that can be sold by Company G are 0.40 and 0.66, respectively. The fat quarters sell for $5.00 each, but are discounted by $1.50 for each defect found.

(c) What are the mean and standard deviation of the selling price for the fat quarters sold by Company G?

SCORING:

This FRQ is worth 4 points total across three parts.

**Part (a) — 1 point**
- Essentially correct (E): identifies all three values of Y as 0, 1, and 2 AND gives the correct conditional probabilities for each. P(X ≤ 2) = 0.58 + 0.23 + 0.11 = 0.92; then P(Y = 0) = 0.58/0.92 ≈ 0.630, P(Y = 1) = 0.23/0.92 = 0.250, P(Y = 2) = 0.11/0.92 ≈ 0.120.
- Partially correct (P): satisfies only one of the two components (correct values OR correct conditional probabilities, but not both).
- Incorrect (I): does not satisfy either component.

**Part (b) — 2 points**
- Essentially correct (E): correct mean (μ_Y = 0(0.63) + 1(0.25) + 2(0.12) = 0.49) AND correct standard deviation (σ_Y = √[0.63(0−0.49)² + 0.25(1−0.49)² + 0.12(2−0.49)²] ≈ 0.70) AND both are clearly labeled. Note: follow-through from a reasonable part (a) (probabilities non-negative and summing to 1) is accepted.
- Partially correct (P): satisfies only one or two of the three components.
- Incorrect (I): does not satisfy either component.

**Part (c) — 1 point**
- The selling price is a linear transformation: Price = 5 − 1.5 × (defects). Apply EK VAR-5.F.1: mean price = 5 − 1.5(0.40) = $4.40; SD of price = |−1.5|(0.66) = $0.99.
- Essentially correct (E): correct mean ($4.40) AND correct standard deviation ($0.99) AND both are clearly labeled.
- Partially correct (P): satisfies only one or two of the three components.
- Incorrect (I): does not satisfy either component.

**5-level response:** A complete response correctly identifies Y as a conditional distribution of X given X ≤ 2, computes conditional probabilities that sum to 1, uses the probability distribution to calculate μ_Y = 0.49 and σ_Y ≈ 0.70 with labeled work, then applies the linear transformation rule to Company G's data to get a mean selling price of $4.40 and standard deviation of $0.99 — all with clear labels and context throughout.

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This FRQ tests three layered skills: conditional probability (part a, LO VAR-5.E via the conditional distribution concept), computing mean and SD from a distribution (part b, EK VAR-5.E.1 / VAR-5.E.3), and linear transformation rules (part c, EK VAR-5.F.1). The key conceptual hurdle in (a) is recognizing that Y is a conditional distribution — students must divide each sellable probability by P(X ≤ 2) = 0.92. In (c), the critical distinction is that adding/subtracting a constant shifts the mean but not the SD; multiplying by a constant scales both (SD by the absolute value of the coefficient).

[MCQ] U4-L9-Q05

At a certain company, loan agents are paid based on the number of loans they close in a day. Based on company records, the number of loans that a randomly selected loan agent closes on a randomly selected day has the probability distribution below. At the company, the daily salary of a loan agent is $150 plus $50 per loan closed. Let Y represent the amount of money made by a randomly selected loan agent on a randomly selected day. Which of the following statements is NOT true?

| x | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(x) | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.22 | 0.30 | 0.18 | 0.12 | 0.03 |

(A) The mean of X is less than the mean of Y.
(B) The standard deviation of Y is approximately $71.
(C) The mean daily salary is greater than $350 per day.
(D) The standard deviation of X is less than the standard deviation of Y.
(E) The shape of the probability distribution of Y is unimodal and roughly symmetric.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests EK VAR-5.F.1. Y = 150 + 50X, so μ_Y = 150 + 50μ_X. Compute μ_X = 1(0.05) + 2(0.10) + 3(0.22) + 4(0.30) + 5(0.18) + 6(0.12) + 7(0.03) = 3.94. Then μ_Y = 150 + 50(3.94) = $347. Since $347 < $350, statement C ("greater than $350") is false — it is the NOT true statement. Statement A is true (Y is a scaled-up version of X so its mean is larger). Statement B is true (σ_Y = 50σ_X; σ_X ≈ 1.42, so σ_Y ≈ $71). Statement D is true (σ_Y = 50σ_X > σ_X). Statement E is true (linear transformation preserves shape). Probe whether students correctly compute the weighted mean rather than assuming it equals a "nice" value.

[MCQ] U4-L9-Q06

Data were collected on the ages, in years, of the men and women enrolled in a large sociology course. Let the random variables M and W represent the ages of the men and women, respectively. The distribution of M has mean 20.7 years and standard deviation 1.73 years. The distribution of W has mean 20.2 years and standard deviation 1.60 years. Of all of those enrolled in the course, 54 percent are men and 46 percent are women. What is the mean age of the combined distribution of both men and women in the course?

(A) 20.2 years
(B) 20.43 years
(C) 20.45 years
(D) 20.47 years
(E) 40.9 years

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests EK VAR-5.E.1 — the mean of a weighted combination aX + bY is aμ_X + bμ_Y. Here a = 0.54 (proportion men) and b = 0.46 (proportion women): 0.54(20.7) + 0.46(20.2) = 11.178 + 9.292 = 20.47 years. Choice C (20.45) is the distractor for students who use 0.5/0.5 weights (simple average). Choice B (20.43) may arise from rounding or arithmetic errors. Choice E (40.9) is the distractor for students who add the two means directly instead of taking a weighted average. Choice A is the women's mean alone. Emphasize that the weights must reflect the group proportions, not equal shares.

[MCQ] U4-L9-Q07

The random variable X has mean 12 and standard deviation 3. The random variable W is defined as W = 7 + 2X. What are the mean and standard deviation of W?

(A) The mean is 24, and the standard deviation is 6.
(B) The mean is 24, and the standard deviation is 13.
(C) The mean is 31, and the standard deviation is 3.
(D) The mean is 31, and the standard deviation is 6.
(E) The mean is 31, and the standard deviation is 13.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item directly tests EK VAR-5.F.1. For W = a + bX: μ_W = a + bμ_X = 7 + 2(12) = 31; σ_W = |b|σ_X = |2|(3) = 6. The key conceptual point is that adding a constant (7) shifts the mean but does NOT affect the standard deviation — only the multiplicative factor (2) scales the SD. Choice A (mean 24) is the distractor for students who forget to add the 7 to the mean. Choice C (SD = 3) catches students who think adding a constant changes nothing and multiplying only affects the mean. Choice E (SD = 13) may arise from students who add 7 + 2(3) = 13 for the SD, treating the transformation the same way for both parameters. Probe the distinction: constants shift location, coefficients scale spread.
