[MCQ — U4-L7-Q01]

Let random variable \(U\) represent the field goal percentage (percentage of shots made) for players in a basketball league. The following table shows the probability distribution of the random variable \(U\). Fatima claims that the distribution of \(U\) is uniform with a median of 0.4 field goal percentage. Is Fatima's claim supported by the table?

| Field Goal Percentage | Probability |
|-----------------------|-------------|
| 0.3 | 0.10 |
| 0.4 | 0.45 |
| 0.5 | 0.30 |
| 0.6 | 0.10 |
| 0.7 | 0.05 |

(A) Yes, the distribution is uniform with a median of 0.4 field goal percentage.
(B) No, the distribution is uniform with a median of 0.5 field goal percentage.
(C) No, the distribution is skewed to the right with a median of 0.4 field goal percentage.
(D) No, the distribution is skewed to the right with a median of 0.5 field goal percentage.
(E) No, the distribution is skewed to the left with a median of 0.4 field goal percentage.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Fatima's claim is not supported. A uniform distribution requires all probabilities to be equal — but P(U = 0.4) = 0.45 while P(U = 0.3) = P(U = 0.6) = 0.10, so the distribution is clearly not uniform (EK VAR-5.A.2). A relative frequency histogram would show most weight at 0.3 and 0.4 with a long tail toward 0.7, indicating right skew (EK VAR-5.A.3, VAR-5.B.1). The median IS 0.4: cumulative probability reaches 0.10 at U=0.3 and 0.55 at U=0.4, so 0.4 is the value where cumulative probability first reaches or exceeds 0.5 (EK VAR-5.A.4). The top distractor is D, which gets the skew direction right but claims the median is 0.5 — push students to work through the cumulative distribution carefully to locate the true median.

[MCQ — U4-L7-Q02]

Let random variable \(Y\) represent the number of interviews conducted for job openings at a certain company. The following table shows the cumulative probability distribution of the discrete random variable \(Y\). Khaleed claims that the distribution of \(Y\) is skewed to the left with mean equal to 8 interviews. Is Khaleed's claim correct?

| \(y\) | \(P(Y \leq y)\) |
|--------|-----------------|
| 5 | 0 |
| 6 | 0.2 |
| 7 | 0.4 |
| 8 | 0.6 |
| 9 | 0.8 |
| 10 | 1.0 |

(A) Yes, the distribution is skewed to the left with mean equal to 8 interviews.
(B) No, the distribution is skewed to the left with mean greater than 8 interviews.
(C) No, the distribution is skewed to the right with mean equal to 8 interviews.
(D) No, the distribution is uniform with mean equal to 8 interviews.
(E) No, the distribution is uniform with mean greater than 8 interviews.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The table shows a cumulative probability distribution, not individual probabilities (EK VAR-5.A.4). To recover individual probabilities, the student must take consecutive differences: P(Y=6)=0.2−0=0.2, P(Y=7)=0.4−0.2=0.2, P(Y=8)=0.6−0.4=0.2, P(Y=9)=0.8−0.6=0.2, P(Y=10)=1.0−0.8=0.2. All five values have equal probability 0.2, so the distribution is uniform — not skewed in any direction (EK VAR-5.A.2, VAR-5.B.1). With a uniform distribution over 6–10, the mean is the center value: 8 interviews (EK VAR-5.A.2). Khaleed is half right on the mean but wrong on the shape — the critical misconception is reading cumulative probabilities as individual probabilities.

[MCQ — U4-L7-Q03]

Let random variable \(R\) represent the number of visitors to a certain museum during a given day. The following table shows the probability distribution of the random variable. Which of the following claims about the distribution of random variable \(R\) is best supported by the histogram?

[This item shows a chart in the quiz.]

WHY — tutor-only chart description (do not compute for the student): The histogram has x-axis "Number of Visitors" with upper-bound labels at 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, 450, 500, and y-axis "Probability" scaled 0 to 0.25 in steps of 0.05. The eight bars have probabilities 0.05, 0.10, 0.12, 0.23, 0.23, 0.12, 0.10, 0.05 from left to right. The distribution is perfectly symmetric, centered around the 250–300 and 300–350 bars (both at 0.23), with mirror-image tails. Have the student describe what they see — the symmetry, where the tallest bars are, what the tails look like — and reason from that description.

(A) The most likely number of visitors to the museum on a given day is between 450 and 500.
(B) The mean number of visitors to the museum is much less than the median number of visitors.
(C) The mean number of visitors to the museum is much greater than the median number of visitors.
(D) On a given day, the number of visitors from 100 through 500 will occur with equal probabilities.
(E) On a given day, it is equally likely for the museum to have less than 300 visitors as it is to have more than 300 visitors.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The histogram is symmetric, centered around 300 visitors (EK VAR-5.A.3, VAR-5.B.1). Because the distribution is symmetric, the probability of being below the center equals the probability of being above it — exactly what choice E states. Choice A is wrong: the 450–500 bar has probability 0.05, the lowest. Choices B and C are wrong: symmetry means the mean and median are approximately equal, not that one is much greater. Choice D is wrong: the bars clearly have unequal probabilities (0.05 vs. 0.23). The key distractor is D, which conflates "all values possible" with "all values equally likely" — a uniform-distribution misconception (EK VAR-5.A.2). Ask the student to compare the heights of the leftmost and tallest bars before committing.
