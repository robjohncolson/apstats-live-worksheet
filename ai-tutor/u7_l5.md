**[MCQ] U7-L5-Q01**

For a certain brand of canned corn, the company claims that the mean weight of the contents of the cans is 15.25 ounces. A random sample of 36 cans were selected. The sample was found to have mean 15.18 ounces and standard deviation 0.12 ounce. A hypothesis test will be conducted to investigate whether there is evidence to support the belief that the mean is less than 15.25 ounces.

Which of the following is the correct test statistic for the hypothesis test?

(A) t = (15.18 − 15.25) / (0.12 / 6)
(B) t = (15.18 − 15.25) / (0.12 / 36)
(C) t = (15.25 − 15.18) / (0.12 / 6)
(D) t = (15.25 − 15.18) / (0.12 / 36)
(E) t = (15.25 − 15.18) / 0.12

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The numerator of the test statistic is the sample mean (15.18) minus the assumed population mean (15.25); order matters — x̄ − μ₀, not μ₀ − x̄ (EK VAR-7.E.1). The denominator is the standard error: sample standard deviation divided by √n = 0.12 / √36 = 0.12 / 6, not divided by 36 itself. Choice B uses n = 36 in the denominator instead of √n = 6 (a common error). Choices C/D/E flip the numerator order (positive instead of negative), which would correspond to an upper-tail test when the hypothesis is lower-tail. The top distractor is C, which gets the denominator right but reverses the subtraction. Students should anchor every step to the formula t = (x̄ − μ₀) / (s / √n) from EK VAR-7.E.1.

**[MCQ] U7-L5-Q02**

A magazine article reported that college students spend an average of $100 on a first date. A university sociologist believed that number was too high for the students at the university. The sociologist surveyed 32 randomly selected students from the university and obtained a sample mean of $92.23 for the most recent first dates. A one-sample t-test resulted in a p-value of 0.026.

Which of the following is a correct interpretation of the p-value?

(A) The probability is 0.026 that the mean amount of money students from the university spend on a first date is less than $100.
(B) The probability is 0.026 that the mean amount of money students from the university spend on a first date is less than $92.23.
(C) The probability is 0.026 that the mean amount of money students from the university spend on a first date is more than $92.23.
(D) If the mean amount of money that students from the university spend on a first date is $100, the probability is 0.026 that a randomly selected group of 32 students from the university would spend a mean of $92.23 or less on their most recent first dates.
(E) If the mean amount of money that students from the university spend on a first date is less than $100, the probability is 0.026 that a randomly selected group of 32 students from the university would spend a mean of $92.23 or less on their most recent first dates.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per EK DAT-3.E.1, the p-value is computed assuming the null hypothesis is true — the true population mean equals the null value ($100), not "less than $100." The p-value is the probability of observing the sample mean ($92.23) or more extreme (i.e., $92.23 or less, since this is a lower-tail test) given that the null is true. Choice A incorrectly treats the p-value as a probability about the population parameter itself (a classic misconception — parameters are fixed, not random). Choice E is the principal distractor: it conditions on the alternative hypothesis being true rather than the null, which inverts the definition. Students must internalize: p-value = P(data this extreme or more | H₀ true).

**[MCQ] U7-L5-Q03**

A fast-food restaurant claims that a small order of french fries contains 120 calories. A nutritionist is concerned that the true average calorie count is higher than that. The nutritionist randomly selects 35 small orders of french fries and determines their calories. The resulting sample mean is 155.6 calories, and the p-value for the hypothesis test is 0.00093.

Which of the following is a correct interpretation of the p-value?

(A) If the population mean is 120 calories, the p-value of 0.00093 is the probability of observing a sample mean of 155.6 calories or more.
(B) If the population mean is 120 calories, the p-value of 0.00093 is the probability of observing a sample mean of 155.6 calories or less.
(C) If the population mean is 120 calories, the p-value of 0.00093 is the probability of observing a sample mean of 155.6 calories or more, or a sample mean of 84.4 calories or less.
(D) If the population mean is 155.6 calories, the p-value of 0.00093 is the probability of observing a sample mean of 120 calories or more.
(E) If the population mean is 155.6 calories, the p-value of 0.00093 is the probability of observing a sample mean of 120 calories or less.

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per EK DAT-3.E.1, the p-value is the probability of observing the sample mean or more extreme, assuming the null hypothesis (μ = 120) is true. Since the alternative is upper-tail (nutritionist suspects higher than 120), "more extreme" means 155.6 calories or more. Choice B reverses the direction of the tail. Choice C describes a two-tailed p-value — wrong because the hypothesis is one-sided (upper). Choices D and E both condition on the sample mean (155.6) as if it were the null — a fundamental reversal. The top distractor is C, which would be correct for a two-tailed test; students should be prompted to identify the direction of the alternative hypothesis before reading the choices.

**[FRQ] U7-L5-Q04**

A recent study reported that high school students spend an average of 94 minutes per day texting. Jenna claims that the average for the students at her large high school is greater than 94 minutes. She will conduct a study to investigate this claim.

(a) To collect data, Jenna will select a sample of size 32 from the population.
    (i) State Jenna's population of interest.
    (ii) Name and describe a sampling method Jenna could use that will satisfy the conditions needed for inference to the population.

(b) Based on a sample of 32 students, Jenna calculated a sample mean of 96.5 minutes and a sample standard deviation of 6.3 minutes. Assume all conditions for inference are met. At the significance level of α = 0.05, do the data provide convincing statistical evidence to support Jenna's claim? Complete an appropriate inference procedure to support your answer.

SCORING:

**Part (a) — 1 point total**

- Essentially correct (E): All three components satisfied — (i) correct description of the population (all students at Jenna's large high school); (ii) identifies that individuals in the population are represented in the sample by name or number; AND (ii) a random sampling method is identified with an explanation of how the sampling will be conducted.
- Partially correct (P): Satisfies only two of the three components.
- Incorrect (I): Does not meet criteria for E or P.

*Note: Examples of acceptable random sampling methods include simple random sample (obtain a numbered list of all students, use a random number generator to select 32 different numbers) or stratified random sample (use class year as strata, randomly select 8 from each of 4 class years). The key is that the method must be clearly described, not just named.*

**Part (b-step1): Identification and hypotheses — 1 point**

- Essentially correct (E): All three components — correctly states the one-sample t-test for a population mean (by name or formula); population parameter correctly defined in context (μ = mean minutes texting per day for all students at Jenna's school); and correct hypotheses: H₀: μ = 94, Hₐ: μ > 94.
- Partially correct (P): Only two of the three components.
- Incorrect (I): Does not meet criteria for E or P.

**Part (b-step2): Calculation — 1 point**

- Essentially correct (E): Both components — correct test statistic t = (96.5 − 94) / (6.3 / √32) ≈ 2.24 with df = 31; AND correct p-value P(t > 2.24) = 0.016 (or correct critical value).
- Partially correct (P): Only one of the two components correct.
- Incorrect (I): Does not meet criteria for E or P.

**Part (b-step3): Conclusion with justification — 1 point**

- Essentially correct (E): All three components — comparison of p-value to α (0.016 < 0.05, or t = 2.24 > t* = 1.696); correct decision to reject H₀; conclusion about the population parameter in context ("There is convincing statistical evidence that the mean number of minutes all students at Jenna's high school spend per day texting is greater than 94 minutes").
- Partially correct (P): Only two of the three components.
- Incorrect (I): Does not meet criteria for E or P.

**5-level response:** A complete, 4-point response correctly identifies the population as all students at Jenna's large high school, describes a valid random sampling procedure (e.g., SRS from a numbered roster), sets up the one-sample t-test with correctly defined parameter and directional hypotheses, computes t ≈ 2.24 with df = 31 and p-value ≈ 0.016, compares p = 0.016 < α = 0.05 to reject H₀, and states a conclusion in context about the population mean — not the sample mean.

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This FRQ tests the complete 4-step inference procedure (per EK VAR-7.E.1 and DAT-3.F.1/F.2). Common student errors: (1) describing the population as "the 32 students Jenna surveyed" rather than all students at the school; (2) stating hypotheses in terms of x̄ rather than μ; (3) using n = 32 in the denominator instead of √32; (4) failing to link the p-value to α explicitly in the conclusion; (5) concluding about the sample rather than the population. For part (a-ii), the description must go beyond naming "SRS" — students must explain the mechanism (numbered list + random number generator). Per DAT-3.F.1: a formal decision compares p-value to α; per DAT-3.F.2: the conclusion must reference the population parameter in context.

**[MCQ] U7-L5-Q06**

The distribution of mass for United States pennies minted since 1982 is approximately normal with mean 2.5 grams. A random sample of 10 pennies minted since 1982 was selected. The sample had a mean mass of 2.47 grams and a standard deviation of 0.04 gram.

The test statistic for the population mean has which of the following distributions?

(A) A normal distribution with mean 0 and standard deviation 1
(B) A normal distribution with mean 2.5 and standard deviation 0.04
(C) A normal distribution with mean 2.47 and standard deviation 0.04
(D) A t-distribution with 9 degrees of freedom
(E) A t-distribution with 10 degrees of freedom

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per EK VAR-7.E.1 and VAR-7.A.1: when the sample standard deviation s is used in place of the unknown σ, the test statistic follows a t-distribution (not standard normal) with n − 1 degrees of freedom. Here n = 10, so df = 10 − 1 = 9. Choice A (standard normal) would only apply if σ were known — it is not given here. Choice E is the most common distractor: students confuse df = n with df = n − 1. **[MCQ] U7-L5-Q07**

Most dermatologists recommend that the ideal shower lasts approximately 10 minutes. A researcher suspects that the average shower length of high school students is greater than 10 minutes. To test the belief, the researcher surveyed 125 randomly selected high school students and found that their average shower length was 14.7 minutes. With all conditions for inference met, a hypothesis test was conducted at the significance level of α = 0.05, and the test produced a p-value of 0.0000. Which of the following is an appropriate conclusion?

(A) The test was flawed because the p-value cannot equal 0.
(B) The researcher has statistical evidence to conclude that the sample mean shower length for high school students is greater than 10 minutes.
(C) The researcher does not have statistical evidence to conclude that the sample mean shower length for high school students is greater than 10 minutes.
(D) The researcher has statistical evidence to conclude that the population mean shower length for high school students is greater than 10 minutes.
(E) The researcher does not have statistical evidence to conclude that the population mean shower length for high school students is greater than 10 minutes.

KEY: D

Choice B correctly rejects H₀ but misstates the conclusion as being about the sample mean rather than the population mean — a critical AP error. The group tests 50 randomly selected GoFast2000 cars and finds a sample mean of 34.8 mpg. With all assumptions for inference met, a hypothesis test resulted in a p-value of 0.324.

For a significance level of α = 0.05, which of the following is a correct conclusion?

(A) The p-value is less than 0.05, and the null hypothesis is rejected. There is convincing statistical evidence that the mean is less than 35 mpg.
(B) The p-value is less than 0.05, and the null hypothesis is not rejected. There is not convincing statistical evidence that the mean is less than 35 mpg.

**[MCQ] U7-L5-Q10**

For a certain brand of tomato seeds, the seed package claims that it takes 87 days after planting for the tomato plants to produce fruit. Sarah, a botanist, wanted to know whether the mean number of days for the plants to produce fruit where she lives is different from 87 days. She planted 40 seeds and recorded the number of days for each plant to produce fruit. With all conditions for inference met, the hypothesis test was conducted at the significance level α = 0.05, and the test resulted in a p-value of 0.0752.

Which of the following is a correct conclusion?

(A) Sarah has convincing statistical evidence to conclude that the population mean number of days for the plants to produce fruit is greater than 87 days.
(B) Sarah has convincing statistical evidence to conclude that the population mean number of days for the plants to produce fruit is different from 87 days.
(C) Sarah does not have convincing statistical evidence to conclude that the population mean number of days for the plants to produce fruit is greater than 87 days.
(D) Sarah does not have convincing statistical evidence to conclude that the population mean number of days for the plants to produce fruit is different from 87 days.
(E) Sarah does not have convincing statistical evidence to conclude that the population mean number of days for the plants to produce fruit is equal to 87 days.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per EK DAT-3.F.1: p-value (0.0752) > α (0.05), so fail to reject H₀ — there is NOT convincing statistical evidence that μ ≠ 87 days. Critically, this is a two-tailed test (Hₐ: μ ≠ 87) since Sarah wants to know whether the mean is "different from" 87. Choice C correctly states no convincing evidence, but frames the conclusion as if the alternative were one-sided (greater than 87) — wrong because the test is two-sided. Choices A and B incorrectly reject when the p-value exceeds α. Choice E is a trap: concluding there is no evidence that μ = 87 is a confusion of the roles of null and alternative; failing to reject never proves the null. Tether the student to DAT-3.F.1 and ask: "What was Sarah's alternative hypothesis? One-sided or two-sided? Now look at the p-value vs. α."
