**[MCQ] U5-L2-Q02**

The mean and standard deviation of the sample data collected on continuous variable \(x\) are \(-0.25\) and \(0.03\), respectively. Based on the table, do the data support the use of a normal model to approximate population characteristics?

| Interval | Relative Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| -0.34 ≤ x < -0.31 | 0.02 |
| -0.31 ≤ x < -0.28 | 0.15 |
| -0.28 ≤ x < -0.25 | 0.33 |
| -0.25 ≤ x < -0.22 | 0.36 |
| -0.22 ≤ x < -0.19 | 0.11 |
| -0.19 ≤ x < -0.16 | 0.03 |

(A) Yes, because the sum of the relative frequencies is 1.00.
(B) Yes, because the distribution of relative frequencies is very close to the empirical rule for normal models.
(C) No, because the values are negative and normal models are used for positive values.
(D) No, because the distribution of relative frequencies is very far from the empirical rule for normal models.
(E) No, because the sample size and the population parameters are not known.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): Based on the table, 69% of the data falls within 1 standard deviation of the mean, 95% falls within 2 standard deviations, and 100% falls within 3 standard deviations. This closely matches the empirical rule (68%–95%–99.7%), supporting the use of a normal model (VAR-6.C.1). Choice A is wrong because summing to 1.00 is required for any valid probability distribution — it says nothing about normality. Choice C is a misconception: normal distributions can be centered on any value, including negatives (VAR-6.A.2). Choice D is wrong because the distribution actually is close to the empirical rule. Choice E is wrong because checking the empirical rule with the available data is sufficient to assess approximate normality.

**[MCQ] U5-L2-Q03**

The continuous random variable \(N\) has a normal distribution with mean 7.5 and standard deviation 2.5. For which of the following is the probability equal to 0?

(A) \(P(N = 8)\)
(B) \(P(N > 8)\)
(C) \(P(N < 8)\)
(D) \(P(7 < N < 8)\)
(E) \(P(N < 7)\) or \(P(N > 8)\)

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): For a continuous random variable, probability is measured as area under the curve. At the single point N = 8, there is no interval and therefore no area — so P(N = 8) = 0 (VAR-6.A.1, VAR-6.A.3). All other choices involve intervals and therefore have positive area (and positive probability). The top distractor is E, where students may think both tails together are negligible; but both tails are intervals with positive area. This is a foundational conceptual distinction between discrete and continuous random variables.

**[MCQ] U5-L2-Q04**

Data will be collected on the following variables. Which variable is most likely to be approximated by a normal model?

(A) The distribution of the number of books read last week by middle school students, where the right tail of the distribution is longer than the left
(B) The distribution of life span, in minutes, for batteries of a certain size, where most life spans cluster around the center of the distribution but with some very low and some very high life spans
(C) The distribution of ages, in years, of the students at a certain college, where most students are between 18 and 22 years old, but ages greater than 22 will probably be more spread out than ages less than 18
(D) The distribution of the number of birthdays per month for the employees at a certain company, where the number of birthdays in each month is approximately equal
(E) The distribution of the length of a stay, in days, in a hospital after surgery, where many patients have very short hospital stays, but some stays are quite lengthy and considered high outliers

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): Battery life span is described as clustering around the center with some values very low and some very high — this is the classic symmetric, bell-shaped pattern that makes a normal model appropriate (VAR-6.C.1). Choice A describes a right-skewed distribution (longer right tail) — not normal. Choice C describes an asymmetric distribution (more spread above 22 than below 18) — not symmetric. Choice D describes approximately equal frequencies across months — that is uniform, not normal. Choice E describes a right-skewed distribution with many short stays and high outliers — not normal. The key is that VAR-6.C.1 requires a distribution to be symmetrical and bell-shaped for the normal to be an appropriate approximation.

**[MCQ] U5-L2-Q06**

The distribution of time needed to complete a certain programming task is approximately normal, with mean 47 minutes and standard deviation 6 minutes. Which of the following is closest to the probability that a randomly chosen task will take less than 34 minutes or more than 60 minutes to complete?

(A) 0.0151
(B) 0.0303
(C) 0.4849
(D) 0.9697
(E) 0.9849

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The z-score for 34 minutes is z = (34 − 47)/6 = −13/6 ≈ −2.17, and the z-score for 60 minutes is z = (60 − 47)/6 = 13/6 ≈ 2.17. By the symmetry of the normal distribution, P(x < 34) = P(x > 60), so the total probability is P(z < −2.17) + P(z > 2.17) ≈ 2 × 0.0151 ≈ 0.0303 (VAR-6.A, VAR-6.B.1). Choice A (0.0151) is the probability in only one tail — a common error of counting just one side. Choice C (0.4849) is the area between one z-score and the mean — students who forget to interpret the area correctly. Choice D (0.9697) is the complement of the correct answer — students who find P(34 ≤ x ≤ 60) instead. Choice E (0.9849) is the area within one tail toward the center. The key skill is recognizing a two-tailed probability and correctly applying z-score standardization (Skill 3.A, VAR-6.A.3).

**[MCQ] U5-L2-Q07**

A machine is used to fill bags with a popular brand of trail mix. The machine is calibrated so the distribution of the weights of the bags of trail mix is normal, with mean 240 grams and standard deviation 3 grams. Of the following, which is the least weight of a bag in the top 5 percent of the distribution?

(A) 234 grams
(B) 240 grams
(C) 243 grams
(D) 246 grams
(E) 248 grams

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The upper 5% of the standard normal distribution begins to the right of z ≈ 1.645 (since P(z < 1.645) ≈ 0.95). Converting back to the original scale: weight = z × σ + μ = 1.645 × 3 + 240 ≈ 244.95 grams (VAR-6.B.1). The question asks for the least weight among the answer choices that falls in the top 5%, so the answer must be at least 244.95. Of the choices, 246 is the smallest value exceeding 244.95. Choice C (243) is below the threshold — it falls within the middle 95%, not the top 5%. Choices D and E are both above 244.95, but only D is the least such value. Choice A (234) is well below the mean. Choice B (240) is the mean itself. Common errors: students compute the threshold correctly but then choose the nearest value rather than the least value that is still in the top 5%.
