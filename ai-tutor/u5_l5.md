[MCQ] U5-L5-Q01

A bag contains chips of which 27.5 percent are blue. A random sample of 5 chips will be selected one at a time and with replacement. What are the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion of blue chips for samples of size 5?

(A) The mean is 5(0.275), and the standard deviation is sqrt(5(0.275)(0.725)).
(B) The mean is 0.275, and the standard deviation is sqrt(5(0.275)(0.725)).
(C) The mean is 0.275, and the standard deviation is sqrt(0.275(0.725)/5).
(D) The mean is 27.5, and the standard deviation is sqrt(5(27.5)(72.5)).
(E) The mean is 27.5, and the standard deviation is sqrt(27.5(72.5)/5).

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The mean of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion equals the population proportion p = 0.275 (EK UNC-3.K.1). The standard deviation uses the formula sqrt(p(1−p)/n) = sqrt(0.275 × 0.725 / 5) (EK UNC-3.K.1). Choice A confuses the sampling distribution of a count with that of a proportion (uses np instead of p for the mean and the binomial SD formula). Choice B has the correct mean but uses the count-based SD formula rather than the proportion formula. Choices D and E use the percentage 27.5 instead of the decimal 0.275.

[MCQ] U5-L5-Q02

A manufacturer of cell phone screens has found that 5 percent of all screens produced have defects. Let p_d represent the population proportion of all cell phone screens with a screen defect, therefore p_d = 0.05. For the sampling distribution of the sample proportion of cell phone screens from this manufacturer with a screen defect for sample size 400, μ_p̂_d = 0.05. Which of the following is the best interpretation of μ_p̂_d = 0.05?

(A) For a randomly selected cell phone screen from this population, the mean number of screen defects for the selected screen will be equal to 0.05.
(B) For every sample of size 400 from this population, the proportion of cell phone screens with a screen defect will be 0.05.
(C) For all samples of size 400 from this population, the mean number of screen defects for the samples is 0.05.
(D) For all samples of size 400 from this population, the mean of all resulting sample proportions of cell phone screens with a screen defect is 0.05.
(E) For all samples of size 400 from this population, the standard deviation of all resulting sample proportions of cell phone screens with a screen defect is 0.05.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): μ_p̂_d represents the mean of all sample proportions for samples of size 400 from this population — the long-run average of all possible p̂ values, which equals the population proportion (EK UNC-3.M.1 and EK UNC-3.K.1). Choice A confuses an individual screen's characteristic with the mean of a sampling distribution. Choice B incorrectly claims every single sample will have proportion exactly 0.05 — that would be true only for the mean of the distribution, not each individual sample. Choice C confuses "mean number of defects" (a count) with "mean of sample proportions." Choice E confuses the mean with the standard deviation of the sampling distribution (LO UNC-3.M).

[MCQ] U5-L5-Q03

A national charity contacted 100 randomly selected people by phone, and 7 percent of those contacted made a donation to the charity. The population proportion of those who make a donation when contacted by phone is known to be p = 0.05. For samples of size 100, which of the following best interprets the mean of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion of people who make a donation when contacted by phone?

(A) For all random samples of 100 people contacted by phone, the sample proportion of those who make a donation will be 0.05.
(B) For all random samples of 100 people contacted by phone, the sample proportion of those who make a donation will be 0.07.
(C) The mean of all sample proportions of those who make a donation from all random samples of 100 people contacted by phone is 0.05.
(D) The mean of all sample proportions of those who make a donation from all random samples of 100 people contacted by phone is 0.07.
(E) The probability that the mean of the sampling distribution of sample proportions is greater than 0.07 is 0.05.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The mean of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion equals the population proportion p = 0.05, not the observed sample proportion 0.07 (EK UNC-3.K.1 and EK UNC-3.M.1). The observed 0.07 is just one sample result; the theoretical mean of all possible sample proportions equals p. Choice A is wrong because the mean being 0.05 does not mean every sample will show 0.05 — individual samples vary. Choice B and D incorrectly use the observed sample proportion 0.07 rather than the population parameter. Choice E inverts the relationship between the mean and a probability statement (LO UNC-3.M).

[MCQ] U5-L5-Q04

On any given day, the proportion of workers at a factory who are more than 5 minutes late to work is 0.11. A random sample of 20 workers will be selected. Which of the following is the best interpretation of the mean of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion of workers in the sample who are more than 5 minutes late to work for samples of size 20?

(A) For all samples of size 20, the mean of all possible sample proportions is equal to 0.11.
(B) For a randomly selected worker, the probability the worker will be more than 5 minutes late to work is 0.11.
(C) For a random sample of 20 workers, the proportion of workers who are more than 20 minutes late to work will be 0.11.
(D) For a random sample of 20 workers, the probability that 2.2 workers will be more than 5 minutes late to work is very high.
(E) For repeated samples of size 20, the proportion of workers who are more than 5 minutes late to work varies in each sample by no more than 0.11.

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The mean of a sampling distribution is the mean of all possible sample proportions for samples of size 20 taken from the population; in repeated sampling, the long-run average of all sample proportions equals the population proportion of 0.11 (EK UNC-3.K.1 and EK UNC-3.M.1). Choice B conflates the population proportion with an individual probability — while the probability a single worker is late is 0.11, interpreting the mean of a sampling distribution requires talking about all samples (LO UNC-3.M). Choice C introduces an error (changes "5 minutes" to "20 minutes"). Choice D confuses the expected count 2.2 workers with a probability statement. Choice E incorrectly describes the mean as a variability bound (confuses mean with standard deviation).

[MCQ] U5-L5-Q05

City officials estimate that 46 percent of all city residents are in favor of building a new city park. A random sample of 150 city residents will be selected. Suppose that 51 percent of the sample are in favor of building a new city park. Which of the following is true about the sampling distribution of the sample proportion for samples of size 150?

(A) The distribution is not normal, and the mean is 0.46.
(B) The distribution is not normal, and the mean is 0.51.
(C) The distribution is not normal, and the mean is the average of 0.46 and 0.51.
(D) The distribution is approximately normal, and the mean is 0.46.
(E) The distribution is approximately normal, and the mean is 0.51.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): np = 150(0.46) = 69 ≥ 10 and n(1−p) = 150(0.54) = 81 ≥ 10, satisfying the large-sample normality condition (EK UNC-3.L.1). The mean of the sampling distribution equals the population proportion p = 0.46, not the observed sample proportion 0.51 — the sample proportion is one realization from the distribution; the mean is a fixed parameter (EK UNC-3.K.1). Choices A, B, C all incorrectly conclude the distribution is not normal. Choice E correctly identifies approximate normality but wrongly places the mean at the observed 0.51 instead of the population parameter (LO UNC-3.K and LO UNC-3.L).

[MCQ] U5-L5-Q06

A sample of size n will be selected from a population with population proportion p. Which of the following must be true for the sampling distribution of the sample proportion to be approximately normal?

(A) Both np and n(1−p) are at least 10.
(B) n is greater than 30.
(C) p is greater than 0.5.
(D) The mean is equal to np.
(E) The variance is equal to np(1−p).

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The conditions np ≥ 10 and n(1−p) ≥ 10 are the standard normality conditions for sampling distributions of proportions — they ensure the distribution is not overly skewed toward 0 or 1 (EK UNC-3.L.1). Choice B misapplies the n ≥ 30 rule, which governs normality of the sampling distribution of the sample mean (CLT), not proportions. Choice C is false — p > 0.5 is irrelevant; what matters is that both np and n(1−p) are large enough. Choices D and E describe characteristics of the binomial count distribution (not the proportion distribution), and neither is the normality condition (LO UNC-3.L).

[MCQ] U5-L5-Q07

For a certain population of sea turtles, 18 percent are longer than 6.5 feet. A random sample of 90 sea turtles will be selected. What is the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion of sea turtles longer than 6.5 feet for samples of size 90?

(A) sqrt(6.5(18−6.5)/90)
(B) sqrt(0.18(1−0.18)/90)
(C) sqrt(0.18(1−0.18)/6.5)
(D) sqrt(18(90−18)/100)
(E) sqrt(18(100−18)/90)

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The standard deviation formula for a sampling distribution of a sample proportion is σ_p̂ = sqrt(p(1−p)/n), where p is the population proportion expressed as a decimal (0.18) and n is the sample size (90), giving sqrt(0.18 × 0.82 / 90) (EK UNC-3.K.1). Choice A uses the measurement value 6.5 feet as if it were a proportion — a context confusion. Choice C has the correct numerator but uses 6.5 (the cutoff in feet) as n instead of the actual sample size. Choices D and E use the percentage 18 instead of the decimal 0.18 and also use incorrect formulas (LO UNC-3.K).

[MCQ] U5-L5-Q08

A certain skin cream is 80 percent effective in curing a common rash. A random sample of 100 people with the rash will use the cream. Which of the following is the best description of the shape of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion of those who will be cured?

(A) Bimodal
(B) Uniform
(C) Approximately normal
(D) Strongly skewed to the left
(E) Strongly skewed to the right

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): With n = 100 and p = 0.80: np = 100(0.8) = 80 ≥ 10 and n(1−p) = 100(0.2) = 20 ≥ 10 — both normality conditions are satisfied (EK UNC-3.L.1). Although p = 0.80 is far from 0.5, the sample size of 100 is large enough to overcome the skew. A common misconception is that because p = 0.80 is near 1 the distribution must be skewed left; but with n(1−p) = 20 ≥ 10, the condition is met and the distribution is approximately normal (LO UNC-3.L).

[MCQ] U5-L5-Q09

At a large corporation, 6,000 employees from department A and 4,000 employees from department B are attending a training session. A random sample of 500 employees attending the session will be selected. Consider two sampling methods: with replacement and without replacement. How will the methods affect the standard deviations of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion of employees from department B?

(A) The standard deviations will be equal from either method.
(B) Sampling without replacement will result in a standard deviation less than but close to sqrt(0.4(0.6)/500).
(C) Sampling without replacement will result in a standard deviation greater than but close to sqrt(0.4(0.6)/500).
(D) Sampling with replacement will result in a standard deviation less than but close to sqrt(0.4(0.6)/500).
(E) Sampling with replacement will result in a standard deviation greater than but close to sqrt(0.4(0.6)/500).

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The proportion of employees from department B is 4,000/10,000 = 0.4. When sampling without replacement, the finite population correction factor reduces the standard deviation below sqrt(p(1−p)/n) = sqrt(0.4(0.6)/500). Because the sample size of 500 is less than 10% of the total population of 10,000 (500/10,000 = 5%), the reduction is small — the standard deviation is less than but very close to sqrt(0.4(0.6)/500) (EK UNC-3.K.2). Choice A is wrong because the methods do differ, just slightly. Choice C reverses the direction — without-replacement reduces (not increases) the SD. Choices D and E describe the with-replacement scenario: sampling with replacement gives exactly sqrt(p(1−p)/n), not less (LO UNC-3.K, EK UNC-3.K.2).
