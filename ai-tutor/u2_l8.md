[MCQ] U2-L8-Q01

The height \(h\) and collar size \(c\), both in centimeters, measured from a sample of boys were used to create the regression line \(\hat{c} = -94 + 0.9h\). The line is used to predict collar size from height, both in centimeters, for boys' shirt collars. Which of the following has no logical interpretation in context?

(A) The predicted collar size of a boy with height 140 cm
(B) The h values in the sample
(C) The c values in the sample
(D) The slope of the regression line
(E) The \(c\)-intercept of the regression line

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The reasoning field states: the c-intercept of the regression line is determined when h = 0 centimeters, and it is not meaningful to predict the collar size of a boy with a height of 0 centimeters. This directly maps to EK DAT-1.G.3 — sometimes the y-intercept does not have a logical interpretation in context. The top distractor is D (the slope), because students may think "the slope is a formula thing, not real-world," but the slope (0.9) is perfectly interpretable: for each 1 cm increase in height, predicted collar size increases by 0.9 cm (EK DAT-1.H.2). Option A is also interpretable because h = 140 cm is a realistic height within the data range.

[MCQ] U2-L8-Q02

A researcher studying a specific type of tree creates a least-squares regression line for relating the height and the diameter, both in meters, of a fully grown tree. The results are shown in the following computer output. Which of the following values represents the predicted change in the height of the tree for each one-meter increase in the diameter of the tree?

| Variable  | DF | Estimate | SE   | T     |
|-----------|----|----------|------|-------|
| Intercept | 1  | 5        | 4.00 | 2.50  |
| Diameter  | 1  | 30       | 2.50 | 13.20 |

(A) 30
(B) 5
(C) 4
(D) 2.5
(E) \(\frac{1}{30}\)

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The reasoning field states: the value 30 is the slope of the model; it represents the predicted change in height for each one-meter increase in diameter. In computer output the explanatory variable's Estimate row is the slope (EK DAT-1.H.2). The top distractor is B (5), which is the y-intercept (the Intercept row's Estimate) — students confuse which row gives the slope vs. the constant. Reinforce EK DAT-1.H.1: the coefficient of the explanatory variable (Diameter row) is the slope; the Intercept row is the y-intercept.

[MCQ] U2-L8-Q03

Dairy farmers are aware there is often a linear relationship between the age, in years, of a dairy cow and the amount of milk produced, in gallons per week. The least-squares regression line produced from a random sample is \(\widehat{\text{Milk}} = 40.8 - 1.1(\text{Age})\). Based on the model, what is the difference in predicted amounts of milk produced between a cow of 5 years and a cow of 10 years?

(A) A cow of 5 years is predicted to produce 5.5 fewer gallons per week.
(B) A cow of 5 years is predicted to produce 5.5 more gallons per week.
(C) A cow of 5 years is predicted to produce 1.1 fewer gallons per week.
(D) A cow of 5 years is predicted to produce 1.1 more gallons per week.
(E) A cow of 5 years and a cow of 10 years are both predicted to produce 40.8 gallons per week.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The reasoning field states: the difference of 5 years produces a 5.5 gallon per week difference in favor of the younger cow. Since the slope is −1.1, for each year increase in age, milk production decreases by 1.1 gallons. The difference between ages 10 and 5 is 5 years, so 5 × 1.1 = 5.5 gallons more for the younger cow. This tests EK DAT-1.H.2 (slope = predicted change per unit increase in x) applied across a 5-unit gap. Common distractors: C and D (using 1.1 directly — confusing a 1-year gap with a 5-year gap); A (getting the direction backwards — forgetting the slope is negative so the younger cow produces more).

[MCQ] U2-L8-Q04

A set of bivariate data was used to create a least-squares regression line. Which of the following is minimized by the line?

(A) The sum of the residuals
(B) The sum of the squared residuals
(C) The sum of the absolute values of the residuals
(D) The influence of outliers
(E) The slope

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The reasoning field states: the least-squares regression line minimizes the sum of the squares of the residuals — this is the fundamental principle behind least-squares regression. This is directly EK DAT-1.G.1. The top distractor is A (sum of residuals) — students may know residuals are involved but not recall that squaring is the key step; in fact, any line makes the sum of residuals equal to zero (positive and negative cancel), so minimizing the sum of residuals is not the defining property. Option C (absolute values) describes a different, valid estimator (LAD regression) but is not the least-squares line.

[MCQ] U2-L8-Q05

A restaurant manager collected data to predict monthly sales for the restaurant from monthly advertising expenses. The model created from the data showed that 36 percent of the variation in monthly sales could be explained by monthly advertising expenses. What was the value of the correlation coefficient?

(A) 0.64
(B) 0.60
(C) 0.40
(D) 0.36
(E) 0.13

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The reasoning field states: the proportion of variation explained by the explanatory variable is the coefficient of determination r², so r² = 0.36, and r = √0.36 = 0.60. This tests EK DAT-1.G.4 (r² is the proportion of variation explained) and the relationship r = √(r²). The problem context (sales predicted by advertising expenses — both increase together) confirms r is positive, so r = +0.60 not −0.60. Top distractors: D (0.36 — students confuse r with r²), A (0.64 — possibly 1 − 0.36), C (0.40 — possibly √0.16 by error).

[MCQ] U2-L8-Q06

The least-squares regression line \(\hat{S} = 0.5 + 1.1L\) models the relationship between the listing price and the actual sales price of 12 houses, with both amounts given in hundred-thousands of dollars. Let \(L\) represent the listing price and \(\hat{S}\) represent the sales price. Which of the following is the best interpretation of the slope of the regression line?

(A) For each hundred-thousand-dollar increase in the listing price, the sales price will increase by $1.1.
(B) For each hundred-thousand-dollar increase in the listing price, the sales price will increase by $110,000.
(C) For each hundred-thousand-dollar increase in the listing price, the sales price will decrease by $110,000.
(D) For each hundred-thousand-dollar increase in the listing price, the sales price is predicted to increase by $1.1.
(E) For each hundred-thousand-dollar increase in the listing price, the sales price is predicted to increase by $110,000.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The reasoning field states: nondeterministic language (predicted, on average, estimated, etc.) is required, and the correct increase must be stated in the proper units. The slope is 1.1, measured in hundred-thousands of dollars, so 1.1 × $100,000 = $110,000. This tests EK DAT-1.H.2 (slope = predicted change in ŷ per unit increase in x) and Skill 4.B (interpret in context with appropriate language). Key errors: A and D omit the unit conversion (stating $1.1 instead of $110,000 — failing to account for the hundred-thousands scale); B gets the amount right but drops the word "predicted," making a deterministic claim where the regression model gives only a predicted/estimated value; C has the wrong direction.
