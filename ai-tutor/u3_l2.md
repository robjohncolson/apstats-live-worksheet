**[U3-L2-Q01] MCQ**

An observational study found that the amount of sleep an employee gets each night is associated with job performance. The correlation coefficient was found to be \(r = 0.86\). A reader of the study concluded that more sleep causes employees to perform better. Why is such a conclusion not correct?

(A) The correlation value should equal 1 for such a conclusion.
(B) The correlation value should be negative for such a conclusion.
(C) The sample was not representative of the population.
(D) Causation cannot be determined from an observational study.
(E) The correlation value implies that less than 75% of the variability in job performance can be explained by sleep.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The study was observational; causation can only be determined from an experiment. In observational studies, we can identify associations and correlations between variables, but we cannot establish causal relationships because we do not control or manipulate the variables. Only controlled experiments where treatments are randomly assigned can provide evidence for causation (EK DAT-2.B.3). Choice A misunderstands the role of r — a perfect correlation is neither required nor sufficient for causation. Choice E refers to r², not causation; r² ≈ 0.74 here, but that has no bearing on causal inference from a non-experimental design.

**[U3-L2-Q02] MCQ**

A researcher conducted an experiment to study the effects of an herbal supplement on the duration of the common cold. From a sample of 50 people who had a cold, the researcher assigned 25 people to take the supplement each day. The other 25 people were asked to drink water each day and were not given the supplement. The researcher recorded the number of days the cold lasted for each person. What are the experimental units of the study?

(A) All people with a cold
(B) The sample of 50 people who had a cold
(C) The 25 people who were given the herbal supplement
(D) The 25 people who were asked to drink water and were not given the supplement
(E) The recorded number of days that the cold lasted for each person

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Experimental units are the smallest entities to which treatments are applied (EK DAT-2.A.4). In this experiment, treatments (supplement vs. water) were applied to individual people — so all 50 people in the study are the experimental units, regardless of which group they were assigned to. Choice A is the broader population (not just participants). Choices C and D each describe only one treatment arm — the common mistake is equating "experimental units" with just one group. Choice E names the response variable, not the units.

**[U3-L2-Q03] MCQ**

A certain monthly magazine has both print and online subscribers. Print subscribers are people who pay to have the magazine physically delivered to them each month. Online subscribers are people who pay to have access to the electronic version of the magazine. The editors of the magazine want to study how online subscribers feel about the design of the electronic version, and they will gather data from a sample. Which of the following is a sample of the population of interest?

(A) 50 subscribers to the magazine
(B) 50 print subscribers
(C) 50 online subscribers
(D) 50 people who buy a copy of the magazine at a newsstand
(E) 50 people who find an article in the magazine while searching online

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The research question specifically focuses on how online subscribers feel about the electronic design, so the population of interest (EK DAT-2.A.1) is online subscribers only. A sample must come from that population (EK DAT-2.A.2). Choice A includes print subscribers who are irrelevant to the question. Choice B includes only print subscribers — the wrong group entirely. Choices D and E include people who are not even subscribers, so they are outside the population from which the editors want to generalize (EK DAT-2.B.2).

**[U3-L2-Q04] MCQ**

A researcher selects a simple random sample of 1,200 women who are students at Midwestern colleges in the United States to use for an observational study. Which of the following describes the population to which it would be most reasonable to generalize the results?

(A) All students in the United States
(B) All college students in the United States
(C) All women who are students in the United States
(D) All students at Midwestern colleges in the United States
(E) All women who are students at Midwestern colleges in the United States

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): A sample is only generalizable to the population from which the sample was drawn (EK DAT-2.B.2). The sample has three defining characteristics: (1) women, (2) students, and (3) at Midwestern colleges in the United States. All three must be preserved in the target population. Choices A–D each drop at least one of these characteristics — broadening generalization beyond what the sample supports. Students commonly over-generalize to "all college students" or "all women students" without noticing that geography (Midwestern) is also a scope constraint.

**[U3-L2-Q05] MCQ**

Carla wants to investigate whether a person's political party affiliation causes the person to be more vocal about political issues. She plans to administer a survey to a large sample of people. Which of the following describes why the method of data collection used will prevent Carla from achieving her goal?

(A) Surveys are not a valid method of collecting data.
(B) Causation cannot be determined from a survey.
(C) Political opinions are less likely to be voiced on a survey.
(D) Carla needs to administer the survey to the entire population to determine causation.
(E) Some surveys might not be returned, which could result in bias.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Causation is determined only through experiments, not observational studies — and surveys are a type of observational study (EK DAT-2.A.3). Carla's goal is to establish that party affiliation causes increased vocality; but a survey merely observes what already exists without randomly assigning treatments (EK DAT-2.B.3). Choice A is false — surveys are a perfectly valid observational method. Choice C raises a response-bias concern that is unrelated to the causation question. Choice D is false — a census does not confer causal inference either. Choice E raises nonresponse bias, which is a sampling concern, not the fundamental barrier to causal conclusions.

**[U3-L2-Q06] MCQ**

A city planner is investigating traffic congestion at a certain intersection. To collect data, a camera will record the number of cars that pass through the intersection at different hours of the day and on different days of the week. Which of the following best describes the type of investigation being conducted by the city planner?

(A) The investigation is an experiment because the cars go through the intersection at random.
(B) The investigation is an experiment because the hours and the days are the treatments and the cars are the units.
(C) The investigation is an observational study because treatments are not imposed.
(D) The investigation is an observational study because only a sample of cars can go through the intersection on any given day.
(E) The investigation is a sample survey because the cars that are recorded will inform the city planner about the population of cars on city roads.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The defining feature of an observational study is that no treatments are imposed — the researcher simply records what naturally occurs (EK DAT-2.A.3). The city planner is observing natural traffic behavior without manipulating any variables. Choice A mistakes "random" arrival of cars for experimental random assignment — two completely different concepts. Choice B incorrectly labels hours/days as "treatments" when nothing is being assigned or manipulated; hours and days are observational categories, not imposed conditions (EK DAT-2.A.4 specifies that in an experiment treatments are *assigned*). Choice D offers a technically true statement but gives the wrong reason — sample size or sampling logistics do not determine study type. Choice E misidentifies the study as a survey; a survey collects self-reported data from people, which is not happening here.
