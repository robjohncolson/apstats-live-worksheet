[MCQ] U2-L9-Q05

PROMPT: Data were collected on two variables, x and y, to create a model to predict y from x. A scatterplot of the collected data revealed a curved pattern with a possible cubic relationship (y = ax³, where a is a constant) between the variables. Which of the following transformations would be most appropriate for creating linearity between the variables?

(A) Taking the cube of y
(B) Taking the cube root of y
(C) Taking the cube root of both y and x
(D) Taking the log of y
(E) Taking the log of both y and x

KEY: E

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): Tests EK DAT-1.J.1 on choosing an appropriate transformation. The relationship is a power function y = ax³. Taking log of both sides: log(y) = log(a) + 3·log(x), which is linear in log(x) with slope 3 and intercept log(a). This log-log transformation is the standard approach for linearizing power relationships. Distractor (D) — taking log of y only — would linearize an exponential relationship (y = a·e^(bx)), not a power relationship. Distractor (B) — cube root of y — partially undoes the cubing but does not fully linearize without also transforming x. Distractor (C) — cube root of both — gives y^(1/3) = a^(1/3)·x, which is linear only if the original is exactly cubic, but this is the specific transformation for that case and is less general. However, (E) log-log is the standard AP approach for power relationships in general. The reasoning field confirms: log-log is correct for power functions; log-y only is for exponential. Ask a student who picks (D): "If you only transform y, what kind of relationship does that linearize — exponential or power?"
