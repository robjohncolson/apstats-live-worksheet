**[MCQ] U8-L6-Q01**

A chi-square test for homogeneity was conducted to investigate whether the four high schools in a school district have different absentee rates for each of four grade levels. The chi-square test statistic and *p*-value of the test were 19.02 and 0.025, respectively. Which of the following is the correct interpretation of the *p*-value in the context of the test?

(A) Assuming that each high school has the same absentee rate for each grade level, there is a 2.5 percent chance of finding a test statistic 19.02 or smaller.
(B) Assuming that each high school has the same absentee rate for each grade level, there is a 2.5 percent chance of finding a test statistic 19.02 or larger.
(C) Assuming that each high school has a different absentee rate for each grade level, there is a 2.5 percent chance of finding a test statistic 19.02 or larger.
(D) There is a 2.5 percent chance that the absentee rate for each grade level at the four schools is the same.
(E) There is a 2.5 percent chance that the absentee rate for each grade level at the four schools is different.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The p-value is always computed assuming the null hypothesis is true — so the conditional clause must reference H₀ (same absentee rates), ruling out C. The p-value is the probability of obtaining a test statistic as extreme as or more extreme than observed, which in a chi-square test means as large or larger (chi-square is one-tailed right), ruling out A. Options D and E confuse the p-value with the probability that a hypothesis is true, a classic misconception addressed by EK DAT-3.K.1. Key B correctly states: under H₀, P(χ² ≥ 19.02) = 0.025.

**[MCQ] U8-L6-Q03**

The manager of the cafeteria at a local high school wanted to see if there was an association between a student's grade level and whether the student approves of the food choices in the cafeteria. The manager selected a random sample of students and asked if they approved of the food choices and also recorded the grade levels of the students. If a student was in ninth or tenth grade, he or she was labeled as an underclassman, and if the student was in eleventh or twelfth grade, he or she was labeled as an upperclassman. The table shows the results of the survey.

| | Underclassman | Upperclassman | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approves of the Cafeteria Food | 50 | 20 | 70 |
| Does Not Approve of the Cafeteria Food | 30 | 60 | 90 |
| Total | 80 | 80 | 160 |

Assuming that all conditions for inference have been met, which of the following equations gives the appropriate chi-square test statistic and the correct number of degrees of freedom to determine if there is an association between grade level and whether a student approves of the food choices in the cafeteria?

(A) χ² = (50−40)²/40 + (20−40)²/40 + (30−40)²/40 + (60−40)²/40 with 4 degrees of freedom
(B) χ² = (50−35)²/50 + (20−35)²/20 + (30−45)²/30 + (60−45)²/60 with 4 degrees of freedom
(C) χ² = (50−35)²/35 + (20−35)²/35 + (30−45)²/45 + (60−45)²/45 with 4 degrees of freedom
(D) χ² = (50−40)²/40 + (20−40)²/40 + (30−40)²/40 + (60−40)²/40 with 1 degree of freedom
(E) χ² = (50−35)²/35 + (20−35)²/35 + (30−45)²/45 + (60−45)²/45 with 1 degree of freedom

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): Two things must both be correct: the expected counts and the degrees of freedom. Expected counts use (row total × column total) / grand total — e.g., Approves × Underclassman = (70)(80)/160 = 35, not 40 (which would be the mean of all cells). This eliminates A and D. The formula places the expected count in the denominator (not the observed count), eliminating B. Degrees of freedom for a chi-square test for independence = (rows − 1)(columns − 1) = (2−1)(2−1) = 1, eliminating C (df = 4 is wrong). Only E has correct expected counts (35 and 45) and df = 1. Tests EK VAR-8.L.1 and EK VAR-8.H.1.

| | Northern | Southern | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remove Cash Lanes | 112 | 98 | 210 |
| Keep Cash Lanes | 89 | 105 | 194 |
| Total | 201 | 203 | 404 |

Which of the following is closest to the *p*-value of the appropriate test to investigate whether the proportion of people living in the northern part of the state who are in favor of removing the cash lane is different from the proportion of people living in the southern part of the state who are in favor of removing the cash lane?

(B) 0.1342
(C) 0.5235
(D) 0.6912
(E) 0.9329
