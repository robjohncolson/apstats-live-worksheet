**[MCQ] U7-L9-Q01**

A sociologist will conduct a two-sample t-test for a difference in means to investigate whether there is a significant difference, on average, between the salaries of people with bachelor's degrees and people with master's degrees. From a random sample of 32 people with a bachelor's degree, the average salary was $55,000 with standard deviation $3,500. From a random sample of 28 people with a master's degree, the average salary was $58,000 with a standard deviation of $4,000.

With a null hypothesis of no difference in the means, which of the following is the test statistic for the appropriate test to investigate whether there is a difference in population means (master's degree minus bachelor's degree)?

(A) t = (58,000 − 55,000) / √(3,500/28 + 3,500/32)
(B) t = (58,000 − 55,000) / √(4,000²/28 + 3,500²/32)
(C) t = √[(58,000 − 55,000) / (4,000²/28 + 3,500²/32)]
(D) t = (58,000 − 55,000) / √[(4,000 + 3,500)/(28 + 32)]
(E) t = (58,000 − 55,000) / √[(4,000² + 3,500²)/(28 + 32)]

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per the item's reasoning — the two-sample t-test statistic formula is t = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) / √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂) (EK VAR-7.I.1). The numerator is the difference of sample means: 58,000 − 55,000. The denominator is the standard error of the difference in sample means: √(4,000²/28 + 3,500²/32), using the sample variances (standard deviations squared) divided by their respective sample sizes. Choice A uses raw standard deviations (not squared) and mixes up the sample sizes; Choice C takes the square root of the entire fraction instead of just the denominator; Choice D and E pool the standard deviations and sample sizes incorrectly rather than keeping each group separate. The top distractor is likely A or E — students who forget to square the standard deviations or who pool them rather than computing separate variance/n terms for each group.

**[MCQ] U7-L9-Q02**

A two-sample t-test for a difference in means was conducted to investigate whether defensive players on a football team can bench-press more weight, on average, than offensive players. The conditions for inference were met, and the test produced a test statistic of t = 1.083 and a p-value of 0.15.

Based on the p-value and a significance level of α = 0.05, which of the following is the correct conclusion?

(A) Reject the null hypothesis because 0.15 > 0.05. There is not convincing evidence that defensive players can bench-press more weight, on average, than offensive players.
(B) Reject the null hypothesis because 0.15 > 0.05. There is convincing evidence that defensive players can bench-press more weight, on average, than offensive players.
(C) Fail to reject the null hypothesis because 0.15 > 0.05. There is not convincing evidence that defensive players can bench-press more weight, on average, than offensive players.
(D) Fail to reject the null hypothesis because 0.15 > 0.05. There is convincing evidence that defensive players can bench-press more weight, on average, than offensive players.
(E) Fail to reject the null hypothesis because 0.15 > 0.05. There is convincing evidence that defensive players can bench-press the same amount of weight, on average, as offensive players.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per the item's reasoning — when the p-value (0.15) is greater than the significance level α (0.05), the formal decision rule (EK DAT-3.H.1) requires failing to reject the null hypothesis. Failing to reject does not mean accepting the null, and it means there is not convincing evidence to support the alternative. Choices A and B both incorrectly say "reject" when p > α. Choice D correctly says "fail to reject" but then incorrectly asserts there IS convincing evidence — a direct contradiction of what failing to reject means. Choice E wrongly states "convincing evidence that they bench-press the same," which is also not a valid conclusion (failing to reject ≠ proving H₀ true). The key error to probe: students who confuse the direction of the p-value comparison or who think fail-to-reject means "accept H₀."

**[MCQ] U7-L9-Q03**

Animal researchers studying cows and horses conducted a two-sample t-test for a difference in means to investigate whether grazing cows eat more grass, on average, than grazing horses. All conditions for inference were met, and the test produced a test statistic of t = 1.664 and a p-value of 0.0487.

Which of the following is a correct interpretation of the p-value?

(A) The probability that cows eat more grass than horses, on average, is 0.0487.
(B) The probability that cows eat the same amount of grass as horses, on average, is 0.0487.
(C) Assuming that the mean amount of grass eaten by cows is greater than the mean amount of grass eaten by horses, the probability of observing a test statistic of at most 1.664 is 0.0487.
(D) Assuming that the mean amount of grass eaten by cows is equal to the mean amount of grass eaten by horses, the probability of observing a test statistic of at most 1.664 is 0.0487.
(E) Assuming that the mean amount of grass eaten by cows is equal to the mean amount of grass eaten by horses, the probability of observing a test statistic of at least 1.664 is 0.0487.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per the item's reasoning — this is a right-tailed test because the researchers are investigating whether cows eat more grass than horses (Ha: μ_cows > μ_horses). The p-value represents the probability of obtaining a test statistic equal to or greater than the observed value (1.664), computed assuming the null hypothesis is true (EK DAT-3.G.1) — i.e., assuming mean amounts are equal. That is the area under the t-curve to the right of t = 1.664. Choice A incorrectly treats the p-value as the probability a claim is true (a fundamental misconception about what p-values measure). Choice B treats it as P(H₀ true), also wrong. Choices C and D correctly reference assuming something in the condition but C uses the wrong assumption (alternative), and D correctly assumes null = equal but says "at most" instead of "at least," reversing the tail direction. E is the only choice with both the correct assumption (equal means, i.e., null true) and the correct tail direction (at least 1.664, right tail for a one-sided greater-than test).

**[MCQ] U7-L9-Q04**

City health officials will conduct a two-sample t-test for a difference in means to investigate whether stray dogs in the city have more fleas, on average, than do stray cats. A random sample of 22 stray dogs had a mean of 120 fleas and a standard deviation of 20 fleas. A random sample of 15 stray cats had a mean of 100 fleas and a standard deviation of 30 fleas.

Assuming a null hypothesis of no difference, which of the following is a correct test statistic for the test?

(A) t = (120 − 100) / √[(20 + 30)/(22 + 15)]
(B) t = (120 − 100) / √[(20² + 30²)/(22 + 15)]
(C) t = (120 − 100) / √(20²/22 + 30²/15)
(D) t = (120 − 100) / √(20/22 + 30/15)
(E) t = √[(120 − 100) / (20²/22 + 30²/15)]

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per the item's reasoning — the correct formula is t = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) / √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂) (EK VAR-7.I.1). Numerator: 120 − 100. Denominator: √(20²/22 + 30²/15) — sample variances (standard deviations squared) each divided by their own sample size, then summed under the square root. Choice A pools by adding standard deviations and dividing by the combined n (wrong on both counts). Choice B sums the squared SDs and divides by combined n (pooling error). Choice D uses raw SDs (not squared) divided by their respective n (forgetting to square). Choice E takes the square root of the entire ratio, not just the denominator (structural formula error). The most common distractor is D (remembering to separate the groups but forgetting to square the SDs) or B (remembering to square but pooling the sample sizes).

**[MCQ] U7-L9-Q05**

A two-sample t-test for a difference in means was conducted to investigate whether the average time to swim a lap with the freestyle stroke is different from the average time to swim a lap with the butterfly stroke. With all conditions for inference met, the test produced a test statistic of t = −2.073 and a p-value of 0.042.

Based on the p-value and a significance level of α = 0.05, which of the following is a correct conclusion?

(A) There is convincing statistical evidence that the average time to swim a lap with the freestyle stroke is less than the average time to swim a lap with the butterfly stroke.
(B) There is convincing statistical evidence that the average time to swim a lap with the freestyle stroke is different from the average time to swim a lap with the butterfly stroke.
(C) There is not convincing statistical evidence that the average time to swim a lap with the freestyle stroke is greater than the average time to swim a lap with the butterfly stroke.
(D) There is not convincing statistical evidence that the average time to swim a lap with the freestyle stroke is different from the average time to swim a lap with the butterfly stroke.
(E) There is not convincing statistical evidence that the average time to swim a lap with the freestyle stroke is less than the average time to swim a lap with the butterfly stroke.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per the item's reasoning — since the p-value (0.042) is less than the significance level α (0.05), we reject the null hypothesis (EK DAT-3.H.1), providing convincing statistical evidence to support the alternative hypothesis. This is a two-sided test (investigating whether times are different — not specifically greater than or less than), so the correct conclusion names the two-sided alternative. Choice A correctly states "convincing evidence" but draws a directional conclusion ("less than") rather than the two-sided "different from" that the test actually tested. Choice D and E correctly state "not convincing" but that contradicts the p < α decision — this is a "confuse reject vs. fail to reject" error. Choice C says "not convincing" about greater than — also wrong; the decision is reject, and the alternative was two-sided. Probe students who choose A: ask them what the alternative hypothesis was for this test.

**[MCQ] U7-L9-Q06**

Researchers on car safety studied driver reaction time and cell phone use while driving. Participants in the study talked on either a hands-free phone or a handheld phone while driving in a car simulator. A two-sample t-test for a difference in means was conducted to investigate whether the mean driver reaction time between the two groups of participants was different. All conditions for inference were met, and the test produced a test statistic of t = −2.763 and a p-value of 0.03.

Which of the following is a correct interpretation of the p-value?

(A) Assuming that the mean reaction times for hands-free and handheld phones are equal, the probability of obtaining a test statistic less than −2.763 is 0.03.
(B) Assuming that the mean reaction times for hands-free and handheld phones are equal, the probability of obtaining a test statistic greater than 2.763 or less than −2.763 is 0.03.
(C) Assuming that the mean reaction times for hands-free and handheld phones are not equal, the probability of obtaining a test statistic less than −2.763 is 0.03.
(D) Assuming that the mean reaction times for hands-free and handheld phones are not equal, the probability of obtaining a test statistic greater than 2.763 or less than −2.763 is 0.03.
(E) The probability that the mean reaction time for hands-free phones will be less than that for handheld phones is 0.03.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per the item's reasoning — this is a two-sided test investigating whether the mean reaction times are different. The p-value is the combined area in both tails: to the left of −2.763 and to the right of +2.763 (EK DAT-3.G.1). That combined area of 0.03 represents the probability of obtaining a test statistic at least as extreme as the observed value in either direction, assuming the null hypothesis (equal means) is true. Choice A correctly assumes H₀ true but only names one tail ("less than −2.763"), missing the two-sided nature. Choices C and D both assume the alternative ("not equal") in the conditional — a fundamental misunderstanding of p-value definition. Choice E treats the p-value as the probability a claim about the population is true. Key probe for students who pick A: ask what "two-sided" means for the p-value calculation and which tails contribute.

**[MCQ] U7-L9-Q08**

To prepare for a certification exam, candidates can use one of two exam preparation books, book J or book K. A two-sample t-test for a difference in means was conducted to investigate whether the average score on the exam for candidates using book J is less than the average score for candidates using book K. With all conditions for inference met, the test produced a test statistic of t = −1.356 and a p-value of 0.101.

Based on the p-value and a significance level of α = 0.05, which of the following is a correct conclusion?

(A) There is convincing statistical evidence that the average score of candidates using book J is equal to the average score of candidates using book K.
(B) There is convincing statistical evidence that the average score of candidates using book J is less than the average score of candidates using book K.
(C) There is not convincing statistical evidence that the average score of candidates using book J is less than the average score of candidates using book K.
(D) There is not convincing statistical evidence that the average score of candidates using book J is different from the average score of candidates using book K.
(E) There is not convincing statistical evidence that the average score of candidates using book J is greater than the average score of candidates using book K.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per the item's reasoning — since the p-value (0.101) is greater than the significance level α (0.05), we fail to reject the null hypothesis (EK DAT-3.H.1). This means there is not convincing evidence to support the alternative hypothesis that candidates using book J score less than those using book K. Choice B says "convincing evidence … less than" — this would require p ≤ α, which is not the case. Choice A says "convincing evidence … equal to" — failing to reject never means proving H₀ true; that's an over-reach. Choice D says "not convincing … different from" — the alternative was one-sided (J less than K), not two-sided; the conclusion should name the actual alternative tested. Choice E says "not convincing … greater than" — the alternative was about J being less, not greater. Choice C correctly reflects the actual alternative tested (J less than K) and the correct fail-to-reject outcome (not convincing evidence).

**[MCQ] U7-L9-Q09**

Zoologists studying two populations of tigers conducted a two-sample t-test for the difference in means to investigate whether the tigers in population X weigh more, on average, than the tigers in population Y. Two independent random samples were taken, and the difference between the sample means was calculated. All conditions for inference were met, and the test produced a p-value of 0.02.

Which of the following is a correct interpretation of the p-value?

(A) The probability that the mean weight for tigers in population X is greater than that for population Y is 0.02.
(B) The probability that the mean weight for tigers in population X is equal to that for population Y is 0.02.
(C) Assuming that the mean weights for populations X and Y are equal, the probability of observing a difference equal to the sample difference is 0.02.
(D) Assuming that the mean weights for populations X and Y are equal, the probability of observing a difference as great or greater than the sample difference is 0.02.
(E) Assuming that the mean weight for population X is greater than the mean weight for population Y, the probability of observing a difference as great as or greater than the sample difference is 0.02.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Per the item's reasoning — this is a right-tailed test because the zoologists are investigating whether population X weighs more than population Y (Ha: μ_X > μ_Y). The p-value is the area under the t-curve to the right of the test statistic, representing the probability of observing a difference equal to or greater than the sample difference, computed assuming the null hypothesis is true — i.e., that the population means are equal (EK DAT-3.G.1). Choice A treats the p-value as the probability the alternative hypothesis is true — a fundamental misconception. Choice B treats it as the probability H₀ is true. Choice C correctly assumes H₀ true but says "equal to" rather than "as great or greater than" — it describes only the point probability, not the tail area. Choice E correctly captures the direction (right tail, "as great or greater than") but uses the wrong conditional assumption: it assumes the alternative (μ_X > μ_Y) rather than the null. D is the only choice with both the correct assumption (means equal → null true) and the correct tail interpretation (at least as extreme as the observed difference).
