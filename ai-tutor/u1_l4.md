**[MCQ] U1-L4-Q01**

A market researcher asked a group of men and women to choose their favorite color design from a sample of advertisements. Which of the following statements is not supported by the table?

| | Red with Black | Green with Black | Yellow with Black | Red with Blue | Green with Blue | Yellow with Blue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Men | 21 | 15 | 8 | 12 | 35 | 9 |
| Women | 15 | 3 | 11 | 31 | 24 | 16 |

(A) More men than women chose the color design red with black.
(B) More women than men chose the color design yellow with black.
(C) For men, the number who chose a design with black was greater than the number who chose a design with blue.
(D) The color design chosen by the most people was green with blue.
(E) The total number of men surveyed by the market researcher was equal to the total number of women surveyed by the market researcher.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO UNC-1.D (describe categorical data represented graphically/in a table) and EK UNC-1.D.1 (graphical/tabular representations can be used to justify claims in context). The student must compute the group totals and compare them. For men: designs with black = 21+15+8 = 44; designs with blue = 12+35+9 = 56. Since 44 < 56, the claim in C is false — it is NOT supported. All other choices can be verified as true directly from the table (A: 21>15; B: 11>8; D: green with blue totals 35+24=59, the highest combined total; E: men total = 21+15+8+12+35+9 = 100, women total = 15+3+11+31+24+16 = 100). The top distractor is D — students may misread "most people" as just the men's column rather than summing both rows.

**[MCQ] U1-L4-Q02**

In a certain school district, students from grade 6 through grade 12 can participate in a school-sponsored community service activity. The following bar chart shows the relative frequencies of students from each grade who participate in the community service activity. Which of the following statements is supported by the bar chart?

[This item shows a chart in the quiz.]

Tutor-only chart description: A bar chart with grades 6–12 on the x-axis and relative frequency on the y-axis. The approximate bar heights (relative frequencies) are: Grade 6 = 0.30, Grade 7 = 0.30, Grade 8 = 0.375, Grade 9 = 0.55, Grade 10 = 0.45, Grade 11 = 0.50, Grade 12 = 0.10. Do not compute counts for the student or confirm exact bar values; instead ask the student to describe what they see and reason from the chart.

(A) The greatest number of participating students was in grade 9.
(B) The number of participating students in grade 6 was equal to the number of participating students in grade 7.
(C) The relative frequency of all participating students in grades 6 and 7 combined was 0.60.
(D) Grade 12 had the least relative frequency of participating students.
(E) Grade 11 had the greatest relative frequency of participating students.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item is a critical test of EK UNC-1.C.2 (bar height = number or proportion in each category) and LO UNC-1.D (describing categorical data from a graph). The key distinction is between relative frequency and count: A is wrong because the bar chart shows relative frequencies, not counts — without knowing the total enrollment per grade, you cannot determine which grade had the greatest number of participants. B is wrong for the same reason: equal relative frequencies do not imply equal counts if the grade sizes differ. C is wrong because relative frequencies for separate groups cannot be added unless they are proportions of the same whole. D is correct: Grade 12 has the lowest bar at approximately 0.10, making its relative frequency the smallest — this statement is directly readable from bar height. E is wrong because Grade 9 (≈0.55) is visibly higher than Grade 11 (≈0.50). The top distractor is A — students commonly confuse relative frequency (proportion of a group) with count.

**[MCQ] U1-L4-Q03**

A child psychologist asked 100 five year olds and 50 ten year olds to name their favorite color. Which of the following statements is supported by the table?

| | Red | Orange | Yellow | Green | Blue | Purple |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Five year olds | 21 | 15 | 8 | 10 | 35 | 11 |
| Ten year olds | 7 | 2 | 6 | 15 | 12 | 8 |

(A) The percentage of five year olds who selected red or blue as their favorite color is greater than the percentage of ten year olds who selected red or blue as their favorite color.
(B) The percentage of five year olds who selected yellow as their favorite color is greater than the percentage of ten year olds who selected yellow as their favorite color.
(C) The percentage of children who selected red, yellow, or blue as their favorite color was equal for both ages.
(D) Less than half of the five year olds selected red, yellow, or blue as their favorite color.
(E) Less than half of the ten year olds selected red, yellow, or blue as their favorite color.

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO UNC-1.E (compare multiple sets of categorical data, Skill 2.D) and EK UNC-1.E.1, along with EK UNC-1.B.1 (proportions and percentages convey the same information). Because the two groups have different totals (100 vs. 50), raw counts cannot be directly compared — students must convert to relative frequencies. A: Five year olds red+blue = 21+35=56 out of 100 = 56%; ten year olds = 7+12=19 out of 50 = 38%. 56% > 38% — supported. B: Five year olds yellow = 8/100=8%; ten year olds = 6/50=12%. 8% < 12% — not supported. C: Five year olds red+yellow+blue = 64/100=64%; ten year olds = 25/50=50%. Not equal. D: 64% > 50%, so more than half of five year olds — not supported. E: 25/50=50%, exactly half, not less than half. The top distractor is B or D — students often forget to convert to percentages and compare raw counts instead (a classic failure of LO UNC-1.E reasoning).

**[MCQ] U1-L4-Q04**

The following pie chart summarizes the results of a survey given to airlines about the primary reason for flight delays. Which of the following statements is supported by the pie chart?

[This item shows a chart in the quiz.]

Tutor-only chart description: A pie chart showing five slices for flight delay reasons with the following approximate percentages: Weather = 70%, Overbooking = 15%, Runway Closure = 8%, Other = 5%, Equipment Failure = 2%. Do not confirm the exact percentage values for the student; ask them to describe what they observe about the relative sizes of the slices and reason from there.

(A) The reason given most frequently was runway closure.
(B) More delays were caused by weather than by all other reasons combined.
(C) More delays were caused by runway closure than were caused by overbooking.
(D) Overbooking and runway closure accounted for greater than one-fourth of the reasons given for flight delays.
(E) The combined percentage for other and runway closure was equal to the percentage for overbooking.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO UNC-1.C (representing categorical data graphically) via EK UNC-1.C.3 (pie charts as an additional representation of relative frequencies) and LO UNC-1.D / EK UNC-1.D.1 (using graphical representations to justify claims). Weather accounts for 70% of the pie, which is more than the remaining 30% combined — B is directly supported. A is wrong: weather (70%) is the most frequent, not runway closure (8%). C is wrong: runway closure (8%) < overbooking (15%). D is wrong: overbooking + runway closure = 15+8 = 23%, which is less than 25% (one-fourth). E is wrong: other + runway closure = 5+8 = 13% ≠ overbooking 15%. The top distractor is D — students may round loosely or miscalculate whether 23% exceeds 25%.
