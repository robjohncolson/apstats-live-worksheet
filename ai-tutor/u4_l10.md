[MCQ] U4-L10-Q01

According to a recent survey, 31 percent of the residents of a certain state who are age 25 years or older have a bachelor's degree. A random sample of 50 residents of the state, age 25 years or older, will be selected. Let the random variable B represent the number in the sample who have a bachelor's degree. What is the probability that B will equal 40?

(A) C(50, 40) · (0.31)^40 · (0.69)^10
(B) C(50, 40) · (0.69)^40 · (0.31)^10
(C) C(40, 10) · (0.31)^40 · (0.69)^10
(D) C(40, 10) · (0.69)^40 · (0.31)^10
(E) 40 · (0.31)^50

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This is a binomial probability problem where success is selecting a person with a bachelor's degree. B has a binomial distribution with n = 50 trials, probability of success p = 0.31, and probability of failure q = 0.69. Applying EK UNC-3.B.1, P(B = 40) = C(50, 40) · (0.31)^40 · (0.69)^10 — the combination uses n = 50 (total trials) and x = 40 (successes), not 40 and 10. Choice B swaps p and q; choices C and D misuse n = 40 in the combination; choice E is not the binomial formula at all. The key distractor is B, which confuses which probability belongs to the success count vs. the failure count.

[MCQ] U4-L10-Q02

According to a recent survey, 81 percent of adults in a certain state have graduated from high school. If 15 adults from the state are selected at random, what is the probability that 5 of them have not graduated from high school?

(A) C(20, 15) · (0.19)^15 · (0.81)^5
(B) C(10, 5) · (0.19)^15 · (0.81)^15
(C) C(10, 5) · (0.81)^5 · (0.19)^10
(D) C(15, 5) · (0.19)^5 · (0.81)^10
(E) C(15, 5) · (0.81)^5 · (0.19)^10

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Let X = number of adults who have NOT graduated. The key first step is recognizing that "not graduating" is the success event here, so p = 1 − 0.81 = 0.19 and q = 0.81 (EK UNC-3.A.2). With n = 15 and x = 5, EK UNC-3.B.1 gives P(X = 5) = C(15, 5) · (0.19)^5 · (0.81)^10. Choice E swaps p and q, putting 0.81 as the success probability and 0.19 as the failure probability — the most common error when students forget to take the complement of the given graduation rate. Choices A–C use wrong values for n or the combination.
