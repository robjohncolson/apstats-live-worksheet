[MCQ] U7-L3-Q01

To assess the effectiveness of a kindergarten-readiness program, 15 children from a random sample were each given a diagnostic assessment before beginning the program and a follow-up assessment after completing the program. For each child, the difference in the score points between the two assessments was calculated and used to create the 95 percent confidence interval (20.1, 23.9). Assuming all conditions for inference are met, which of the following is a correct interpretation of the interval?

(A) For all children in the program, 95 percent of the children will have a mean difference in scores of between 20.1 points and 23.9 points.
(B) There is a 0.95 probability that the mean difference in scores for all children in the sample is between 20.1 points and 23.9 points.
(C) There is a 0.95 probability that the mean difference in scores for the children in the program is between 20.1 points and 23.9 points.
(D) We are 95 percent confident that the mean difference in scores for all children in the program is between 20.1 points and 23.9 points.
(E) We are 95 percent confident that the mean difference in scores for the children in the sample is between 20.1 points and 23.9 points.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal prematurely): The correct interpretation of a confidence interval is that we are 95 percent confident that the interval has captured the population mean. A confidence interval estimates the population parameter (mean difference for all children in the program), not the sample statistic. The confidence level refers to our confidence that the interval contains the true population parameter. Choice E is the most common distractor — it correctly uses "confident" language but misidentifies the subject as the sample rather than the population; per EK UNC-4.S.2 and EK UNC-4.S.3, the interpretation must name the population. Choices B and C incorrectly treat the confidence level as a probability about a fixed interval; the interval either captures the mean or it doesn't (EK UNC-4.S.1). Choice A wrongly treats the interval as if it describes where 95% of individual children fall.

[MCQ] U7-L3-Q02

Researchers are investigating the effectiveness of leg-strength training on cycling performance. A sample of 7 men will be selected to participate in a training program that lasts for one month. Peak power during cycling will be recorded for each man both before training and after training. The mean difference in times will be used to construct a 95 percent confidence interval for the mean difference in the population. When all other things remain the same, which of the following statements about the width of the interval is correct?

(A) The interval will be narrower if 15 men are used in the sample.
(B) The interval will be wider if 15 men are used in the sample.
(C) The interval will be narrower if 5 men are used in the sample.
(D) The interval will be narrower if the level is increased to 99% confidence.
(E) The interval will be wider if the level is decreased to 90% confidence.

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal prematurely): When all other things remain the same, the width of a confidence interval will decrease as the sample size increases (from n = 7 to n = 15). This is because the standard error of the mean decreases as sample size increases, leading to a smaller margin of error and thus a narrower confidence interval. Per EK UNC-4.U.1, increasing sample size narrows the interval. Choice B is the opposite. Choice C (n = 5, fewer than 7) would widen, not narrow, the interval. Choice D incorrectly claims increasing confidence level narrows — it actually widens (EK UNC-4.U.3). Choice E incorrectly claims decreasing the confidence level widens the interval — it actually narrows it.

[MCQ] U7-L3-Q03

Animal scientists studied foraging behavior of the scrub lizard, found in central Florida. Foraging is the process of searching for food. To study such behavior, the scientists recorded the number of head movements per minute for a sample of 63 lizards. A 95 percent confidence interval constructed from the sample is given as 2.7 ± 0.62 head movements per minute. Based on the interval, is a claim of 3 head movements per minute plausible?

(A) The claim is not plausible because 3 head movements per minute is contained within the interval.
(B) The claim is not plausible because 3 head movements per minute is not contained within the interval.
(C) The claim is not plausible because 95 percent of 3 is greater than the sample mean of 2.7.
(D) The claim is plausible because 3 head movements per minute is not contained within the interval.
(E) The claim is plausible because 3 head movements per minute is contained within the interval.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal prematurely): The claim is plausible. The confidence interval extends from 2.08 to 3.32 (2.7 − 0.62 = 2.08 and 2.7 + 0.62 = 3.32). Because the value 3 is contained in the interval, it is a plausible value for the population mean (EK UNC-4.T.1: the interval provides values that may provide sufficient evidence to support a claim). A value inside the interval is plausible; a value outside is not. Choices A and B reverse the logic of what "contained" means. Choice C is statistical nonsense. Choice D correctly notes that plausibility follows from containment but gets the containment fact backwards.

[MCQ] U7-L3-Q04

To investigate hospital costs for pets in a certain state, researchers selected a random sample of 46 owners of parrots who had recently taken their parrot to an animal hospital for care. The cost of the visit for each parrot owner was recorded and used to create the 95 percent confidence interval ($44.99, $80.27). Assuming all conditions for inference are met, which of the following is a correct interpretation of the interval?

(A) We are 95 percent confident that the mean cost of a hospital visit for all parrot owners in the state is between $44.99 and $80.27.
(B) We are 95 percent confident that the mean cost of a hospital visit for the parrot owners in the sample is between $44.99 and $80.27.
(C) For all parrot owners in the state, 95 percent of hospital visits for parrot care cost between $44.99 and $80.27.
(D) There is a 0.95 probability that the mean cost of a hospital visit for all parrot owners in the state is between $44.99 and $80.27.
(E) There is a 0.95 probability that the mean cost of a hospital visit for the parrot owners in the sample is between $44.99 and $80.27.

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal prematurely): The correct interpretation of a confidence interval is that we are 95 percent confident that the interval has captured the population mean. The confidence interval provides an estimate for the population parameter (mean cost for all parrot owners in the state), not the sample statistic or individual values. Per EK UNC-4.S.2 and EK UNC-4.S.3, the interpretation must reference the population, use "confident" language (not probability), and name the specific population in context. Choice B uses correct language but misidentifies "sample" instead of population — the most common student error. Choices D and E incorrectly treat the confidence level as a probability about the specific interval rather than a long-run capture rate (EK UNC-4.S.1). Choice C misinterprets the interval as containing 95% of individual visit costs rather than capturing the mean.

[MCQ] U7-L3-Q05

A manufacturer of piston rings for automobile engines frequently tests the width of the rings for quality control. Last week, a random sample of 15 rings were measured, and the mean and standard deviation of the sample were used to construct a 95 percent confidence interval for the population mean width of the rings. When all other things remain the same, which of the following conditions would have resulted in a wider interval than the one constructed?

I. A sample size of 20 with 95 percent confidence
II. A sample size of 15 with 99 percent confidence
III. A sample size of 12 with 95 percent confidence

(A) I only
(B) II only
(C) III only
(D) I and II
(E) II and III

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal prematurely): Both statements II and III give conditions that increase the width of the interval. With all else remaining the same, the width of a confidence interval increases if sample size decreases (statement III: from 15 to 12, per EK UNC-4.U.1 and EK UNC-4.U.2 — width is proportional to 1/√n so smaller n means wider) or if confidence level increases (statement II: from 95% to 99%, per EK UNC-4.U.3). Statement I would actually decrease the width because it increases sample size from 15 to 20, resulting in a smaller margin of error. Students often confuse the direction of both effects; the key diagnostic question is whether the change raises or lowers the critical value t* or raises or lowers the standard error.

[MCQ] U7-L3-Q06

Porcupines can cause damage to wood structures by chewing them. Researchers studied a liquid repellent designed to reduce such damage. A sample of 20 wooden blocks of the same size were treated with the repellent and left outside in an area where porcupines are known to live. After a certain amount of time, the blocks were inspected for the number of porcupine teeth marks visible. The data were used to create the 95 percent confidence interval (4.9, 5.8). Which of the following claims is supported by the interval?

(A) The expected number of porcupine teeth marks on a wooden block treated with the repellent is less than 5.
(B) The expected number of porcupine teeth marks on a wooden block treated with the repellent is 5.
(C) The mean number of porcupine teeth marks on all wooden blocks treated with the repellent is 6.
(D) The mean number of porcupine teeth marks on all wooden blocks treated with the repellent is greater than 6.
(E) The mean number of porcupine teeth marks on all wooden blocks treated with the repellent is less than 6.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal prematurely): All plausible values for the population mean are contained within the confidence interval (4.9 to 5.8). Since all values in this interval are less than 6, we can conclude that the mean number of porcupine teeth marks on all wooden blocks treated with the repellent is less than 6 (EK UNC-4.T.1: when every value in the CI lies below a comparison value, the interval supports a directional claim). Choices C and D claim the mean equals or exceeds 6, but 6 is outside the interval entirely. Choice A claims the mean is less than 5, but the lower bound of the interval is 4.9 — values between 4.9 and 5 are inside the interval, so the mean being less than 5 is not supported. Choice B pinpoints 5 as the mean, but a CI provides a range, not a single value.

[MCQ] U7-L3-Q07

Engineers at a tire manufacturing company investigated the effect of a new rubber compound on the tire life of a certain brand of tires. From a sample of 16 tires, the engineers constructed a 99 percent confidence interval for the mean tire life, in miles, as 62,550 ± 2,026. Suppose the company intends to claim a maximum tire life for advertising purposes. Based on the interval, of the following, which is the maximum plausible value for the mean tire life, in miles?

(A) 64,000
(B) 64,250
(C) 64,500
(D) 64,750
(E) 65,000

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal prematurely): The confidence interval is (60,524, 64,576), calculated as 62,550 ± 2,026. Of the listed tire life values, the maximum value that is contained in the interval is 64,500, since the upper bound 64,576 is greater than 64,500 but less than 64,750. Per EK UNC-4.T.1, only values inside the interval are plausible values for the population mean. Choice D (64,750) and Choice E (65,000) both exceed the upper bound of 64,576 and are therefore not plausible values. This item tests whether students can correctly apply the CI endpoint to identify what values are and aren't plausible — a direct application of Skill 4.D.

[MCQ] U7-L3-Q08

An environmental agency frequently samples the water in a region to ensure that the levels of a certain contaminant do not exceed 30 parts per billion (ppb). From 12 randomly selected samples of the water, the agency constructed the 99 percent confidence interval (22.5, 28.7). Assuming all conditions for inference are met, which of the following is a correct interpretation of the interval?

(A) For all water in the region, 99 percent of the water contains a level of the contaminant between 22.5 ppb and 28.7 ppb.
(B) We are 99 percent confident that the mean level of the contaminant in the sample is between 22.5 ppb and 28.7 ppb.
(C) We are 99 percent confident that the mean level of the contaminant in all the water in the region is between 22.5 ppb and 28.7 ppb.
(D) There is a 0.99 probability that the mean level of the contaminant in the sample is between 22.5 ppb and 28.7 ppb.
(E) There is a 0.99 probability that the mean level of the contaminant in all the water in the region is between 22.5 ppb and 28.7 ppb.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal prematurely): The correct interpretation of a confidence interval is that we are 99 percent confident that the interval has captured the population mean. The confidence interval estimates the population parameter (mean level of contaminant in all water in the region), not the sample statistic or individual measurements. Per EK UNC-4.S.2 and EK UNC-4.S.3, the interpretation must use "confident" (not probability) language and refer to the population, not the sample. Choice B uses correct "confident" language but refers to the sample rather than the population — the most common student error on CI interpretation. Choices D and E incorrectly frame the confidence level as a probability about the specific computed interval. [MCQ] U7-L3-Q09

Two independent random samples were collected from the same population to estimate the population mean. Sample A had a sample size of 25 and a sample mean of 50. The 95 percent confidence interval constructed from sample A had a margin of error of 4.2. Sample B had a sample size of n and a sample mean of x̄_B. The k percent confidence interval constructed from sample B had a margin of error of 3.7. Assume both samples had the same sample standard deviation. Which of the following values from sample B explains why the margin of error for sample A is greater than the margin of error for sample B?

(A) n = 25, x̄_B = 50, k = 95
(B) n = 25, x̄_B = 60, k = 95
(C) n = 25, x̄_B = 40, k = 95
(D) n = 20, x̄_B = 50, k = 99
(E) n = 30, x̄_B = 40, k = 90

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal prematurely): For sample B to have a smaller margin of error than sample A, it needs either a larger sample size or a lower confidence level (or both), since margin of error = t* × (s/√n). In option E, the sample size of sample B is greater than sample A (30 > 25, which decreases the standard error per EK UNC-4.U.1 and EK UNC-4.U.2) and the confidence level of sample B is less than sample A (90% < 95%, which lowers t* per EK UNC-4.U.3). Both factors reduce the margin of error. Sample mean x̄_B does not affect the margin of error — it only shifts the center of the interval. Choices A, B, and C all keep n = 25 and k = 95, so the margin of error would be the same as sample A (the mean is irrelevant). Choice D decreases n to 20 (which widens the interval) and increases k to 99% (which also widens), producing a larger margin of error, not a smaller one.
