**[MCQ] U8-L4-Q01**

Ecologists conducted a study to investigate the potential ecological impact of golf courses. Investigators monitored the reproductive success of bluebirds in birdhouses at nine golf courses and ten similar birdhouses at nongolf sites. Data on nests in birdhouses occupied only by bluebirds are shown in the table. If the proportions of nests occupied is the same for golf and nongolf sites, what would be the expected count of birdhouses with 1 nest in nongolf locations?

| | 0 nests | 1 nest | 2 or 3 nests | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Golf | 30 | 42 | 8 | 80 |
| Nongolf | 40 | 58 | 22 | 120 |
| Total | 70 | 100 | 30 | 200 |

(A) 40
(B) 42
(C) 50
(D) 58
(E) 60

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item directly tests LO VAR-8.H and EK VAR-8.H.1 — the expected count formula. The target cell is "Nongolf, 1 nest": row total = 120 (Nongolf), column total = 100 (1 nest), table total = 200. Applying the formula: (120 × 100) / 200 = 12000 / 200 = 60, so KEY = E. The top distractor is D (58), which is simply the observed count in that cell — students who skip the formula and read from the table will land there. Distractor A (40) matches the observed count in "Nongolf, 0 nests." The core misconception is treating observed counts as expected counts; push the student back to EK VAR-8.H.1 and ask them to identify row total, column total, and table total before calculating.

**[MCQ] U8-L4-Q02**

Nonnative speakers of English often have characteristics in their handwriting that differ depending on the speaker's first language. Handwriting samples from a random sample of Chinese, Malay, and Indian children in a school in Singapore were taken to investigate these differences. One characteristic of interest was a rounding at the top of the capital letter A. Data for the top-rounding characteristic for a sample of 153 children are shown in the table. If children from these countries exhibit top rounding in the same proportions, what is the expected count for Malay children who show top rounding?

| | Malay | Indian | Chinese | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yes | 9 | 21 | 8 | 38 |
| No | 42 | 31 | 42 | 115 |
| Total | 51 | 52 | 50 | 153 |

(A) 9/38
(B) 9/51
(C) 9
(D) (38)(51)/153
(E) (38)(9)/153

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests LO VAR-8.H and EK VAR-8.H.1 — applying the formula to a specific cell of a two-way table. The target cell is "Malay, Yes (top rounding)": row total = 38 (Yes), column total = 51 (Malay), table total = 153. The correct setup is (38)(51)/153, so KEY = D. The item is presented symbolically rather than numerically, testing whether the student knows which numbers belong in numerator versus denominator. Distractor E uses the observed cell count (9) in the numerator instead of the row total (38) — a common error where students confuse the observed value with the marginal total. Distractor C (= 9) is the raw observed count with no formula applied. Guide the student to identify which row total and which column total correspond to the target cell before they evaluate any answer choice.

**[MCQ] U8-L4-Q03**

The National Park Service writes materials for students to use while in the parks. In a study of the effectiveness of some of these materials, a random sample of students was selected to take a short quiz about oak trees after using these materials. A random sample of park professionals also took the quiz. Investigators compared classifications (low, medium, and high) of the crown shapes—the general shapes of the leafy parts of the trees—made by students in grades 6 through 12 with classifications made by professionals. Data from the study are shown in the table. If the professionals and the students do not differ in the distributions of their responses, which of the following is equal to the expected number of students who classify the crown shapes as medium?

| | Professionals | Students | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 54 | 43 | 97 |
| Medium | 48 | 39 | 87 |
| High | 7 | 9 | 16 |
| Total | 109 | 91 | 200 |

(A) 39
(B) (39)(87)/91
(C) (87)(91)/200
(D) (87)(109)/200
(E) (97)(91)/200

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests LO VAR-8.H and EK VAR-8.H.1 — expected count formula in a two-way table. The target cell is "Medium, Students": row total = 87 (Medium), column total = 91 (Students), table total = 200. Applying the formula: (87)(91)/200, so KEY = C. Distractor A (= 39) is the observed count for that cell — the classic error of reading the table instead of using the formula. Distractor D swaps the column: it uses the Professionals column total (109) instead of the Students column total (91), which would give the expected count for "Medium, Professionals" instead. Distractor E uses the wrong row total (97 = Low row) instead of the Medium row total (87). Ask the student to state out loud which row and which column define the target cell before they pick any answer.
