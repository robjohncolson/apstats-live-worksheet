**[MCQ] U2-L5-Q01**

A botanist found a correlation between the length of an aspen leaf and its surface area to be 0.94. Why does the correlation value of 0.94 not necessarily indicate that a linear model is the most appropriate model for the relationship between length of an aspen leaf and its surface area?

(A) The value must be exactly 1 or −1 to indicate a linear model is the most appropriate model.
(B) The value must be 0 to indicate a linear model is the most appropriate model.
(C) A causal relationship should be established first before determining the most appropriate model.
(D) The value of 0.94 implies that only 88% of the data have a linear relationship.
(E) Even with a correlation value of 0.94, it is possible that the relationship could still be better represented by a nonlinear model.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests EK DAT-1.B.3 directly — a correlation close to 1 or −1 does not necessarily mean that a linear model is appropriate. A value close to 1 does indicate a strong linear relationship, but it does not rule out the possibility that an exponential, quadratic, or other nonlinear model could fit the data even better. Correlation measures only the strength of linear association; another model form might fit equally well or better. Choice A is wrong: r = 1 or −1 signals perfect linear association, not that only those exact values justify a linear model. Choice D is a classic misconception that conflates r with r² (r² = 0.94² ≈ 0.88 is the coefficient of determination, not a statement about what fraction of points are "linear"). Choice C conflates causation with model-fitting — establishing causation is a separate question entirely (EK DAT-1.C.2). The top distractor is likely D; push students who pick it to distinguish r from r² and return to EK DAT-1.B.3.

**[MCQ] U2-L5-Q02**

The table shows several values of x and their corresponding values of y. Which of the following is closest to the correlation between x and y?

| x | y |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 4 |
| 3 | 7 |
| 4 | 8 |
| 5 | 12 |

(A) −0.98
(B) −0.95
(C) 0.20
(D) 0.95
(E) 0.98

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO DAT-1.B / EK DAT-1.B.1 and DAT-1.B.2 — determining correlation from data, typically using technology. As x increases from 1 to 5, y increases from 3 to 12 in a nearly linear pattern, establishing that the relationship is positive (eliminating A and B). The growth is quite consistent, suggesting a strong association close to 1 rather than a weak one (eliminating C). Between D (0.95) and E (0.98), the data show a very tight linear trend with only minor deviations, so r ≈ 0.98 is the closer value; technology or the formula confirms this. The key Socratic move: ask the student to first decide the sign (positive or negative) and then decide strength (close to 0 or close to 1), before choosing between D and E. Reference DAT-1.B.2: using technology is the expected method.

**[MCQ] U2-L5-Q03**

A restaurant manager collected data on the number of customers in a party in the restaurant and the time elapsed until the party left the restaurant. The manager computed a correlation of 0.78 between the two variables. What information does the correlation provide about the relationship between the number of customers in a party at the restaurant and the time elapsed until the party left the restaurant?

(A) The relationship is linear because the correlation is positive.
(B) The relationship is not linear because the correlation is positive.
(C) The parties with a larger number of customers are associated with the longer times elapsed until the party left the restaurant.
(D) The parties with a larger number of customers are associated with the shorter times elapsed until the party left the restaurant.
(E) There is no relationship between the number of customers in a party at a table in the restaurant and the time required until the party left the restaurant.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO DAT-1.C / EK DAT-1.B.1 and DAT-1.C.1 — interpreting the sign and meaning of a positive correlation in context. A positive correlation (r = 0.78) means that as values of one variable increase, values of the other variable tend to increase as well (DAT-1.B.1). In context: larger parties are associated with longer times elapsed before leaving. Choice D gets the direction exactly backwards — a common error when students confuse positive and negative correlation. Choices A and B both draw unwarranted conclusions about the form of the relationship: correlation magnitude and sign alone cannot confirm or deny that the relationship is linear (DAT-1.B.3); a scatterplot is needed. Choice E is wrong because r = 0.78 clearly indicates a meaningful positive association, not the absence of one. The key Socratic move: ask the student what sign of r means about direction, then ask them to translate that into the restaurant context using the variable names.

**[MCQ] U2-L5-Q04**

For a random sample of 20 professional athletes, there is a strong, linear relationship between the number of hours they exercise per week and their resting heart rate. For the athletes in the sample, those who exercise more hours per week tend to have lower resting heart rates than those who exercise less. Which of the following is a reasonable value for the correlation between the number of hours athletes exercise per week and their resting heart rate?

(A) 0.71
(B) 0.00
(C) −0.14
(D) −0.87
(E) −1.00

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO DAT-1.B and DAT-1.C / EK DAT-1.B.1 and DAT-1.C.1 — reasoning about the sign and magnitude of r from a verbal description. The problem states that more exercise is associated with lower resting heart rates: as one variable increases, the other decreases — a negative direction, eliminating A and B. The relationship is described as "strong," so r should be close to −1, eliminating C (−0.14 is weak). Between D (−0.87) and E (−1.00), a perfect −1.00 would require every data point to fall exactly on a line, which is virtually never true for real biological data; "strong but not perfect" is captured by −0.87. The top distractor is E — students may think "strong = perfect." Push them to distinguish "strong" from "perfect" using DAT-1.C.1.

**[MCQ] U2-L5-Q05**

A researcher in Alaska measured the age (in months) and the weight (in pounds) of a random sample of adolescent moose. When the least-squares regression analysis was performed, the correlation was 0.59. Which of the following is the correct way to label the correlation?

(A) 0.59 months per pound
(B) 0.59 pounds per month
(C) 0.59
(D) 0.59 months times pounds
(E) 0.59 month pounds

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO DAT-1.C / EK DAT-1.C.1 — the property that correlation r is unit-free. Unlike slope (which carries units of y per unit of x) or the variables themselves (months, pounds), the correlation coefficient is a pure number between −1 and 1 with no units attached. Choices A, B, D, and E all attach units to r, which is incorrect by definition. The distractor A (months per pound) is especially tempting because it mimics the format of a slope, and students may confuse r with the regression slope. The Socratic move: ask the student what the formula for r does to the units (it standardizes both variables by their standard deviations — each z-score is dimensionless, so their product is dimensionless). Reference DAT-1.C.1: "r is unit-free."

**[MCQ] U2-L5-Q06**

A tennis ball was thrown in the air. The height of the ball from the ground was recorded every millisecond from the time the ball was thrown until it reached the height from which it was thrown. The correlation between the time and height was computed to be 0. What does this correlation suggest about the relationship between the time and height?

(A) There is no relationship between time and height.
(B) There is no linear relationship between time and height.
(C) The distance the ball traveled upward is the same as the distance the ball traveled downward.
(D) The correlation suggests that there is measurement or calculation error.
(E) The correlation suggests that more measurements should be taken to better understand the relationship.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO DAT-1.C / EK DAT-1.C.1 and DAT-1.B.3 — understanding that r = 0 indicates no linear relationship, not no relationship at all. A tennis ball follows a parabolic (quadratic) trajectory: it rises and then falls symmetrically. This is a strong nonlinear relationship, but because the pattern is symmetric around the peak, the linear trend averages out to zero — yielding r ≈ 0 despite a clear and predictable functional connection between time and height. Choice A is the critical misconception to diagnose: "no linear relationship" is not the same as "no relationship." The ball's height is completely determined by time (via a quadratic function) — there is a relationship, just not a linear one. Choices C, D, and E each introduce ideas not supported by the data or the correlation itself. The Socratic move: ask the student what the ball's path looks like (draw it mentally), then ask whether that shape is linear. Reference DAT-1.C.1 ("r = 0 indicates no linear association") and DAT-1.B.3 (correlation does not determine model appropriateness).
