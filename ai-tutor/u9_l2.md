- Skill 1.D — Identify an appropriate inference method for confidence intervals.
- Skill 4.C — Verify that inference procedures apply in a given situation.
- Skill 3.D — Construct a confidence interval, provided conditions for inference are met.

- EU UNC-4 — An interval of values should be used to estimate parameters, in order to account for uncertainty.

- LO UNC-4.AC — Identify an appropriate confidence interval procedure for a slope of a regression model. [Skill 1.D]
  - EK UNC-4.AC.1 — For a simple random sample of n observations, the sample regression line ŷ = a + bx is an estimate of the population regression line μ_y = α + βx. The sample slope b estimates the population slope β. The standard deviation of the residuals, s = √(Σ(y_i − ŷ_i)² / (n−2)), estimates σ (uses n−2 in the denominator because two parameters, α and β, must be estimated).
  - EK UNC-4.AC.2 — The mean of the sampling distribution for b equals β (μ_b = β). The standard deviation of the sampling distribution for b is σ_b = σ / (σ_x √n).
  - EK UNC-4.AC.3 — The appropriate confidence interval for the slope of a regression model is a t-interval for the slope.

- LO UNC-4.AD — Verify the conditions to calculate confidence intervals for the slope of a regression model. [Skill 4.C]
  - EK UNC-4.AD.1 — Conditions for a confidence interval for slope:
    a. The true relationship between x and y is linear (check with residual plot).
    b. The standard deviation of y, σ_y, does not vary with x (check with residual plot for constant spread).
    c. Independence: data collected via random sample or randomized experiment; if sampling without replacement, check n ≤ 10%N.
    d. For a particular x, the y-values are approximately normally distributed (check graphical representations of residuals); if distribution is skewed, n should be greater than 30.

- LO UNC-4.AE — Determine the given margin of error for the slope of a regression model. [Skill 3.D]
  - EK UNC-4.AE.1 — Margin of error = t* × SE_b (critical value times standard error of the slope).
  - EK UNC-4.AE.2 — Standard error for slope: SE_b = s / (s_x √(n−1)), where s is the estimate of σ and s_x is the sample standard deviation of the x values.

- LO UNC-4.AF — Calculate an appropriate confidence interval for the slope of a regression model. [Skill 3.D]
  - EK UNC-4.AF.1 — The point estimate for the slope is the sample slope b (the slope of the least-squares regression line).
  - EK UNC-4.AF.2 — The interval estimate for the slope is b ± t*(SE_b).

**[Item 1 of 9] — [MCQ] U9-L2-Q01**

A scientist is investigating whether percent concentration can be used to predict density in apple juice. A scientist selected a random sample of 12 apple juice varieties and recorded the density, in pounds per cubic inch, and the percent concentration of each apple juice variety. The scientist wants to estimate the mean change in the density, in pounds per cubic inch, for each increase of 1 percent concentration of apple juice.

Assuming the conditions for inference have been met, which of the following inference procedures is most appropriate for this investigation?

(A) A linear regression \(t\)-interval for slope
(B) A matched-pairs \(t\)-interval for a mean difference
(C) A two-sample \(t\)-interval for a difference between means
(D) A one-sample \(t\)-test for means
(E) A two-sample \(z\)-interval for a difference between proportions

KEY: A

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): This tests LO UNC-4.AC / Skill 1.D — identifying the correct inference procedure. The scientist is modeling a linear relationship between two quantitative variables (percent concentration → density) and wants to estimate the mean change in the response per unit change in the explanatory variable — that is exactly the slope of the population regression line. A linear regression t-interval for slope is the right procedure (EK UNC-4.AC.3). Distractors B, C, D describe procedures for comparing means across groups or a single mean; distractor E is for proportions. The key signal is the phrase "mean change for each increase of 1" — that phrase defines the slope parameter β.

The table provides bivariate quantitative data for a single group, not two groups to compare (ruling out C and D) or a proportion (ruling out E). **[Item 3 of 9] — [MCQ] U9-L2-Q03**

A teacher conducted a regression analysis to investigate the relationship between student height and femur length. Computer output from the linear regression analysis is shown in the table. The analysis was performed on a sample of 24 students.

| Term | Coef | SE Coef |
|---|---|---|
| Constant | 28.34 | 0.945 |
| Femur length | 1.73 | 0.023 |

Assume that the conditions for inference for the slope of the regression equation have been met. Which of the following defines the margin of error for a 99.5 percent confidence interval for the slope of the least-squares regression equation?

(A) \(3.104(0.023)\)
(B) \(3.104(1.73)\)
(C) \(3.119(0.023)\)
(D) \(3.119(0.945)\)
(E) \(3.119(1.73)\)

KEY: C

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): This tests LO UNC-4.AE / EK UNC-4.AE.1 — margin of error = t* × SE_b. Two decisions must be made correctly: (1) the SE to use is the SE Coef for the slope variable (femur length), 0.023 — not the SE for the constant (0.945) and not the slope coefficient itself (1.73); (2) the critical value t* comes from a t-distribution with df = n − 2 = 24 − 2 = 22. For a 99.5% confidence interval, the tail area is 0.25% on each side, so t* is at the 99.75th percentile of t(22) = 3.119 (not 3.104, which is for t(23) or a different df). Distractor A has the right SE but wrong df/t* (3.104 is t* for df = 23 — off by one on the degrees of freedom). Distractor E has the right t* but uses the slope coefficient instead of SE. Distractors B and D both use an incorrect SE (1.73 or 0.945).

**[Item 4 of 9] — [MCQ] U9-L2-Q04**

A random sample of 15 college soccer players were selected to investigate the relationship between heart rate and maximal oxygen uptake. The heart rate and maximal oxygen uptake were recorded for each player during a training session. A regression analysis of the data was conducted, where heart rate is the explanatory variable and maximal oxygen uptake is the response variable.

If a 95 percent confidence interval is constructed for the slope of the population regression line, which of the following is a condition that must be checked?

(A) The true relationship between heart rate and maximal oxygen uptake is linear.
(B) The correlation between heart rate and maximal oxygen uptake is not equal to zero.
(C) The confidence interval is not biased.
(D) The point \((\bar{x}, \bar{y})\) falls on the regression line.
(E) The slope is not equal to zero.

KEY: A

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): This tests LO UNC-4.AD / EK UNC-4.AD.1 — the conditions for a confidence interval for slope. Condition (a) in UNC-4.AD.1 is that the true relationship between x and y is linear; this is checked via residual plots. This condition is fundamental — if the true relationship is not linear, the entire regression inference framework is invalid. Distractors B and E describe hypotheses that might be tested (non-zero correlation / non-zero slope) but are not preconditions for constructing the interval. Distractor D (the point (x̄, ȳ) lies on the regression line) is always mathematically true for least-squares and is not a condition to check. Distractor C ("not biased") is vague and not one of the four named conditions (linear, equal σ_y, independence, normality of responses).

**[Item 6 of 9] — [MCQ] U9-L2-Q06**

Twenty members of an athletic club are studying the relationship between the time it takes an individual athlete to reach a given level of fatigue during exercise (time to fatigue, measured in minutes) and athletic performance. For each member, time to fatigue and a performance score were recorded. The computer output of the regression analysis is shown in the table.

| Term | Coef | SE Coef | T |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constant | 39.88 | 4.24 | 9.41 |
| Time to fatigue | 3.92 | 0.71 | 5.52 |

Which of the following is a 90 percent confidence interval for the slope of the regression line relating performance score and time to fatigue? Assume that the conditions for inference are met.

(A) \(3.92 \pm 1.645(0.71)\)
(B) \(3.92 \pm 1.729(0.71)\)
(C) \(3.92 \pm 1.734(0.71)\)
(D) \(39.88 \pm 1.729(4.24)\)
(E) \(39.88 \pm 1.734(4.24)\)

For a 90% CI, the tail area is 5% on each side, so t* is at the 95th percentile of t(18) = 1.734. **[Item 8 of 9] — [MCQ] U9-L2-Q08**

When computing a confidence interval for the slope of a regression line, a plot of the residuals versus the fitted values can be used to check which of the following conditions?

(A) The variables \(x\) and \(y\) are inversely related.
(B) The standard deviation of \(y\) does not vary as \(x\) varies.
(C) The correlation is not equal to zero.
(D) The observations are independent.
(E) The confidence interval contains zero.

KEY: B

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): This tests LO UNC-4.AD / EK UNC-4.AD.1(b) — the condition that the standard deviation of y (σ_y) does not vary with x (constant variance / homoscedasticity). A residual plot versus fitted values is specifically used to check this condition: if the vertical spread of residuals is roughly constant across all fitted values, constant variance is supported. Distractor A confuses direction of association with a condition for inference. Distractor C (correlation ≠ 0) is not a condition checked by a residual plot — correlation is about the strength/direction of the linear relationship, not the spread. Distractor D (independence) is checked by the study design (random sample / randomized experiment / 10% condition), not by a residual plot. Distractor E (CI contains zero) cannot be determined from a residual plot; it requires actually computing the interval.

**[Item 9 of 9] — [MCQ] U9-L2-Q09**

Eliza plans on constructing a confidence interval for the slope of a regression line. She creates the residual plot shown to check the conditions for creating the interval. Which of the following conditions appear to be met based on the residual plot?

I. The true relationship between \(x\) and \(y\) is linear.
II. The standard deviation of \(y\) is constant for different levels of \(x\).
III. The value of 0 is contained in the confidence interval.

(A) I only
(B) II only
(C) III only
(D) I and II only
(E) II and III only

KEY: D

[This item shows a chart in the quiz.]

Tutor-only chart description (do not compute for the student): The residual plot is a scatter plot with x-axis labeled "Explanatory Variable" (range 0–60, tick interval 10) and y-axis labeled "Residual" (range −10 to 10, tick interval 5), with a reference line at y = 0. There are approximately 20 data points plotted. The points are: (5, 6), (8, −3), (10, 5), (12, 3), (15, −5), (18, 2), (20, 4), (22, −4), (24, −5), (26, −2), (28, −3), (30, −2), (32, −6), (35, 5), (38, −3), (40, 9), (42, 10), (45, 5), (48, −2), (50, −5), (52, −8). The residuals appear randomly scattered above and below zero with no systematic pattern (supporting linearity, condition I). The vertical spread of residuals appears roughly constant across the range of x-values (supporting constant variance, condition II). Whether 0 is in the confidence interval (condition III) cannot be determined from a residual plot — it requires computing the interval.

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): This tests LO UNC-4.AD / EK UNC-4.AD.1 — interpreting a residual plot to assess conditions for slope inference. Statement I (linearity): the residuals show no systematic curvature — they scatter randomly around zero, suggesting the linear model is appropriate (EK UNC-4.AD.1a). Statement II (constant σ_y): as x increases from 5 to 52, the spread of the residuals does not fan out or narrow — variance appears constant (EK UNC-4.AD.1b). Statement III (CI contains 0): this is a conclusion about the interval, not a condition. A residual plot only addresses the conditions (linear, constant variance, normal residuals, independence) — it gives no information about where the CI falls. Therefore I and II are supported, III is not determinable → answer D.
