**[U5-L7-Q01]** For which of the following conditions is it not appropriate to assume that the sampling distribution of the sample mean is approximately normal?

(A) A random sample of 8 taken from a normally distributed population
(B) A random sample of 50 taken from a normally distributed population
(C) A random sample of 10 taken from a population distribution that is skewed to the right
(D) A random sample of 75 taken from a population distribution that is skewed to the left
(E) A random sample of 100 taken from a population that is uniform

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): It is not appropriate to assume the sampling distribution of the sample mean is approximately normal for choice C. Because the distribution is skewed, the sample size needs to be at least 30 to assume normality for the sampling distribution (UNC-3.R.2). A sample size of 10 from a skewed distribution is too small to invoke the Central Limit Theorem. Choices A and B are fine because the population is normal — UNC-3.R.1 applies for any n from a normal population. Choices D and E have n ≥ 30 (or n = 75, 100), so CLT covers them. The key distractor is A: students may think n = 8 is too small, but the normal-population path (UNC-3.R.1) has no minimum n requirement.

**[U5-L7-Q02]** At a certain high school, the distribution of backpack weight is approximately normal with mean 19.7 pounds and standard deviation 3.1 pounds. A random sample of 5 backpacks will be selected, and the weight, in pounds, of each backpack will be recorded.

For samples of size 5, which of the following is the best interpretation of P(x̄ > 22) ≈ 0.05?

(A) The probability that each of the 5 backpacks selected will have a weight above 22 pounds is approximately 0.05.
(B) The probability that each of the 5 backpacks selected will have a weight above 19.7 pounds is approximately 0.05.
(C) The probability that the population mean is greater than 22 pounds is approximately 0.05.
(D) For all samples of size 5, approximately 5% of the sample will have a probability greater than 22 pounds.
(E) For all samples of size 5, the probability that the sample mean will be greater than 22 pounds is approximately 0.05.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The notation x̄ (x-bar) represents the sample mean, not individual values and not the population mean (UNC-3.S.1). This is a probability statement about the sampling distribution of the sample mean — the probability that the mean of a random sample of size 5 exceeds 22 pounds is about 0.05. Choice A confuses the sample mean with all individual values simultaneously. Choice C confuses the sample mean probability with a claim about the population mean. Choice D is nonsensical (individual observations don't have probabilities greater than 22 pounds). Students should be pushed to articulate that x̄ is a statistic, not an individual observation, and that the statement describes the behavior of repeated samples (UNC-3.S.1).

**[U5-L7-Q03]** The distribution of prices for a certain car model is approximately normal with mean $21,800 and standard deviation $400. A random sample of 4 cars of the model will be selected.

What is the correct unit of measure for the mean of the sampling distribution of x̄?

(A) Dollars
(B) Models
(C) Cars
(D) Samples
(E) There are no units associated with the mean of the sampling distribution.

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The unit for the mean of the sampling distribution of x̄ is the same as the unit for the individual sample means, which is dollars (UNC-3.Q.1 / UNC-3.S.1). The sampling distribution preserves the units of measurement from the original variable — it is a distribution of sample means, and each sample mean is measured in dollars. The top distractor is E: students sometimes think that because the sampling distribution is an abstract construct about repeated samples, it loses the original units. Push students to connect the units to what x̄ actually measures.

**[U5-L7-Q04]** A fair die has its faces numbered from 1 to 6. Let random variable F represent the number landing face up when the die is tossed. The probability distribution for the random variable has mean 3.5 and standard deviation 1.7078.

Consider a simulation with 400 trials designed to estimate the sampling distribution of the sample mean for 5 tosses of the die. For each trial, the die is tossed 5 times, and the mean of the 5 values landing face up is recorded.

The mean and standard deviation of the results of the simulation should be close to which of the following?

(A) Mean 3.5 and standard deviation 1.7078
(B) Mean 3.5 and standard deviation 0.7638
(C) Mean 3.5 and standard deviation 0.0854
(D) Mean 17.5 and standard deviation 1.7078
(E) Mean 17.5 and standard deviation 0.7638

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Since 400 trials is large, the mean of the 400 simulated sample means will be close to the mean of the sampling distribution, which equals the population mean of 3.5 (UNC-3.Q.1: μ_x̄ = μ). The standard deviation will be close to σ_x̄ = σ/√n = 1.7078/√5 ≈ 0.7638 (UNC-3.Q.1). Choice A is wrong because it uses the population standard deviation rather than the standard deviation of the sampling distribution — students who pick A are confusing the distribution of individual die rolls with the sampling distribution of sample means. Choice C uses σ/n rather than σ/√n. Choices D and E multiply the mean by n rather than keeping it equal to μ, a common error. The key skill tested is applying the formula σ_x̄ = σ/√n correctly (Skill 3.B).

**[U5-L7-Q05]** The mean number of pets owned by the population of students at a large high school is 3.2 pets per student with a standard deviation of 1.7 pets. A random sample of 16 students will be selected and the mean number of pets for the sample will be calculated.

What is the mean of the sampling distribution of the sample mean for samples of size 16?

(A) 1.7
(B) 3.2
(C) 3.2/√16
(D) 1.7/√16
(E) √(1.7/16)

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The mean of the sampling distribution of the sample mean is equal to the population mean (UNC-3.Q.1: μ_x̄ = μ = 3.2). This is a fundamental property — the expected value of the sample mean equals the population mean regardless of sample size. Choice D is the standard deviation of the sampling distribution (σ_x̄ = σ/√n = 1.7/√16 = 0.425), not the mean — students who pick D are applying the standard deviation formula when the question asks for the mean. Choice A is the population standard deviation. Choices C and E misapply division by √n to the mean rather than the standard deviation. Push students to clearly distinguish which parameter formula (mean vs. standard deviation) applies.

**[U5-L7-Q06]** For which of the following is the shape of the sampling distribution of the sample mean approximately normal?

I. A random sample of size 5 from a population that is approximately normal
II. A random sample of size 10 from a population that is strongly skewed to the right
III. A random sample of size 60 from a population that is strongly skewed to the left

(A) I only
(B) III only
(C) I and II only
(D) I and III only
(E) I, II, and III

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): If the population is normal, the sampling distribution is approximately normal for any sample size — this covers condition I (UNC-3.R.1). If the population is not normal, the sampling distribution is approximately normal if n ≥ 30 — this covers condition III (n = 60 ≥ 30, UNC-3.R.2). In condition II, the population is strongly skewed and n = 10 < 30, so the CLT condition is not met; the sampling distribution will also be skewed. Students who choose E are incorrectly applying the CLT to n = 10 from a skewed population. This item directly tests understanding of the two pathways to normality in UNC-3.R.1 and UNC-3.R.2.

**[U5-L7-Q07]** The distribution of age for players of a certain professional sport is strongly skewed to the right with mean 26.8 years and standard deviation 4.2 years. Consider a random sample of 4 players and a different random sample of 50 players from the population.

Which of the following statements is true about the sampling distributions of the sample mean ages for samples of size 4 and samples of size 50?

(A) Both will be skewed to the right, and the mean for size 50 will be closer to 26.8 than the mean for size 4.
(B) Both will be skewed to the right, and the standard deviation for size 50 will be closer to 4.2 than the standard deviation for size 4.
(C) Both will be approximately normal, and the mean for size 50 will be closer to 26.8 than the mean for size 4.
(D) Only the sampling distribution for size 4 will be approximately normal, and the standard deviation for both will be 4.2.
(E) Only the sampling distribution for size 50 will be approximately normal, and the mean for both will be 26.8.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The sampling distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normal for non-normal populations only when n ≥ 30 (UNC-3.R.2). n = 4 does not meet this threshold, so the sampling distribution for size 4 will still be skewed right. n = 50 ≥ 30, so the CLT applies and that sampling distribution is approximately normal. Critically, both sampling distributions have the same mean equal to the population mean of 26.8 years (UNC-3.Q.1: μ_x̄ = μ for any n). Choice A is wrong because the size-50 distribution is normal (not still skewed) and both means equal 26.8. Choice B is wrong because size-50 gives a smaller SD (4.2/√50 ≈ 0.594), not one closer to 4.2. Choice C is wrong because n = 4 is insufficient for CLT. Choice D has the normality verdict backwards and incorrectly states both SDs equal 4.2.

**[U5-L7-Q08]** A reading specialist wanted to estimate the mean word length, in number of letters, for an elementary school history textbook. The specialist took repeated random samples of size 100 words and estimated the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution to be 4.9 letters and 0.15 letter, respectively.

Based on the estimates for the sampling distribution, which of the following provides the best estimates of the population parameters?

(A) Mean 4.9 letters and standard deviation 0.015 letter
(B) Mean 4.9 letters and standard deviation 0.15 letter
(C) Mean 4.9 letters and standard deviation 1.5 letters
(D) Mean 0.49 letter and standard deviation 0.15 letter
(E) Mean 49 letters and standard deviation 15 letters

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The mean of the sampling distribution equals the population mean (UNC-3.Q.1: μ_x̄ = μ), so the population mean is estimated as 4.9 letters. The standard deviation of the sampling distribution is σ_x̄ = σ/√n, so σ = σ_x̄ × √n = 0.15 × √100 = 0.15 × 10 = 1.5 letters (UNC-3.Q.1). Choice B confuses the sampling distribution SD with the population SD — students who pick B are not reversing the formula. Choice A divides again (perhaps dividing 0.15 by 10), a double-division error. Choices D and E apply arithmetic to the mean, which stays fixed at 4.9. Students need to understand the inverse relationship: to recover σ from σ_x̄, multiply by √n, not divide.

**[U5-L7-Q09]** At a large corporation, the distribution of years of employment for the employees has mean 20.6 years and standard deviation 5.3 years. A random sample of 100 employees was selected and surveyed about employee satisfaction. The sample of employees had a mean 20.3 years and standard deviation 6.1 years.

Remy claims that the mean of the sampling distribution of the sample mean for samples of size 100 is 20.6 years. Is Remy's claim correct?

(A) No. The mean of the sampling distribution is 20.3 years.
(B) No. The mean of the sampling distribution is 20.3 employees.
(C) No. The mean of the sampling distribution is 5.3 years.
(D) No. The mean of the sampling distribution is 20.6 employees.
(E) Yes. The mean of the sampling distribution is 20.6 years.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Remy's claim is correct. The mean of the sampling distribution of the sample mean equals the population mean, which is 20.6 years (UNC-3.Q.1: μ_x̄ = μ). The sample statistics (x̄ = 20.3 years, s = 6.1 years) are just one realization from the sampling distribution and do not change the theoretical properties — a single sample's statistics are not the same as the sampling distribution's parameters (UNC-3.S.1). Choice A confuses the observed sample mean from one sample (20.3) with the mean of the entire sampling distribution. Choice D is a units trap: the unit is years, not employees. Choice C confuses the population standard deviation with the mean. Students must distinguish between a sample statistic (one outcome) and a sampling distribution parameter (a theoretical property based on the population).
