[MCQ] U4-L8-Q01

The following table shows the probability distribution for the number of books a student typically buys at the annual book fair held at an elementary school. Let the random variable B represent the number of books a student buys at the next book fair. What is the expected value of B?

| Number of Books | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Probability | 0.35 | 0.20 | 0.15 | 0.10 | 0.07 | 0.08 | 0.04 | 0.01 |

(A) 0
(B) 1.00
(C) 1.79
(D) 3.50
(E) 28

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests LO VAR-5.C / EK VAR-5.C.2 — computing the expected value using μ_X = Σ x_i · P(x_i). The reasoning from the item: E(B) = 0(0.35) + 1(0.20) + 2(0.15) + 3(0.10) + 4(0.07) + 5(0.08) + 6(0.04) + 7(0.01) = 1.79. The top distractors expose common errors: (A) confuses expected value with the mode or minimum; (D) 3.50 is the simple average of the values 0–7 (ignoring probabilities as weights), a classic mistake when students forget EK VAR-5.C.2 requires probability-weighted sums, not arithmetic means of the possible values; (E) is the sum of all possible values without any probability weighting at all.

[MCQ] U4-L8-Q02

At a certain bakery, the price of each doughnut is $1.50. Let the random variable D represent the number of doughnuts a typical customer purchases each day. The expected value and variance of the probability distribution of D are 2.6 doughnuts and 3.6 doughnuts², respectively. Let the random variable P represent the price of the doughnuts that a typical customer purchases each day. Which of the following is the standard deviation, in dollars, of the probability distribution of P?

(A) 1.5(3.6)
(B) 1.5√(3.6)
(C) √(1.5(3.6))
(D) 1.5(2.6)
(E) 1.5√(2.6)

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests the linear transformation rule from EK VAR-5.C.3 and the relationship between variance and standard deviation. The reasoning from the item: P = 1.5 · D, so when a random variable is multiplied by a constant, the standard deviation is also multiplied by that constant — σ_P = 1.5 · σ_D = 1.5 · √(3.6). The key distractors: (A) multiplies the constant by the variance rather than the standard deviation — confusing Var(P) = 1.5²·Var(D) with σ_P; (C) takes the square root of 1.5 times the variance, mixing up variance and standard deviation; (D) mistakenly applies the constant to the mean rather than the standard deviation; (E) uses the mean (2.6) instead of the standard deviation (√3.6), showing confusion about which parameter is being requested.

[MCQ] U4-L8-Q03

The quality control manager at a factory records the number of equipment breakdowns each day. Let the random variable Y represent the number of breakdowns in one day. The standard deviation of Y is 0.28. Which of the following is the best interpretation of the standard deviation?

(A) The number of breakdowns on a randomly selected day is expected to be 0.28.
(B) The number of breakdowns on a randomly selected day will be 0.28 away from the mean.
(C) The average number of breakdowns per day for a random sample of days is expected to be 0.28.
(D) On average, the number of breakdowns per day varies from the mean by about 0.28.
(E) The number of breakdowns per day for a random sample of days is expected to be 0.28 away from the mean.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests LO VAR-5.D / EK VAR-5.D.1 — interpreting the standard deviation in context. The reasoning from the item: the standard deviation of a random variable is, roughly speaking, the average or typical amount of variability from the mean for the population. Key word: "on average" and "varies … by about." (A) describes the expected value (mean), not the standard deviation. (B) uses "will be" (certainty) rather than "on average" — every individual day's deviation is not exactly 0.28. (C) confuses a population parameter with a sample statistic and also describes the mean. (E) introduces a sample ("random sample of days") and "expected to be" — the standard deviation is a population-level parameter describing typical variation, not the guaranteed deviation for any one sample.

[MCQ] U4-L8-Q04

Data were collected on the number of days per week that members visit a certain fitness center. The values varied from 0 to 7, and a distribution of relative frequencies for the values was created. Let the random variable X represent the number of days per week that a member visits. The mean of X is 3.12. Which of the following statements is the best interpretation of the mean?

(A) Each member visits the fitness center 3 or 4 days per week.
(B) The average number of days that each member visits the fitness center is 3.12 days per week.
(C) Half the members visit the fitness center 3 days per week or less, and the other half visit 4 days per week or more.
(D) The long-run average resulting from repeated sampling of members of the fitness center will approach 3.12 days per week.
(E) For a random sample of members selected from the population, the average number of visits for the sample will be 3.12 days per week.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests LO VAR-5.D / EK VAR-5.D.1 — the mean of a random variable is a population parameter, specifically the long-run average based on repeated sampling from the population (not a guarantee for any one sample or person). The reasoning from the item: the mean of a random variable is the long-run average based on repeated sampling from the population. (A) wrongly implies every individual member visits exactly 3 or 4 days. (B) sounds plausible but conflates the population parameter with a claim that each individual averages 3.12 — the mean is a population-level expectation, not a per-person average. (C) describes the median, not the mean. (E) is a common close distractor: it describes a sample statistic (the sample mean) rather than the population parameter — EK VAR-5.D.1 requires the interpretation to reference the population/long-run behavior.

[MCQ] U4-L8-Q05

Let the random variable Q represent the number of students who go to a certain teacher's office hour each day. The standard deviation of Q is 2.2. Which of the following is the best interpretation of the standard deviation?

(A) On average, the number of students going to an office hour varies from the mean by about 2.2 students.
(B) For a randomly selected office hour, the number of students who will go is 2.2.
(C) For a randomly selected office hour, the number of students expected to go will vary from the mean by 2.2 students.
(D) For a random selection of office hours, the average number of students expected to go is 2.2.
(E) For a random selection of office hours, the average number of students expected to go will vary from the mean by 2.2 students.

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item again tests LO VAR-5.D / EK VAR-5.D.1 — interpreting standard deviation as the typical (average) variability from the mean across the population. The reasoning from the item: the standard deviation is, roughly speaking, the average or typical amount of variability from the mean of the population. The critical phrase is "on average … varies from the mean by about." (B) states the value IS the number of students — confusing the standard deviation with the mean or an actual outcome. (C) uses "will vary by" with certainty for a single randomly selected office hour — no single observation is guaranteed to deviate by exactly 2.2. (D) states 2.2 is the average number going — confusing standard deviation with the mean. (E) combines a sample frame with the "varies from the mean" language but adds "expected to go," mixing mean and standard deviation ideas together.

[MCQ] U4-L8-Q06

For a certain dog breed, the number of puppies in a litter typically varies from 2 to 6. The following table shows the probability distribution of the random variable N, where N represents the number of puppies in a litter. Also shown are the squared deviations, or distances, from the expected value of 4.5 for the distribution. What is the variance of the distribution?

| Number of puppies | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Squared deviation | 6.25 | 2.25 | 0.25 | 0.25 | 2.25 |
| Probability | 0.05 | 0.15 | 0.25 | 0.35 | 0.20 |

(A) 1.12
(B) 1.25
(C) 1.41
(D) 1.58
(E) 2.25

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests LO VAR-5.C / EK VAR-5.C.3 — computing variance as Var(X) = Σ (x_i − μ_X)² · P(x_i). The reasoning from the item: Var(N) = 6.25(0.05) + 2.25(0.15) + 0.25(0.25) + 0.25(0.35) + 2.25(0.20) = 0.3125 + 0.3375 + 0.0625 + 0.0875 + 0.45 = 1.25. The table provides the squared deviations directly to isolate whether the student can correctly apply probability-weighted sums. (A) 1.12 likely results from incorrectly weighting or summing a subset of terms. (C) 1.41 is √1.99 or a similar arithmetic error — or the student confused variance with standard deviation (√1.25 ≈ 1.118, so this is a different error path). (D) 1.58 is another arithmetic slip. (E) 2.25 is a single squared deviation value — the student who picks this has not summed the weighted deviations at all.
