**[MCQ] U7-L8-Q02**

A reporter responsible for the food section of a magazine investigated the belief that grocery stores sell beef at a higher price in the fall than in the spring. The reporter selected independent random samples of grocery-store beef prices in November and April and computed the mean and standard deviation for the samples. Which of the following are the correct null and alternative hypotheses for the reporter's investigation, where μ_F represents the mean price of beef in the fall and μ_S represents the mean price of beef in the spring?

(A) H₀: x̄_F − x̄_S = 0, Hₐ: x̄_F − x̄_S < 0
(B) H₀: x̄_F − x̄_S = 0, Hₐ: x̄_F − x̄_S > 0
(C) H₀: μ_F − μ_S = 0, Hₐ: μ_F − μ_S ≠ 0
(D) H₀: μ_F − μ_S = 0, Hₐ: μ_F − μ_S < 0
(E) H₀: μ_F − μ_S = 0, Hₐ: μ_F − μ_S > 0

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The null hypothesis states there is no difference between the population mean price in fall and spring; the alternative states fall prices are higher (one-sided, μ_F > μ_S, i.e., μ_F − μ_S > 0), matching the reporter's specific belief (EK VAR-7.G.1). Options A and B use sample statistics (x̄) instead of population parameters (μ) — a direct violation of LO VAR-7.G, since hypotheses are always about population parameters. Option C uses parameters but a two-sided alternative, which does not match the directional claim. Option D has the inequality reversed (< 0 implies spring is higher). The top distractor is B: same direction but wrong notation (x̄ instead of μ).

**[MCQ] U7-L8-Q10**

They recorded the length of time it took them to solve the cube 18 times each during a one-month period. Let μ_A equal the mean time it took Alice to solve the puzzle cube and μ_S equal the mean time it took Sean. Option D uses sample statistics (x̄) instead of parameters (μ). Option E reverses the labeling (Sean minus Alice) and uses a one-sided alternative — wrong direction and wrong test type. The top distractor is A: students see "Alice" named first and assume the one-sided direction, missing that both siblings claim to be faster, making the research question symmetric.
