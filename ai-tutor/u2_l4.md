[MCQ] U2-L4-Q01

Researchers observed the grouping behavior of deer in different regions. The following scatterplot shows data collected on the size of the group and the percent of the region that was woodland. The relationship between group size and percent woodland appears to be negative and nonlinear. Which of the following statements explains such a relationship?

[This item shows a chart in the quiz.]

Tutor-only chart description (do not share this with the student — use it to guide questioning only): Scatterplot with x-axis "Percent Woodland" (0 to 100, tick interval 10) and y-axis "Group Size" (0 to 12, tick interval 2). No grid lines. 12 data points: (3, 12), (5, 10), (7, 8), (8, 5.5), (8.5, 5.5), (9, 4.8), (15, 5.8), (50, 3), (60, 2.8), (80.5, 2), (92, 1.5), (100, 1.8). The points drop steeply from roughly (3, 12) down to about (15, 5.8), then flatten and continue decreasing slowly through (100, 1.8) — a clear nonlinear, concave-up curve. Do not compute specific values for the student; prompt them to describe what they see.

(A) As the percent of woodland increases, the number of deer observed in a group decreases at a fairly constant rate.
(B) As the percent of woodland increases, the number of deer observed in a group increases at a fairly constant rate.
(C) As the percent of woodland increases, the number of deer observed in a group decreases quickly at first and then more slowly.
(D) As the percent of woodland increases, the number of deer observed in a group increases quickly at first and then more slowly.
(E) As the percent of woodland increases, the number of deer observed in a group remains fairly constant.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The item directly tests whether students can read direction and form from a scatterplot (DAT-1.A.2, DAT-1.A.4). A negative relationship means group size decreases as percent woodland increases — eliminating B, D, and E. The nonlinear qualifier is the key discriminator between A and C: "fairly constant rate" describes a linear decline (A), but the data show a steep initial drop that levels off — the defining signature of a nonlinear (curved) decrease, which C captures as "quickly at first and then more slowly." The item's own reasoning confirms this: "The group size decreases quickly at first when woodland percentage is low, then decreases more slowly as woodland percentage continues to increase." Students who pick A likely understand the direction but miss the non-constant rate of change that makes the relationship nonlinear. Guide them to look at the gap between points at low vs. high woodland percentages.

[MCQ] U2-L4-Q02

Which of the following is the best description of a positive association between two variables?

(A) The values will create a line when graphed on a scatterplot.
(B) The values will create a line with positive slope when graphed on a scatterplot.
(C) As the value of one of the variables increases, the value of the other variable tends to decrease.
(D) As the value of one of the variables increases, the value of the other variable tends to increase.
(E) All values of both variables are positive.

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests the precise definition of positive association (DAT-1.A.2, DAT-1.A.3). Positive association is about the direction of co-movement between two variables — not about the shape of the pattern (A and B incorrectly require linearity), not about the sign of the data values themselves (E is a common but wrong intuition). C describes a negative association. D is correct because it captures the fundamental definition: when one variable increases, the other tends to increase as well. The word "tends" is important and AP-appropriate — positive association is a tendency, not a guarantee for every data point. Students attracted to B conflate "positive association" with "linear with positive slope," which is a linearity claim beyond what association requires.

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item requires students to apply all three dimensions of a scatterplot description — direction, form, and strength (DAT-1.A.1, DAT-1.A.2, DAT-1.A.4, DAT-1.A.5).
