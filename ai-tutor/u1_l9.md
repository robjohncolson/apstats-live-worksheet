KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO UNC-1.N (comparing graphical representations) via EK UNC-1.N.1. The IQR equals the length of the box in a boxplot (Q3 − Q1). The medians differ (3.5 vs. 4.0), the whisker extents differ (ruling out equal range), and the minimums and maximums differ — eliminating A, C, D, and E. (A) The median of the earthquake disturbances is equal to the median of the mining disturbances.
(B) The median of the earthquake disturbances is less than the median of the mining disturbances.
(C) The range of the earthquake disturbances is equal to the range of the mining disturbances.
(D) The range of the earthquake disturbances is less than the range of the mining disturbances.
(E) The mode of the earthquake disturbances is equal to the mode of the mining disturbances.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO UNC-1.N and LO UNC-1.O (comparing graphical representations and summary statistics) via EK UNC-1.N.1 and EK UNC-1.O.1. To locate the median in a relative-frequency histogram, cumulate bar heights until the running total first exceeds 0.50. For earthquake disturbances: 0.35 + 0.20 = 0.55, so the median falls in the interval ending at 0.4 (the second bar). For mining disturbances: 0.00 + 0.22 + 0.35 = 0.57, so the median falls in the interval ending at 0.8 (the third bar). Because 0.4 < 0.8, the median for earthquakes is less than the median for mining, confirming B. The top distractor is likely A (equal medians) because students may compare the peak bars visually without cumulating. The range comparison (C/D) can be dismissed because both histograms span from roughly 0–1.4, and the mode bars differ (first bar for earthquake, third bar for mining), ruling out E. **[MCQ] U1-L9-Q03**

The following table shows summary statistics for the number of hours a group of students spent playing video games last Monday and last Saturday. Based on the summary statistics, which of the following gives the best comparison of the range and the interquartile range (\(IQR\)) of the two days?

| Day | Minimum | Q1 | Median | Q3 | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | 0 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 12 |
| Saturday | 1 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 18 |

(A) The range and \(IQR\) of hours played on Monday are both greater than the range and \(IQR\) of hours played on Saturday.
(B) The range and \(IQR\) of hours played on Monday are both less than the range and \(IQR\) of hours played on Saturday.
(C) The range and \(IQR\) of hours played on Monday are both equal to the range and \(IQR\) of hours played on Saturday.
(D) The range of hours played on Monday is greater than the range of hours played on Saturday, and the \(IQR\) of hours played on Monday is less than the \(IQR\) of hours played on Saturday.
(E) The range of hours played on Monday is less than the range of hours played on Saturday, and the \(IQR\) of hours played on Monday is greater than the \(IQR\) of hours played on Saturday.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO UNC-1.O (comparing summary statistics) via EK UNC-1.O.1. Range = maximum − minimum; IQR = Q3 − Q1. Monday: range = 12 − 0 = 12 hours, IQR = 4 − 1 = 3 hours. Saturday: range = 18 − 1 = 17 hours, IQR = 8 − 4 = 4 hours. Both the range (12 < 17) and IQR (3 < 4) for Monday are less than the corresponding values for Saturday, confirming B. The top distractor is likely E, which correctly identifies Monday's range as less but incorrectly claims Monday's IQR is greater — a student who reverses the IQR subtraction or misreads Q1/Q3 would land there. Direct a stuck student to EK UNC-1.O.1 and ask them to write out the formula for range and IQR, then substitute the values from each row of the table one at a time.
