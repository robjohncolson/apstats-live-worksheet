**[MCQ] U4-L6-Q01**

For which of the following probability assignments are events A and B independent?

(A) P(A ∩ B^c) = 0.3, P(A ∩ B) = 0.12, and P(A^c ∩ B) = 0.4.
(B) P(A ∩ B^c) = 0.3, P(A ∩ B) = 0.3, and P(A^c ∩ B) = 0.3.
(C) P(A ∩ B^c) = 0.1, P(A ∩ B) = 0.1, and P(A^c ∩ B) = 0.4.
(D) P(A ∩ B^c) = 0.3, P(A ∩ B) = 0.0, and P(A^c ∩ B) = 0.2.
(E) P(A ∩ B^c) = 0.5, P(A ∩ B) = 0.1, and P(A^c ∩ B) = 0.4.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): Independence requires P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B) (EK VAR-4.E.2). For choice C: P(A) = P(A ∩ B) + P(A ∩ B^c) = 0.1 + 0.1 = 0.2; P(B) = P(B ∩ A) + P(B ∩ A^c) = 0.1 + 0.4 = 0.5. Then P(A) · P(B) = (0.2)(0.5) = 0.1, which equals P(A ∩ B) = 0.1 — independence confirmed. The top distractor is choice A: students may assume P(A ∩ B) = 0.12 looks "small enough" to indicate independence, but the actual check requires computing P(A) and P(B) from the partition values first, then verifying the product — a step most distractors are designed to short-circuit.

**[MCQ] U4-L6-Q02**

A company is considering purchasing the mineral rights to two different mountains. The probability that it will purchase the mineral rights to the first mountain is 0.55. The probability that it will purchase the mineral rights to the second mountain is 0.4. Assuming the decisions to purchase the mineral rights to each mountain are made independently, what is the probability that it will purchase the mineral rights to exactly one of the two mountains?

(A) 0.18
(B) 0.22
(C) 0.33
(D) 0.51
(E) 0.95

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): "Exactly one" means either the first is purchased and not the second, or the second is purchased and not the first. Because the events are independent (EK VAR-4.E.2), P(F ∩ S^c) = P(F) · P(S^c) = (0.55)(0.6) = 0.33 and P(F^c ∩ S) = P(F^c) · P(S) = (0.45)(0.4) = 0.18; these two outcomes are mutually exclusive, so P(exactly one) = 0.33 + 0.18 = 0.51. Choice E (0.95) is the addition-rule result for "at least one" — a common error when students confuse "exactly one" with "at least one." Choice A (0.18) is only the second-mountain-only piece; choice C (0.33) is only the first-mountain-only piece.

**[MCQ] U4-L6-Q03**

A student is applying to two different agencies for scholarships. Based on the student's academic record, the probability that the student will be awarded a scholarship from Agency A is 0.55 and the probability that the student will be awarded a scholarship from Agency B is 0.40. Furthermore, if the student is awarded a scholarship from Agency A, the probability that the student will be awarded a scholarship from Agency B is 0.60. What is the probability that the student will be awarded at least one of the two scholarships?

(A) 0.60
(B) 0.62
(C) 0.71
(D) 0.73
(E) 0.95

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): "At least one" calls for the addition rule: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B) (EK VAR-4.E.4). The given conditional probability P(B | A) = 0.60 lets us find P(A ∩ B) via the multiplication rule (Topic 4.5): P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B | A) = (0.55)(0.60) = 0.33. Then P(A ∪ B) = 0.55 + 0.40 − 0.33 = 0.62. Note that P(B | A) = 0.60 ≠ P(B) = 0.40, so A and B are NOT independent here — a key conceptual check (EK VAR-4.E.1/VAR-4.E.2). Choice A (0.60) is the given conditional probability P(B | A), not the union. Choice C (0.71) likely results from omitting subtraction of the intersection. Choice E (0.95) is the naive sum P(A) + P(B) with no subtraction.
