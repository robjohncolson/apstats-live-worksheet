[MCQ] U4-L5-Q01

One student from a high school will be selected at random. Let A be the event that the selected student is a student athlete, and let B be the event that the selected student drives to school. If P(A ∩ B) = 0.08 and P(B|A) = 0.25, what is the probability that the selected student will be a student athlete?

(A) 0.02
(B) 0.17
(C) 0.32
(D) 0.33
(E) 3.13

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): The item tests LO VAR-4.D and EK VAR-4.D.2. By the multiplication rule, P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B|A), so 0.08 = P(A) · 0.25. Solving gives P(A) = 0.08 / 0.25 = 0.32. The top distractors are (D) 0.33, which comes from adding rather than dividing (0.08 + 0.25 = 0.33), and (E) 3.13, which inverts the division (0.25 / 0.08). Both reveal a misapplication of the multiplication rule formula. Redirect students who pick these to EK VAR-4.D.2 and ask them to write out the formula before substituting.

[MCQ] U4-L5-Q02

The seniors at three high schools were surveyed about their plans after graduation. The following table shows the responses, classified by high school. One senior from the high schools will be selected at random. What is the probability that the senior selected will not be from High School B given that the senior responded with a choice other than college?

|               | Work | Military | College | Undecided | Total |
|---------------|------|----------|---------|-----------|-------|
| High School A |  99  |    49    |   138   |    63     |  349  |
| High School B |  62  |    26    |   156   |    54     |  298  |
| High School C |  83  |    31    |   124   |    71     |  309  |
| Total         | 244  |   106    |   418   |   188     |  956  |

(A) 156/418
(B) 538/956
(C) 262/418
(D) 658/956
(E) 396/538

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO VAR-4.D and EK VAR-4.D.1. The condition is "responded other than college," so the denominator is restricted to non-college responders: 956 − 418 = 538. Among those 538, the ones NOT from High School B are from A (99 + 49 + 63 = 211) and C (83 + 31 + 71 = 185), totaling 396. So the correct answer is 396/538. Common errors: (B) 538/956 uses the wrong denominator (all seniors, ignoring the given condition) — redirect to EK VAR-4.D.1 and ask what "given that" does to the sample space. (D) 658/956 uses an incorrect count over the whole group. Prompt students who struggle to first identify the restricted sample space (the given condition), then count the favorable outcomes within it.

[MCQ] U4-L5-Q03

Students at a local elementary school were shown a painting and asked which emotion — joy, happiness, love, or anger — they felt by looking at the painting. The students were classified by their age. The following table summarizes the responses of the students by age-group. One student from the school will be selected at random. What is the probability that the student is in the age-group of 6 to 8 years given that the selected student responded joy?

|              | Joy | Happiness | Love | Anger | Total |
|--------------|-----|-----------|------|-------|-------|
| 6 to 8 years |  28 |    20     |  40  |  18   |  106  |
| 9 to 11 years|  61 |    25     |  80  |  60   |  226  |
| Total        |  89 |    45     | 120  |  78   |  332  |

(A) 89/332
(B) 28/332
(C) 28/106
(D) 106/332
(E) 28/89

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests LO VAR-4.D and EK VAR-4.D.1. The given condition is "responded joy," so the denominator shrinks to the 89 total joy responders. Of those, 28 were in the 6-to-8 age-group, giving 28/89. Common errors: (B) 28/332 ignores the conditioning event entirely (joint probability rather than conditional) — redirect to EK VAR-4.D.1 and ask what the denominator should be when we already know the student responded joy. (C) 28/106 reverses the condition, giving P(joy | 6 to 8 years) instead of P(6 to 8 years | joy). Use this distractor to reinforce the asymmetry of conditional probability and the importance of identifying which event is the given condition.
