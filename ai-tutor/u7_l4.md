[MCQ] U7-L4-Q01

Last year the mean cost for a one-bedroom rental in a certain city was $1,200 per month. Eli is looking for a one-bedroom apartment and is investigating whether the mean cost is less now than what it was last year. A random sample of apartments had a sample mean of $1,180 per month. Assuming all conditions for inference are met, Eli will conduct a hypothesis test as part of his investigation.

Which of the following is the correct set of hypotheses?

(A) H₀: μ = 1,200 / Hₐ: μ > 1,200
(B) H₀: μ = 1,200 / Hₐ: μ < 1,200
(C) H₀: x̄ = 1,200 / Hₐ: x̄ < 1,200
(D) H₀: x̄ = 1,180 / Hₐ: x̄ ≠ 1,180
(E) H₀: x̄ = 1,180 / Hₐ: x̄ < 1,180

KEY: B

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): The item tests LO VAR-7.C / EK VAR-7.C.1. The null hypothesis states the current (claimed) population mean: H₀: μ = 1,200. Eli's question is whether the mean is *less* now, so the alternative is Hₐ: μ < 1,200 — a one-sided left alternative. Options C, D, and E use the sample statistic x̄ in the hypotheses, which violates EK VAR-7.C.1 (hypotheses must reference population parameters, not sample statistics). Option A gets the direction wrong (> instead of <). The top distractors are C (correct parameter direction but wrong symbol) and A (correct symbol but wrong direction).

[MCQ] U7-L4-Q02

Researchers for a company that manufactures batteries want to test the hypothesis that the mean battery life of their new battery is greater than the known mean battery life of their older version. The researchers selected random samples of 32 of the new batteries, subjected the batteries to continuous use, and determined the mean and standard deviation of the battery lives in the sample.

Which of the following is an appropriate test for the researchers' hypothesis?

(A) A one-sample z-test for a population mean
(B) A one-sample t-test for a population mean
(C) A one-sample z-test for a population proportion
(D) A matched-pairs t-test for a mean difference
(E) A two-sample t-test for a difference between means

KEY: B

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): The item tests LO VAR-7.B / EK VAR-7.B.1. The population standard deviation for the new batteries is unknown — only the sample standard deviation was computed. Per EK VAR-7.B.1, when σ is unknown, the correct procedure is a one-sample t-test for a population mean. Option A (z-test) would require knowing σ. Options C (z-test for proportion) and E (two-sample t) address the wrong parameter or design. Option D (matched-pairs) applies when the same units are measured twice, which is not the case here.

[MCQ] U7-L4-Q03

To test the effectiveness of an exercise program in reducing high blood pressure, 15 participants had their blood pressures recorded before beginning the program and again after completing the program. The difference (after minus before) in blood pressure was recorded for each participant, and the sample mean difference x̄_D was calculated. A hypothesis test will be conducted to investigate whether there is convincing statistical evidence for a reduction in blood pressure for all who complete the program.

Which of the following is the correct set of hypotheses?

(A) H₀: x̄_D = 0 / Hₐ: x̄_D ≠ 0
(B) H₀: x̄_D = 0 / Hₐ: x̄_D < 0
(C) H₀: μ_D = 0 / Hₐ: μ_D ≠ 0
(D) H₀: μ_D = 0 / Hₐ: μ_D > 0
(E) H₀: μ_D = 0 / Hₐ: μ_D < 0

KEY: E

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): The item tests LO VAR-7.C / EK VAR-7.C.1 and EK VAR-7.C.2. The null hypothesis states no difference in population blood pressure (μ_D = 0). Because differences are defined as after minus before, a *reduction* means after < before, making the difference negative — so Hₐ: μ_D < 0. Options A and B incorrectly use the sample statistic x̄_D instead of the population parameter μ_D. Option C uses μ_D correctly but uses a two-sided alternative, which doesn't match "evidence for a reduction." Option D points in the wrong direction (> 0 would mean blood pressure increased).

[MCQ] U7-L4-Q05

A recent study reported the mean body mass index for adults in the United States was 26.8. A researcher believes the mean of marathon runners is less than 26.8. A random sample of 35 marathon runners had a mean of 22.7 with a standard deviation of 3.1. The researcher will conduct a one-sample t-test for a population mean.

Have the conditions for inference been met?

(A) Yes, all conditions have been met.
(B) No, because the sample was not selected using a random method.
(C) No, because marathon runners are not a representative sample of all adults in the United States.
(D) No, because the shape of the distribution of the sample is not known.
(E) No, because the sample size is not large enough to assume normality of the sampling distribution of the sample mean.

KEY: A

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): The item tests LO VAR-7.D / EK VAR-7.D.1. Option B is wrong because the problem states "random sample." Option C confuses the scope of inference (the test is about marathon runners, not all US adults — that is correct). Option D is wrong because n ≥ 30 removes the need to inspect the distribution shape. Option E is wrong for the same reason: 35 ≥ 30 meets the large-sample threshold.

[MCQ] U7-L4-Q08

A software company provides specialized resort reservation software that can be tailored to the needs of its customers. The company's 120 customers pay yearly subscription costs that can vary from customer to customer. The company knows that to be profitable, it needs each customer to be spending at least $23,000 per year, on average. The company selects a random sample of 33 customers and computes a mean of $27,871 and a standard deviation of $309.10. It performs a hypothesis test and computes a very small p-value. The software company concludes that the mean is greater than $23,000.

Was it appropriate for the software company to perform the hypothesis test and make the conclusion that was made?

(A) Yes, because the p-value is very small, so the company has sufficient evidence to reject the null hypothesis and conclude that its customers are spending more, on average, than $23,000 per year.
(B) Yes, because the sample size is large enough to perform the hypothesis test.
(C) No, because the sample is more than 10 percent of the population, so one of the conditions for conducting a hypothesis test has not been met.

(E) No, because the distribution of the sample data is skewed.

KEY: C

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): The item tests LO VAR-7.D / EK VAR-7.D.1. The 10% condition requires n ≤ 10% × N when sampling without replacement. Options A and B ignore this condition violation. Option D is wrong because n = 33 ≥ 30 (the large-sample shape condition is satisfied), though that doesn't matter since the 10% condition is the disqualifying issue. [MCQ] U7-L4-Q09

A six-week fitness program was designed to decrease the time it takes retired individuals to walk one mile. At the beginning of the program, 20 randomly selected retired individuals were invited to participate, and their times to walk a mile were recorded. After the six-week program, their times to walk a mile were again recorded. Most participants saw little to no improvement in their times to walk one mile; however, a few participants saw drastic improvements in their times to walk one mile. The program director would like to perform a hypothesis test to determine if the program reduces the mean time for retired individuals to walk a mile.

Which of the following statements is true?

(A) Because there are 40 observations in total, the sample size is large enough to assume that the sampling distribution of the sample mean differences is approximately normal.
(B) Because the walk times before and after the program are paired, a hypothesis test is not appropriate.
(C) The sampling distribution of sample means can be assumed to be approximately normal because the distribution of the sample data is not skewed.
(D) Because the sample size of 20 is less than 30 and the improvements in walk times in the sample data appear to be skewed, the distribution of sample means should not be assumed to be approximately normal.
(E) Because the program recruited only retired individuals, the distribution of sample means should not be assumed to be approximately normal.

KEY: D

WHY (for tutor's eyes; never reveal): The item tests LO VAR-7.D / EK VAR-7.D.1, specifically the shape condition for matched pairs (EK VAR-7.B.2). The relevant sample is the n = 20 *differences* (after minus before), not 40 individual observations. Since n = 20 < 30, the normality condition cannot be invoked via the large-sample rule — we must instead verify that the distribution of differences shows no strong skewness or outliers. The problem states that "a few participants saw drastic improvements," indicating that the distribution of differences is skewed. Therefore the normality condition is NOT met. Option A incorrectly counts 40 total observations as the sample size for this matched-pairs test. Option B is wrong — paired data calls for a matched-pairs t-test (EK VAR-7.B.2), which is a valid hypothesis test. Option C falsely claims the data are not skewed. Option E is irrelevant — the population restriction is the design choice, not a condition for normality.
