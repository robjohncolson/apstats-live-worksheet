[MCQ] U2-L3-Q01

The following data were collected from a random sample of people, who identified their favorite type of juice. The results are shown in the following two-way table. What proportion of the children identified orange as their favorite type of juice?

|  | Orange | Grape | Cranberry | Apple | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adults | 600 | 500 | 50 | 150 | 1,300 |
| Children | 400 | 100 | 50 | 150 | 700 |
| Total | 1,000 | 600 | 100 | 300 | 2,000 |

(A) 400/1,000
(B) 400/700
(C) 400/2,000
(D) 600/1,300
(E) 1,000/2,000

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests EK UNC-1.Q.2 — a conditional relative frequency is a cell count divided by the total for that row (or column), not by the table total. The question asks for the proportion *of children* (not of all people) who chose orange, so the denominator must be the children's row total of 700, giving 400/700. Choice A (400/1,000) uses the orange column total — a common distractor that confuses the conditioning direction. Choice C (400/2,000) uses the grand total, which would give a joint relative frequency, not a conditional one (EK UNC-1.Q.1 vs. UNC-1.Q.2). When a student picks A, ask: "What does the 1,000 represent in the table — is it the total number of children, or something else?"

[MCQ] U2-L3-Q02

The following data were collected from a random sample of people on their favorite types of leisure activities and their age. The results are shown in the two-way table below. What proportion of the people aged 7 to 18 years gave watching television as their favorite type of leisure activity?

|  | Reading | Exercise | Playing Video Games | Watching Television | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 6 years | 10 | 50 | 100 | 140 | 300 |
| 7 to 12 years | 200 | 300 | 200 | 200 | 900 |
| 13 to 18 years | 600 | 500 | 100 | 100 | 1,300 |
| 19 years or greater | 500 | 250 | 50 | 200 | 1,000 |
| Total | 1,310 | 1,100 | 450 | 640 | 3,500 |

(A) 300/2,200
(B) 200/900
(C) 100/1,300
(D) 640/3,500
(E) 300/640

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests EK UNC-1.Q.2 at a slightly elevated difficulty because the condition spans two age rows ("7 to 18 years"), requiring the student to combine rows before conditioning. The total for ages 7 to 18 is 900 + 1,300 = 2,200, and the combined television count is 200 + 100 = 300, giving 300/2,200. Choice B (200/900) conditions only on "7 to 12 years" — the student forgot to include the 13–18 group. Choice C (100/1,300) conditions only on "13 to 18 years." Choice E (300/640) flips the conditioning — it gives the proportion of TV watchers who are aged 7–18, not the proportion of 7–18 year-olds who watch TV. If a student picks B or C, ask: "The question says '7 to 18 years' — does that include just one row, or do we need to combine?"

[MCQ] U2-L3-Q04

A penalty kick in soccer involves two players from different teams, the shooter and the goalie. During the penalty kick the shooter will try to score a goal by kicking a soccer ball to the left or right of the goal area. To prevent the shooter from scoring a goal, the goalie will move to the left or right of the goal area. The following table summarizes the directions taken by the shooter and the goalie for 372 penalty kicks. Which of the following indicates an association between the shooter's choice of direction and the goalie's choice of direction?

|  |  | Shooter |  |  |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  | Kick Left | Kick Right | Total |
| Goalie | Move Left | 80 | 132 | 212 |
|  | Move Right | 85 | 75 | 160 |
|  | Total | 165 | 207 | 372 |

(A) The marginal relative frequencies for the shooter and the goalie are equal.
(B) The marginal relative frequencies for the shooter and the goalie are not equal.
(C) The row totals are not equal.
(D) For the goalie, the relative frequency of a direction is equal to the relative frequency conditioned on the shooter's direction.
(E) For the goalie, the relative frequency of a direction is not equal to the relative frequency conditioned on the shooter's direction.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests EK UNC-1.R.1 at a conceptual level — the definition of association via conditional relative frequencies. Association exists when the conditional distribution of one variable differs depending on the value of the other. The overall (marginal) relative frequency of the goalie moving left is 212/372 ≈ 0.57. But conditioned on the shooter kicking left, it is 80/165 ≈ 0.48; conditioned on the shooter kicking right, it is 132/207 ≈ 0.64. These differ from the marginal, so there is evidence of association — exactly what choice E states. Choices A–C involve marginal relative frequencies or row totals, which are irrelevant to determining association (EK UNC-1.Q.1). Choice D states the opposite of what signals association. If a student picks B, ask: "Does unequal marginal relative frequencies between two variables tell us whether knowing one variable's value helps predict the other's? Think about what conditional relative frequency means (EK UNC-1.Q.2)."

[MCQ] U2-L3-Q05

The following table shows data that were collected from a random sample of people, who indicated their age and their favorite sporting event to watch on television. Based on the results above, what proportion of the randomly sampled people are over age 12 years?

|  | Football | Basketball | Baseball | Soccer | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 6 years | 10 | 50 | 100 | 140 | 300 |
| 7 to 12 years | 200 | 300 | 200 | 200 | 900 |
| 13 to 18 years | 600 | 500 | 100 | 100 | 1,300 |
| 19 years and older | 500 | 250 | 50 | 200 | 1,000 |
| Total | 1,310 | 1,100 | 450 | 640 | 3,500 |

(A) 900/3,500
(B) 1,300/3,500
(C) 1,200/3,500
(D) 2,300/3,500
(E) 1,000/3,500

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests EK UNC-1.Q.1 — a marginal relative frequency is a row (or column) total divided by the table total. "Over age 12" means ages 13–18 AND 19 and older: 1,300 + 1,000 = 2,300. The marginal relative frequency is 2,300/3,500. Choice B (1,300/3,500) includes only the 13–18 row — the student missed "19 and older." Choice E (1,000/3,500) includes only the 19-and-older row. Choice A (900/3,500) gives the proportion aged 7–12, not over 12. The key phrase is "over age 12," which excludes the 0–6 and 7–12 rows. Ask a student who picks B: "Does '13 to 18 years' cover everyone who is over 12, or is there another age group also over 12?"

[MCQ] U2-L3-Q06

A split ticket is a voting pattern in which a voter casts votes for candidates from more than one political party. In a recent study, 1,000 men and women were asked whether they voted a split ticket in the last election. The totals are shown in the following table. What value of a would indicate no association between gender and voting pattern for the people in the sample?

|  | Men | Women | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yes, split ticket | a | b | 600 |
| No, did not split ticket | c | d | 400 |
| Total | 800 | 200 | 1,000 |

(A) 300
(B) 400
(C) 480
(D) 500
(E) 800

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): This item tests EK UNC-1.R.1 at an algebraic/conceptual level — no association exists when the conditional relative frequency equals the marginal relative frequency. The marginal proportion of men in the sample is 800/1,000 = 0.80. For no association, the proportion of split-ticket voters who are men (a/600) must also equal 0.80, so a = 0.80 × 600 = 480. Equivalently, the proportion of men who voted split ticket (a/800) should equal the overall proportion who voted split ticket (600/1,000 = 0.60), giving a = 0.60 × 800 = 480 — both approaches arrive at the same answer, confirming internal consistency. Choice A (300) = 600/2 = splitting the split-ticket row in half regardless of group sizes. Choice B (400) = total non-split-ticket voters. Choice E (800) = all men. If a student is stuck, ask: "If gender and voting pattern are not associated, what should be true about the proportion of men among split-ticket voters compared to the proportion of men overall in the sample? (Think about EK UNC-1.R.1.)"
