[MCQ] U1-L3-Q01

The following frequency table shows the responses from a group of college
students who were asked to choose their favorite flavor of ice cream.
Which of the following statements is not supported by the table?

| Flavor | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Vanilla | 100 |
| Chocolate | 65 |
| Strawberry | 35 |
| Mint chip | 45 |
| Coffee | 30 |
| Butter pecan | 25 |

(A) The number of student responses is 300.
(B) One-third of the students chose vanilla.
(C) One-third of the students chose chocolate or strawberry.
(D) One-fourth of the students chose mint chip or coffee.
(E) One-half of the students chose vanilla or chocolate.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests UNC-1.B / EK UNC-1.B.2 — using counts from a frequency table to evaluate claims in context. The total is 100+65+35+45+30+25 = 300. Choices A–D are all accurate: A: total = 300; B: 100/300 = 1/3; C: (65+35)/300 = 100/300 = 1/3; D: (45+30)/300 = 75/300 = 1/4. Choice E claims one-half chose vanilla or chocolate, but (100+65)/300 = 165/300 = 0.55, not 0.50 — so E is NOT supported and is the correct answer. The most common error is miscounting the total or accepting 55% as "close enough" to half; push the student to compute the exact relative frequency (EK UNC-1.A.1, UNC-1.B.1).

[MCQ] U1-L3-Q02

The following relative frequency table shows the political party affiliation
for a sample of 500 people in a certain town. Which of the following
statements is supported by the table?

| Political Party | Relative Frequency |
|---|---|
| Democrat | 0.35 |
| Republican | 0.30 |
| Independent | 0.20 |
| Green Party | 0.11 |
| Libertarian | 0.03 |
| Other | 0.01 |

(A) The number of people affiliated with the Republicans is 30.
(B) The number of people affiliated with the Independents is 100.
(C) Less than half of the people are affiliated with the Democrats or the Republicans.
(D) At least 200 people are affiliated with the Democrats.
(E) At least 80 people are affiliated with the Green Party or the Libertarians.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests UNC-1.B / EK UNC-1.B.1 and EK UNC-1.B.2 — translating between relative frequencies (proportions) and counts in context, using n = 500. Choice B is correct: 0.20 × 500 = 100 Independents. All other choices contain arithmetic errors or direction errors: A gives 0.30 × 500 = 150, not 30 (confuses relative frequency with count); C is wrong because Dem + Rep = 0.65 > 0.50; D gives 0.35 × 500 = 175, which is fewer than 200; E gives (0.11+0.03) × 500 = 70, which is fewer than 80. The key diagnostic: students who pick A confuse the relative frequency value itself (0.30) with the count — a direct UNC-1.B.1 gap. Prompt them: "A relative frequency is a proportion. How do you get from a proportion to a count when you know the sample size?"

[MCQ] U1-L3-Q03

The following table summarizes the number of pies sold at a booth one day
at a local farmers market. Which of the following statements is supported
by the table?

| Type of Pie | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Apple | 18 |
| Blueberry | 14 |
| Cherry | 16 |
| Key Lime | 12 |
| Peach | 12 |
| Pumpkin | 18 |

(A) More cherry pies were sold than any other type of pie.
(B) Twice as many apple pies as key lime pies were sold.
(C) More than half the pies sold were apple.
(D) Fewer than 50 pies were sold at the booth that day.
(E) The combined percentage of key lime pies sold and pumpkin pies sold was less than 50%.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests UNC-1.B / EK UNC-1.B.2 — evaluating claims against a frequency table by computing relative frequencies in context. Total pies = 18+14+16+12+12+18 = 90. Choice E is correct: Key Lime + Pumpkin = 12+18 = 30; 30/90 ≈ 33.3%, which is less than 50%. All other choices fail: A is false because Apple and Pumpkin (both 18) tie Cherry (16) as the top sellers; B is false because 2×12 = 24 ≠ 18 (Apple); C is false because 18/90 ≈ 20%; D is false because total = 90, not fewer than 50. A common distractor is B — students may misread "twice as many A as B" and check 18/12 ≈ 1.5 without verifying the exact 2× relationship. Use EK UNC-1.A.1 to anchor: frequency tables give exact counts; check the arithmetic precisely.
