| LEARNING OBJECTIVE | ESSENTIAL KNOWLEDGE |
| :--- | :--- |
| **VAR-2.A** Compare a data distribution to the normal distribution model. [Skill 2.D] | **VAR-2.A.1** A parameter is a numerical summary of a population. **VAR-2.A.2** Some sets of data may be described as approximately normally distributed. A normal curve is mound-shaped and symmetric. The parameters of a normal distribution are the population mean, μ, and the population standard deviation, σ. **VAR-2.A.3** For a normal distribution, approximately 68% of the observations are within 1 standard deviation of the mean, approximately 95% of observations are within 2 standard deviations of the mean, and approximately 99.7% of observations are within 3 standard deviations of the mean. | **VAR-2.B** Determine proportions and percentiles from a normal distribution. [Skill 3.A] | **VAR-2.B.1** A standardized score for a particular data value is calculated as (data value – mean)/(standard deviation), and measures the number of standard deviations a data value falls above or below the mean. **VAR-2.B.2** One example of a standardized score is a z-score, calculated as z = (x − μ)/σ. A z-score measures how many standard deviations a data value is from the mean. **VAR-2.B.3** Technology, such as a calculator, a standard normal table, or computer-generated output, can be used to find the proportion of data values located on a given interval of a normally distributed random variable. **VAR-2.B.4** Given the area of a region under the graph of the normal distribution curve, it is possible to use technology to estimate parameters for some populations. |

**[MCQ] U1-L10-Q01**

The distribution of the number of transactions per day at a certain automated teller machine (ATM) is approximately normal with a mean of 80 transactions and a standard deviation of 10 transactions. Which of the following represents the parameters of the distribution?

(A) x̄ = 80; s = 10
(B) x̄ = 80; s² = 10
(C) x̄ = 80; σ = 10
(D) μ = 80; σ = 10
(E) μ = 80; s = 10

KEY: D

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests VAR-2.A.1 (a parameter is a numerical summary of a *population*) and VAR-2.A.2 (the parameters of a normal distribution are μ and σ). The values 80 and 10 are stated as known characteristics of the distribution — they are population parameters, not sample statistics. Therefore the correct notation is μ (population mean) and σ (population standard deviation). The top distractors are C (mixes x̄ with σ — sample mean symbol with population SD symbol) and E (uses μ correctly but pairs it with s, the sample SD symbol). Ask the student: "Are these values describing a sample or the entire distribution? What does the framework say parameters are?"

**[MCQ] U1-L10-Q02**

At a small coffee shop, the distribution of the number of seconds it takes for a cashier to process an order is approximately normal with mean 276 seconds and standard deviation 38 seconds. Which of the following is closest to the proportion of orders that are processed in less than 240 seconds?

(A) 0.17
(B) 0.25
(C) 0.36
(D) 0.83
(E) 0.95

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests VAR-2.B.2 (z-score) and VAR-2.B.3 (using technology/table to find a proportion). The correct procedure is to compute z = (240 − 276)/38 = −0.95, then find the area to the left of z = −0.95 under the standard normal curve, which is approximately 0.17. The top distractor is D (0.83) — the area to the *right* of the same z-score, reflecting a direction error. Distractor E (0.95) tempts students who treat the z-value itself as the answer. Ask the student: "Which direction does 'less than' point on the normal curve? How do you use EK VAR-2.B.2 to set up the z-score here?"

**[MCQ] U1-L10-Q03**

A researcher is studying a group of field mice. The distribution of the weight of field mice is approximately normal with mean 25 grams and standard deviation 4 grams. Which of the following is closest to the proportion of field mice with a weight greater than 33 grams?

(A) 0.023
(B) 0.046
(C) 0.954
(D) 0.977
(E) 1.000

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests VAR-2.B.2 (z-score) and VAR-2.B.3 (normal proportion via technology or table). The z-score for 33 grams is z = (33 − 25)/4 = 2. The area to the *right* of z = 2 under the standard normal curve is approximately 0.023 — consistent with the empirical rule (VAR-2.A.3: ~95% of observations within 2 SDs means ~5% outside, and by symmetry ~2.5% in each tail). Top distractor is D (0.977) — the area to the *left* of z = 2, reflecting a direction error. Distractor B (0.046) is the full two-tail area beyond ±2 SDs, not just the right tail. Ask the student: "What does z = 2 tell you about where 33 grams sits relative to the mean? Does 'greater than' correspond to the left or right tail?"

**[MCQ] U1-L10-Q05**

Shalise competed in a jigsaw puzzle competition where participants are timed on how long they take to complete puzzles of various sizes. Shalise completed a small puzzle in 75 minutes and a large jigsaw puzzle in 140 minutes. For all participants, the distribution of completion time for the small puzzle was approximately normal with mean 60 minutes and standard deviation 15 minutes. The distribution of completion time for the large puzzle was approximately normal with mean 180 minutes and standard deviation 40 minutes.

Approximately what percent of the participants had finishing times greater than Shalise's for each puzzle?

(A) 16% on the small puzzle and 16% on the large puzzle
(B) 16% on the small puzzle and 84% on the large puzzle
(C) 32% on the small puzzle and 68% on the large puzzle
(D) 84% on the small puzzle and 84% on the large puzzle
(E) 84% on the small puzzle and 16% on the large puzzle

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests VAR-2.A.3 (empirical rule) and VAR-2.C.1 (using relative position to compare). For the small puzzle: Shalise's time of 75 minutes is exactly 1 SD above the mean of 60 (z = +1). By the empirical rule, 68% of times fall within 1 SD of the mean; by symmetry, 16% are above 75 minutes — so 16% of participants were slower than Shalise on the small puzzle. For the large puzzle: 140 minutes is exactly 1 SD *below* the mean of 180 (z = −1). By symmetry, 84% of participants had times greater than 140 minutes — Shalise beat most participants on the large puzzle. The critical insight is that the two puzzles require applying the empirical rule in opposite tail directions. Distractor A assumes both are upper tails (both 16%). Distractor E reverses the two results. Ask the student: "For each puzzle, figure out where Shalise's time sits relative to the mean in standard-deviation units — which side of the mean is she on for each one?"

**[MCQ] U1-L10-Q07**

(A) 68%
(B) 32%
(C) 16%
(D) 5%

By the empirical rule, approximately 68% of values fall within 1 SD of the mean, leaving 32% outside. Because the normal distribution is symmetric, half of that 32% — i.e., 16% — lies above 1,400. Distractor A (68%) is the within-1-SD proportion, not the upper tail. Distractor B (32%) is the total outside-1-SD proportion, not just the upper tail. Distractor E (2.5%) would apply if 1,400 were 2 SDs above the mean. Ask the student: "How many standard deviations above the mean is 1,400? **[MCQ] U1-L10-Q08**

The distribution of lengths of salmon from a certain river is approximately normal with standard deviation 3.5 inches. If 10 percent of salmon are longer than 30 inches, which of the following is closest to the mean of the distribution?

(A) 26 inches
(B) 28 inches
(C) 30 inches
(D) 33 inches
(E) 34 inches

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal verbatim): This item tests VAR-2.B.4 (using technology/invNorm to estimate a parameter given an area) and VAR-2.B.2 (z-score). The setup reverses the usual direction: rather than finding a proportion from a known mean, you are given a proportion and a boundary, and must find the mean. The z-score corresponding to the 90th percentile (top 10% to the right) is approximately z ≈ 1.28. Rearranging the z-score formula: μ = x − z·σ = 30 − 1.28 × 3.5 ≈ 30 − 4.48 ≈ 25.5, which is closest to 26 inches (choice A). Common errors: students who choose C (30) are treating the boundary as the mean — they misread the 10% condition. Students who choose D or E invert the direction, adding z·σ instead of subtracting (confusing upper tail with lower tail). Distractor B (28) may arise from using an incorrect z-value or arithmetic error. Ask the student: "If 10% of salmon are *longer* than 30 inches, is 30 inches above or below the mean? Which EK tells you how to work backward from a known area to find the parameter?"
