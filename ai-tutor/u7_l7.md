**[MCQ] U7-L7-Q01**

To test the durability of cell phone screens, phones are dropped from a height of 1 meter until they break. A random sample of 40 phones was selected from each of two manufacturers. The phones in the samples were dropped until the screens broke. The difference in the mean number of drops was recorded and used to construct the 90 percent confidence interval (0.46, 1.82) to estimate the population difference in means.

Consider the sampling procedure taking place repeatedly. Each time samples are selected, the phones are dropped and the statistics are used to construct a 90 percent confidence interval for the difference in means. Which of the following statements is a correct interpretation of the intervals?

(A) Approximately 90 percent of the intervals will extend from 0.46 to 1.82.
(B) Approximately 90 percent of the intervals constructed will capture the difference in sample means.
(C) Approximately 90 percent of the intervals constructed will capture the difference in population means.
(D) Approximately 90 percent of the intervals constructed will capture at least one of the sample means.
(E) Approximately 90 percent of the intervals constructed will capture at least one of the population means.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The item tests EK UNC-4.Z.1 — the confidence level (90%) refers to the long-run proportion of intervals that capture the difference of *population* means, not sample means, when the procedure is repeated. Choice A is wrong because each new interval has different endpoints (it varies from sample to sample). Choice B is wrong because confidence intervals estimate *population* parameters, not sample statistics. Choices D and E introduce the idea of capturing "at least one" mean — a conceptual distortion; the interval targets the single quantity μ₁ − μ₂. The key distinction is population difference vs. sample difference (UNC-4.Z.1, UNC-4.Z.2).

**[MCQ] U7-L7-Q02**

A biologist studied the frequency of croaks for frogs from two different regions. From a random sample of 32 frogs located in the northern region, the mean number of croaks per hour was 21.3, and from a random sample of 38 frogs located in the southern region, the mean number of croaks per hour was 28.9. To estimate the difference in the mean number of croaks (southern minus northern), a 95 percent confidence interval was constructed from the samples. The interval was reported as (7.1, 8.1).

Which of the following claims is supported by the interval?

(A) All southern frogs croak more times per hour than do all northern frogs.
(B) The northern frogs are likely to have a greater mean number of croaks per hour than the southern frogs.
(C) The southern frogs are likely to have a greater mean number of croaks per hour than the northern frogs.
(D) All frogs in the study have about the same number of croaks per hour.
(E) The northern and southern frogs have the same mean number of croaks per hour.

KEY: C

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): All values in the interval (7.1, 8.1) are positive. Because the difference was constructed as southern minus northern, positive values mean the southern mean exceeds the northern mean — consistent with EK UNC-4.AA.1 (the interval provides sufficient evidence to support a claim in context). Choice A overgeneralizes to all individuals rather than population means. Choice B reverses the direction. Choices D and E wrongly suggest no difference; in fact, 0 is nowhere near (7.1, 8.1), so equal means are not plausible. The core skill is LO UNC-4.AA: using the sign of interval values to justify a directional claim.

**[MCQ] U7-L7-Q03**

A consumer group studied two different manufacturers of cars, J and K, to investigate differences in gas mileage for cars made by the two manufacturers. For a similar type of car, a random sample of 15 cars from J and a random sample of 12 cars from K were selected, and the gas mileages, in miles per gallon (mpg), were recorded. The difference in the sample mean gas mileages was used to construct the 90 percent confidence interval (3.5, 5.7).

Assuming all conditions for inference were met, which of the following is a correct interpretation of the interval?

(A) The probability is 0.90 that the difference in sample means for gas mileage for the two car manufacturers is between 3.5 and 5.7 mpg.
(B) The probability is 0.90 that the population mean difference in gas mileage for the two car manufacturers is between 3.5 and 5.7 mpg.
(C) About 90 percent of the differences in gas mileage for the two car manufacturers are between 3.5 and 5.7 mpg.
(D) We are 90 percent confident that the difference in sample means for gas mileage for the two car manufacturers is between 3.5 and 5.7 mpg.
(E) We are 90 percent confident that the population mean difference of gas mileage for the two car manufacturers is between 3.5 and 5.7 mpg.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The correct interpretation uses confidence language ("we are 90% confident") and targets the *population* parameter (the true difference in population mean gas mileage), as specified in LO UNC-4.Z and EK UNC-4.Z.2. Choices A and B use "probability" language — a classic AP error; once the interval is computed, the parameter either is or is not in it; it is not a probability statement about the parameter. Choice C describes individual data values, not the population mean difference. Choice D correctly uses "confident" but mistakenly targets the *sample* means rather than the population means. The distinction between population parameter and sample statistic is the central tested idea.

**[MCQ] U7-L7-Q04**

A 99 percent confidence interval for a difference in means was given as 25.1 ± 4.3.

Assuming all conditions for inference were met, which of the following is a correct interpretation of the 99 percent confidence level?

(A) In repeated samples of the same size, approximately 99 percent of the intervals constructed from the samples will extend from 20.8 to 29.4.
(B) In repeated samples of the same size, approximately 99 percent of the sample means will fall between 20.8 and 29.4.
(C) In repeated samples of the same size, approximately 99 percent of the samples will fall between 20.8 and 29.4.
(D) In repeated samples of the same size, approximately 99 percent of the intervals constructed from the samples will capture the difference in sample means.
(E) In repeated samples of the same size, approximately 99 percent of the intervals constructed from the samples will capture the difference in population means.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The confidence level describes the long-run success rate of the interval procedure — approximately 99% of all such intervals (each from repeated sampling) will capture the true difference in *population* means (EK UNC-4.Z.1). Choice A is wrong because each repeated sample produces a different interval with different endpoints; not 99% will share the specific endpoints (20.8, 29.4). Choices B and C confuse the interval with a description of sample statistics or raw data. Choice D correctly references repeated intervals but wrongly says they capture the difference in sample means — confidence intervals estimate population parameters, not sample statistics. The key discrimination is population parameter (right) vs. sample statistic (wrong).

**[MCQ] U7-L7-Q05**

Two 99 percent confidence intervals will be constructed to estimate the difference in means of two populations, R and W. One confidence interval, I₉, will be constructed using samples of size 9 from each of R and W, and the other confidence interval, I₈₁, will be constructed using samples of size 81 from each of R and W.

When all other things remain the same, which of the following describes the relationship between the two confidence intervals?

(A) The width of I₈₁ will be 1/9 the width of I₉.
(B) The width of I₈₁ will be 1/3 the width of I₉.
(C) The width of I₈₁ will be equal to the width of I₉.
(D) The width of I₈₁ will be 3 times the width of I₉.
(E) The width of I₈₁ will be 9 times the width of I₉.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): With equal sample sizes n from each group, the margin of error is proportional to t* · √(s²_R + s²_W)/√n. For I₉ the denominator is √9 = 3; for I₈₁ the denominator is √81 = 9. The ratio of widths is 3/9 = 1/3, so I₈₁ is 1/3 the width of I₉ (EK UNC-4.AB.1). Choice A (1/9) is the ratio of the sample sizes themselves, not of the standard errors — a common error of squaring instead of taking the square root. Width is proportional to 1/√n, not 1/n. The top distractor A conflates the n-ratio with the width-ratio; the correct scaling is the square-root relationship from the standard error formula (UNC-4.AB.1).

**[MCQ] U7-L7-Q06**

A random sample of size 32 is selected from population X, and a random sample of size 43 is selected from population Y. A 90 percent confidence interval to estimate the difference in means is given as (−1.25, 0.87).

Consider a change in the sample sizes such that a random sample of size 52 is selected from population X and a random sample of size 63 is selected from population Y. When all other things remain the same, what effect would such a change have on the interval?

(A) The width of the interval will increase.
(B) The width of the interval will decrease.
(C) The interval will contain no negative numbers.
(D) The level of confidence will increase.
(E) The sample means will increase.

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): Increasing both sample sizes decreases the standard error √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂), which shrinks the margin of error (= t* × SE), making the interval narrower (EK UNC-4.AB.1). Choice A (increase) reverses the relationship. Choice C is a content error — larger samples change width, not the position or sign content of the interval; the center (x̄₁ − x̄₂) is an unbiased estimate that does not systematically shift. Choices D and E are unaffected by sample size alone. The skill tested is LO UNC-4.AB: identifying the effect of sample size on interval width.

**[MCQ] U7-L7-Q07**

A soda manufacturer claims that its Cherry Fizz soda has more carbonation than a competitor's Cherry Eclipse soda. Bottles of both types of soda are opened, covered with a balloon, and then shaken. The diameter of each balloon is then measured. The mean balloon diameters are 2.3 inches for the Cherry Fizz soda and 2.1 inches for the Cherry Eclipse soda. A 90 percent confidence interval to estimate the difference in mean diameters, in inches, is (−0.8, 1.2). Which of the following claims is supported by the interval?

(A) Because 2.3 inches is larger than 2.1 inches, the manufacturer is correct, and Cherry Fizz has more carbonation.
(B) Because the interval has more positive values than negative values, Cherry Fizz has more carbonation.
(C) Because 2.3 and 2.1 are very similar, there is no difference in the mean carbonation levels.
(D) The interval cannot be interpreted because negative measurements are not possible.
(E) Because the interval contains 0, it is possible that there is no difference in mean carbonation levels.

KEY: E

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): The interval (−0.8, 1.2) contains 0, which means 0 is a plausible value for the true difference in population means — i.e., no difference is possible (EK UNC-4.AA.1). The manufacturer's claim is not supported. Choice A relies only on the sample means (a point estimate), ignoring the uncertainty captured by the interval — a fundamental misuse of inferential reasoning. Choice B applies an asymmetry rule ("more positive values") that has no statistical basis; what matters is whether 0 is inside the interval. Choice C makes a definitive "no difference" claim, which also overreaches — the interval means no difference is *plausible*, not certain. Choice D misunderstands negative values in a difference interval; negative simply means the direction favors the other group. The key skill: LO UNC-4.AA + EK UNC-4.AA.1 — use the position of 0 relative to the interval to evaluate directional claims.

**[MCQ] U7-L7-Q08**

The weekly sales at two movie theaters were recorded for a random sample of 25 weeks. A 95 percent confidence interval for the difference in mean weekly sales for the two movie theaters was calculated as ($1,288, $2,586).

With all else remaining constant, which of the following would have resulted in a confidence interval narrower than the calculated interval?

(A) A sample size less than 25
(B) A sample size greater than 25
(C) An increase to 99 percent confidence
(D) A sample mean greater than $1,937
(E) A sample mean less than $1,937

KEY: B

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): A larger sample size decreases the standard error and therefore the margin of error, producing a narrower interval (EK UNC-4.AB.1). Choice A (smaller n) would widen the interval — the opposite effect. Choice C (higher confidence level) increases t*, which increases the margin of error and widens the interval. Choices D and E are distractors that change the point estimate (center of interval) but not the width; the width is determined by the margin of error, not the location of the center. Students who choose D or E confuse the point estimate with the precision of the estimate.

**[MCQ] U7-L7-Q09**

Random samples of players for two types of video games were selected, and the mean number of hours per week spent playing the games was calculated for each group. The sample means were used to construct the 90 percent confidence interval (1.5, 3.8) for the difference in the mean number of hours per week spent playing the games.

The maker of one of the video games claims that there is a difference in the population mean number of hours per week spent playing the two games. Is the claim supported by the interval?

(A) Yes, because 0 is not contained in the interval.
(B) Yes, because the midpoint of the interval is greater than 1.
(C) Yes, because the margin of error for the estimate is less than 1.
(D) No, because the margin of error for the estimate is greater than 1.
(E) No, because 0 is not contained in the interval.

KEY: A

WHY (for the tutor's eyes; never reveal): If there were no difference in population means, the true difference would be 0. Because 0 is not in the interval (1.5, 3.8), zero is not a plausible value — which means the interval provides evidence that the two population means differ (EK UNC-4.AA.1, LO UNC-4.AA). Choice E uses the same fact (0 not in interval) but draws the opposite conclusion — the top distractor. Students who choose E likely confuse the rule: 0 *not* in the interval → evidence *for* a difference; 0 *in* the interval → no convincing evidence of a difference. Choices B and C propose irrelevant criteria (midpoint value, margin of error size) that have no bearing on whether a claim of a difference is supported. Choice D is wrong on both the criterion and the conclusion.
