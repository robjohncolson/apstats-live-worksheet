AP Statistics Unit 8 Inference for Categorical Data

Topic 8.2: Setting Up a Chi-Square Goodness of Fit Test

Chi-Square Distributions, Hypotheses, and Conditions for a Goodness-of-Fit Test
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Explain what the chi-square statistic measures for a distribution of categorical counts.
  • Describe how degrees of freedom affect the shape of chi-square distributions.
  • State the null hypothesis for a chi-square goodness-of-fit test using specified proportions.
  • State the alternative hypothesis for a chi-square goodness-of-fit test in words.
  • Identify when a chi-square goodness-of-fit test is appropriate and verify its conditions.
Essential Knowledge:
  • The chi-square statistic measures the distance between observed and expected counts relative to expected counts.
  • Expected counts come from the null model and do not have to be equal across categories.
  • For a single categorical variable, degrees of freedom equal the number of categories minus one.
  • Chi-square distributions are right-skewed and can take only nonnegative values.
  • As degrees of freedom increase, the skewness of the chi-square distribution becomes less pronounced.
  • The null hypothesis states that category proportions are equal to specified values, while the alternative says that at least one proportion is different.
  • A chi-square goodness-of-fit test is used for one sample and one categorical variable when we want to compare observed counts to a hypothesized distribution.
  • The main conditions are a random sample or randomized experiment, the 10% condition when sampling without replacement, and all expected counts greater than 5.

Key Vocabulary

Chi-Square StatisticA measure of total discrepancy between observed and expected counts relative to expected counts.
ContributionAn individual category's value of (Observed minus Expected) squared divided by Expected.
Chi-Square DistributionA right-skewed distribution of possible chi-square values for a given number of degrees of freedom.
Degrees of FreedomFor one categorical variable, the number of categories minus one.
Goodness-of-Fit TestA significance test that compares observed counts for one categorical variable to a hypothesized distribution.
Null HypothesisA statement that the population proportions are equal to specified values.
Alternative HypothesisA statement that at least one population proportion is not as specified in the null hypothesis.
Specified ProportionsThe hypothesized category probabilities used to find expected counts.
10% ConditionWhen sampling without replacement, the sample size should be no more than 10% of the population.
Large Counts ConditionAll expected counts must be greater than 5 for the chi-square goodness-of-fit test.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. The chi-square statistic measures total relative discrepancy between observed and expected counts.
  2. For one categorical variable, df = number of categories − 1, and chi-square values are always nonnegative.
  3. Chi-square distributions are more right-skewed with fewer degrees of freedom and become less skewed as degrees of freedom increase.
  4. The null hypothesis gives specified proportions, and the alternative says that at least one proportion is not as specified.
  5. A chi-square goodness-of-fit test requires one sample, one categorical variable, a random process, the 10% condition when needed, and all expected counts greater than 5.
