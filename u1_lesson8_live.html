Topic 1.8: Topic 1.8

Graphical Representations of Summary Statistics
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Use the five-number summary to represent quantitative data graphically.
  • Identify and interpret the parts of a box plot, including the box, median line, whiskers, and outlier symbols.
  • Describe what a box plot shows about quartiles, the middle 50% of the data, and possible outliers.
  • Explain how the shape of a distribution affects the relationship between the mean and the median.
Essential Knowledge:
  • The five-number summary is the minimum, Q1, median, Q3, and maximum.
  • A box plot is a graphical representation of the five-number summary for quantitative data.
  • The box shows the middle 50% of the data, with a line at the median and ends at the quartiles.
  • Whiskers extend to the most extreme values that are not outliers, and outliers are shown separately.
  • Box plots quickly show quartiles and possible outliers, but they hide individual values and some shape details.
  • In a skewed-right distribution the mean is usually greater than the median, in a skewed-left distribution the mean is usually less than the median, and in a symmetric distribution they are about equal.

Key Vocabulary

Five-Number SummaryThe minimum, Q1, median, Q3, and maximum taken together.
Box PlotA graph that represents the five-number summary and possible outliers.
QuartileOne of the four equal sections of an ordered data set.
Q1The first quartile, or the median of the lower half of the data.
Q3The third quartile, or the median of the upper half of the data.
MedianThe middle value in an ordered distribution.
WhiskerA line from the box to the most extreme value that is not an outlier.
OutlierA value plotted beyond the whiskers because it is unusually far from the rest of the data.
SymmetricA distribution whose left and right sides are approximately balanced.
Skewed RightA distribution with a longer tail on the right side.
Skewed LeftA distribution with a longer tail on the left side.
NonresistantA statistic, such as the mean, that can be pulled by outliers.
