AP Statistics Unit 2 Exploring Two-Variable Data

Topic 2.2: Required Course Content

Two-Way Tables, Side-by-Side Bar Graphs, Segmented Bar Graphs, and Mosaic Plots
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Use a two-way table to summarize two categorical variables.
  • Calculate relative frequencies within groups so distributions can be compared fairly.
  • Construct side-by-side bar graphs, segmented bar graphs, and mosaic plots from two-way table data.
  • Use graphical displays to compare distributions of one categorical variable across categories of another categorical variable.
  • Decide whether two categorical variables are associated by comparing distributions across groups.
  • Recognize that a mosaic plot adds information about group size that a segmented bar graph does not show.
Essential Knowledge:
  • A two-way table, also called a contingency table, summarizes two categorical variables.
  • The cells of a two-way table can contain frequency counts or relative frequencies.
  • Side-by-side bar graphs, segmented bar graphs, and mosaic plots compare one categorical variable across categories of another.
  • To compare distributions within groups, convert counts to percents by dividing by the group total.
  • Graphical displays of two categorical variables can be used to compare distributions and determine whether variables are associated.
  • A joint relative frequency is a cell frequency divided by the total for the entire table.
  • If the distributions are not the same for the groups being compared, the variables are associated.

Key Vocabulary

Two-Way TableA table that summarizes the relationship between two categorical variables.
Contingency TableAnother name for a two-way table.
Relative FrequencyA proportion or percent found by dividing a count by an appropriate total.
Conditional Relative FrequencyA relative frequency found within a single row or column category.
Joint Relative FrequencyA cell count divided by the total number of observations in the entire table.
DistributionHow the values of a categorical variable are divided among its categories.
Side-by-Side Bar GraphA display with separate bars for each category within each group.
Segmented Bar GraphA display made by stacking the bars for a group so the total height is 100%.
Mosaic PlotA segmented bar graph whose bar widths represent group sizes.
AssociationA relationship in which knowing one categorical variable helps predict another.
