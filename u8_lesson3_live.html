Topic 8.3: Carrying Out a Chi-Square Test for Goodness of Fit

Test Statistics, P-Values, and Conclusions for a Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Calculate the chi-square test statistic from observed and expected counts for a goodness-of-fit test.
  • Use technology or a chi-square table to find or estimate the p-value for a chi-square goodness-of-fit test.
  • Interpret the p-value in context for a chi-square goodness-of-fit test.
  • Compare the p-value to a significance level and state the correct decision and conclusion.
  • Carry out a complete chi-square goodness-of-fit test and use contributions to identify the largest discrepancy.
Essential Knowledge:
  • Expected counts come from multiplying the null-model proportions by the sample size.
  • Each contribution is calculated as (Observed − Expected)2 / Expected.
  • The chi-square statistic is the sum of the category contributions.
  • Degrees of freedom are based on the number of categories minus one, not the sample size.
  • Chi-square goodness-of-fit tests use the upper-tail probability of a chi-square distribution.
  • A chi-square statistic of 0 means a perfect fit, and larger values indicate a worse fit to the null model.
  • The p-value is interpreted as the probability of getting a result as extreme as the one in the study, or more extreme, assuming the null hypothesis is true.
  • If the p-value is less than or equal to alpha, reject the null hypothesis; if it is greater than alpha, fail to reject the null hypothesis.
  • The category with the largest contribution is the category showing the greatest discrepancy from the null model.

Key Vocabulary

Chi-Square StatisticThe sum of the category contributions, measuring how far the observed counts are from the expected counts.
ContributionA category's value of (Observed minus Expected) squared divided by Expected.
P-ValueThe probability of getting a result as extreme as the observed one, or more extreme, assuming the null hypothesis is true.
Upper TailThe area to the right of the observed chi-square statistic on a chi-square distribution.
Chi-Square GOF-TestThe calculator procedure used to carry out a chi-square goodness-of-fit test.
Chi-Square CDFA calculator function that finds chi-square probabilities from a lower bound to an upper bound.
Table CA chi-square critical value table that gives upper-tail probabilities for selected chi-square values.
Significance LevelThe alpha value used to decide whether to reject the null hypothesis.
Reject H0The decision made when the p-value is less than or equal to alpha.
Fail to Reject H0The decision made when the p-value is greater than alpha.
