[00:00] — TOPIC 8.3 Carrying Out a Chi-Square Goodness of Fit Test
The title slide for "DAILY VIDEO 3" of AP Statistics Topic 8.3, featuring a graphic of a bridge.

[00:08] — What Will We Learn?
The slide outlines the video's learning objective:

How do we perform a complete significance test for a distribution of proportions for one categorical variable?

[00:17] — Let's Look At An Example
A blue transition slide introducing the upcoming practice problem.

[00:19] — 2008 International Exam
The slide introduces the context of a city testing if its assumed proportions of household participation in summer programs are still accurate. A table is provided showing the original expected proportions for six districts:

District A: 0.32

District B: 0.12

District C: 0.10

District D: 0.27

District E: 0.05

District F: 0.14

[00:34] — 2008 International Exam (continued)
The slide adds the observed data from a simple random sample taken this past summer:

Number of Households: A (100), B (35), C (40), D (22), E (12), F (31)
The slide lists two questions to answer:
(a) Give statistical evidence to justify the claim that the proportions are no longer valid.
(b) Which district had the greatest change in participation since 2000?

[01:11] — Planning the Response
The presenter highlights key phrases in the prompt ("one sample," "simple random sample," "number of households") to identify that the variable is categorical with 6 categories, leading to the conclusion that a chi-square goodness-of-fit test is required.

[03:23] — Procedure & Conditions
The presenter begins checking the conditions for the chosen test:

Identification of procedure: We will use a chi-square goodness-of-fit test.

Condition 1: The study uses a simple random sample of households. (Checked)

[03:38] — Procedure & Conditions (continued)
The presenter adds the sampled households to find a total sample size of 240.

Condition 2 (10% rule): We must assume that 240 households are less than or equal to 10% of all the households in the city. (Checked)

[04:05] — Procedure & Conditions (continued)
The slide shows the calculation for expected counts by multiplying the hypothesized proportions by the total sample size (240):

Expected counts: A = 76.8, B = 28.8, C = 24, D = 64.8, E = 12, F = 33.6

Condition 3: All expected counts are greater than 5. (Checked)
