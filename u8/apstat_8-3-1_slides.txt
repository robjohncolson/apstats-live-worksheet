[02:06] — Calculating the Test Statistic
The slide builds a table step-by-step to calculate the statistic. First, an "Expected Number" column is added. Expected counts are found by multiplying the null hypothesis proportions by the sample size (40):

0.452(40) = 18.08

0.292(40) = 11.68

0.256(40) = 10.24

[02:18] — Calculating the Test Statistic (Differences)
A new column "(Obs - Exp)" is added to show the difference between observed and expected counts:

20 - 18.08 = 1.92

17 - 11.68 = 5.32

3 - 10.24 = -7.24

[02:30] — Calculating the Test Statistic (Squared Differences)
A new column "(Obs - Exp)²" is added to keep values positive and give more weight to extreme deviations:

(1.92)² = 3.6864

(5.32)² = 28.3024

(-7.24)² = 52.4176

[02:38] — Calculating the Test Statistic (Ratios)
A final column "(Obs - Exp)² / Exp" is added, taking into account the sample size by finding the ratio of squared differences to expected values:

3.6864 / 18.08 = 0.20389

28.3024 / 11.68 = 2.42315

52.4176 / 10.24 = 5.11891

[05:38] — Why the Upper Tail?
A graph of a chi-square distribution is shown to explain why only the upper tail is used.

Text explains that a chi-square distribution only takes nonnegative values.

A green dot is placed at zero. "A chi-square statistic of zero means the observed and expected counts are equal (a perfect fit!)."

The right tail of the graph is shaded red. "As we move further away from zero, this implies the deviations between the observed and expected counts are larger, which means the fit is less good."
