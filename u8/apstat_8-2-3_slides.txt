[00:29] — Predatory Lending
A slide introducing the context. It features an image of a house resting on a loan application. The text reads:
"Payday loans and title loans are examples of predatory lending where borrowers are left paying interest rates of 100% or more in some cases, making it impossible to pay off the loans. Often, these borrowers have few options due to low credit scores or financial hardship."
"The 'predatory' label of these tactics comes from a pattern of targeting people who are low-income, elderly, or who have little formal education. Predatory lenders argue that they provide a service and that people from all backgrounds use their services, but is this true?"

[00:41] — Predatory Lending Recap
A map of Dallas County, TX, is shown with census tracts outlined and black dots indicating locations. The text reads:
"The map shows census tracts in Dallas County, TX."
"A random sample of 40 predatory lending businesses with Dallas, TX addresses was selected. Their approximate locations are plotted as black points on the map."
"Do these types of businesses tend to be located primarily in lower income regions, or are they found proportionally in regions of all income levels?"

[02:28] — Identifying the Procedure
A sequence of questions and answers appears on screen to determine the correct test:

[02:32] What type of data were collected? Business locations were recorded as observed counts across three categories of one variable: median household income bracket.

[02:44] How many groups? We have one random sample of 40 predatory lending businesses.

[02:51] What are we asked to do? Determine if there is <u>convincing statistical evidence</u> that predatory lending businesses are NOT located in regions of varying income levels proportionally.

[03:03] Conclusion appears: Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test
From [03:09] to [03:19], the presenter highlights parts of the text to explain the name of the test: "three categories" links to "Chi-Square", "one variable" and "one random sample" link to "Goodness-of-Fit", and "convincing statistical evidence" links to "Test".

[03:24] — Checking the Conditions
The slide lists the conditions that must be checked:

"To check for independence:"

[03:32] "1. The data should come from a random sample OR a randomized experiment."

[03:38] "2. When sampling without replacement, the sample should be less than or equal to 10% of the respective population."

[03:47] "The chi-square goodness-of-fit test becomes more accurate with more observations, so large counts should be used (shape)."

[03:56] "3. All expected counts should be greater than 5."

[04:59] — Battleship Quadrants
