[02:35] — Calculating a p-value
The slide explains the definition of a p-value:
"The p-value is the probability of observing a chi-square statistic at least as large as the one observed, assuming the null hypothesis and probability model are true."
"For a chi-square test for homogeneity or independence, the p-value is calculated from a chi-square distribution with degrees of freedom given by the formula:"

[03:03] — Calculating Degrees of Freedom
The formula for degrees of freedom appears: df = (number of rows - 1)(number of columns - 1).
The data table (without totals) is shown to demonstrate the calculation: df = (3 - 1)(2 - 1) = 2. The instructor notes that the total rows and columns are excluded when counting rows and columns for this formula.

[03:37] — Explanation of Degrees of Freedom
The table with expected counts is shown again. The instructor explains why df = 2 makes sense: only the first two expected counts have the freedom to vary. Once those two are calculated, the remaining four expected counts are automatically determined by subtracting from the fixed row and column totals.
