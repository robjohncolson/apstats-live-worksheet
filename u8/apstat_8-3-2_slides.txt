[06:16] — Multiple-Choice Example (Setup)
<Text and an image describe a Battleship game setup where 100 players were asked to place five ships. A grid shows how the board is divided into four quadrants (Quadrant 1 in the top left, going clockwise to Quadrant 4).>

[06:24] — Multiple-Choice Example (Question and Data)
<A table shows the observed counts of ships in each quadrant (1: 16, 2: 22, 3: 33, 4: 29). The question asks: "A chi-square goodness-of-fit test to determine whether the proportions of players preferring each of the four quadrants are the same resulted in a p-value of 0.079. Which of the following is a correct interpretation of the p-value?">

[06:41] — Multiple-Choice Example (Answer Choices)
<Five multiple-choice options (A through E) are presented for the correct interpretation of the p-value.>

[06:51] — Multiple-Choice Example (Walkthrough/Solution)
<Large text warns to "PAY ATTENTION TO WHAT THE QUESTION IS ASKING!" The instructor crosses out incorrect options:

A is wrong because a p-value > alpha means you fail to reject the null.

B is wrong because the condition is Expected Counts > 5, not Observed Counts > 30.

C is wrong because that is not the definition of a p-value.

E is wrong because the assumption must be based on the null hypothesis, not the alternative.

D is selected as the correct answer: "There is a 0.079 probability that we would get results this extreme or more extreme, by chance alone, if the proportions of players preferring different quadrants is the same.">
