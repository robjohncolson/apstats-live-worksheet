[00:33] — Where Do You Go To School?
The slide presents the first problem scenario:
"A random sample of parents with school-aged children was taken during 2019. A separate random sample of parents with school-aged children was taken in 2020. Parents were asked what type of school their children attended. Here are the results."
A table displays the observed counts:

Public: 266 (2019 Sample), 163 (2020 Sample)

Private/Parochial/Charter: 16 (2019 Sample), 21 (2020 Sample)

Home: 38 (2019 Sample), 30 (2020 Sample)
Text at the bottom reads: "Researchers would like to perform a test to determine if the distribution of school type for all school-aged children differed between the two years. Check the conditions for inference." A stock photo of students is on the right.

[01:07] — Where Do You Go To School? (Hypotheses)
The data table remains, but the bottom text is replaced with the hypotheses established in a previous video:
"H_0: There is no difference in the distribution of school types attended by school-aged children from 2019 to 2020."
"H_a: There is a difference in the distribution of school types attended by school-aged children from 2019 to 2020."
A final line notes: "We also identified the procedure as a chi-square test for homogeneity."

[01:40] — Checking the Conditions
A new slide introduces the general concept of checking conditions. Text appears:
"Remember that for all inference procedures in AP Statistics you must verify that the conditions for using that procedure are met."
"In general, you should check for:
• independence in the methods used to collect the data, and
• that the appropriate sampling distribution has the correct shape."

[02:13] — Checking the Conditions (Specifics)
The slide updates to detail the specific conditions for this test:
"Here are the conditions for a chi-square test for homogeneity or independence."
"To check for independence:"
"1. Data should be collected using a stratified random sample or randomized experiment (homogeneity) or a single random sample (independence)."
"2. When sampling without replacement, the sample size is less than or equal to 10% of the population size."
"To check that the shape of the sampling distribution is approximately a chi-square distribution:"
"3. All expected counts should be greater than 5."

[03:15] — Checking the Conditions (Applying to Example 1)
The slide returns to the "Where Do You Go To School?" context. The data table is shown again, now including a "Total" row: 320 for the 2019 sample and 214 for the 2020 sample. The presenter checks off the conditions one by one, with a green checkmark appearing for each:

"1. There are two independent random samples (stratified random sample)."

[03:40] "2. It's reasonable to believe 320 parents is less than or equal to 10% of all parents in 2019 and 214 parents is less than or equal to 10% of all parents in 2020."

[04:00] "3. All expected counts are greater than 5."
At this point, the table updates to display the calculated expected counts in blue parentheses next to the observed counts:

Public: (257.1), (171.9)

Private/Parochial/Charter: (22), (14.8)

Home: (40.7), (27.3)
Finally, the text "The conditions are met." appears at the bottom.

[04:38] — LET'S PRACTICE
A transition title card for a second practice problem.

[04:41] — Are You Working?
A new problem is presented:
"A random sample of 2000 adults revealed the following data about education level and employment status."
A table shows counts cross-referencing employment status with education level:

Employed: 206 (No High School Diploma), 548 (High School Diploma, No College), 1186 (High school Diploma, Some College)

Unemployed: 14 (No High School Diploma), 22 (High School Diploma, No College), 24 (High school Diploma, Some College)
Text below reads: "Researchers wants to determine if there is an association between education level and employment status. Check the conditions for inference." A stock photo of graduates throwing their caps is on the right.

[05:01] — Are You Working? (Hypotheses)
The text below the table is replaced to show the established hypotheses:
"H_0: There is no association between education level and employment status for all adults."
"H_a: There is an association between education level and employment status for all adults."
A note at the bottom says: "We also identified the procedure as a chi-square test for independence."

[05:25] — Are You Working? (Checking conditions)
The slide transitions to checking the conditions for this new example. The data table remains. Green checkmarks appear as each condition is verified:

"1. The 2000 adults were randomly selected."

[05:38] "2. It's reasonable to believe 2000 adults is less than or equal to 10% of all adults."

[05:49] "3. All expected counts are greater than 5."
The table updates to show the expected counts in blue parentheses below the observed counts:

Employed: (213.4), (552.9), (1173.7)

Unemployed: (6.6), (17.1), (36.3)
The text "The conditions are met." appears at the bottom.
