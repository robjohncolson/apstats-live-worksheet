Here is a detailed timeline of the video, describing each slide and visual change:

[00:00] — Title Slide
The video starts with the title slide: "TOPIC 8.2 Setting Up a Chi-Square Goodness of Fit Test". Below it, "DAILY VIDEO 1 In this video, we will illustrate the properties of chi-square distributions." The AP Statistics logo is visible on the left.

[00:05] — What Will We Learn?
This slide outlines two main learning objectives:

What does the chi-square statistic measure?

How do the degrees of freedom affect the shape of chi-square distributions?
An overlay appears that says "LET'S LOOK AT AN EXAMPLE".

[00:15] — Predatory Lending
A text-heavy slide introduces the example context, explaining that payday and title loans are forms of predatory lending with interest rates often exceeding 100%. These tactics frequently target low-income, elderly, or individuals with less formal education. An image of a house on top of a loan application is shown.

[00:51] — Predatory Lending Map
A map of Dallas County, TX census tracts is shown. The text explains that a random sample of 40 predatory lending businesses was selected and plotted as black dots on the map. The question is posed: Do these businesses tend to be located primarily in lower-income regions, or are they found proportionally across all income levels?

[01:21] — Observed Counts
A table titled "Observed Counts" is introduced. It categorizes household incomes into three brackets and lists the observed number of businesses in census tracts corresponding to each bracket:

$0 to less than $50,000: 20 businesses

$50,000 to less than $100,000: 17 businesses

$100,000 and above: 3 businesses
Total businesses = 40.

[01:40] — Expected Counts (Bar Graph)
To find expected counts, the instructor assumes businesses should be distributed proportionally to households in these income brackets. A bar graph labeled "Percent of Dallas, TX Households" displays 16 income brackets. The instructor groups these into the three broad categories used in the problem, calculating the totals: 45.2% for the lowest bracket, 29.2% for the middle, and 25.6% for the highest.

[02:21] — Calculating Expected Counts & Differences
The previous table is expanded to include a column for the "Expected Number of Predatory Lending Businesses". This is calculated by multiplying the percentage of households by the sample size (40):

0.452 * 40 = 18.08

0.292 * 40 = 11.68

0.256 * 40 = 10.24
A final column, "Difference (Obs – Exp)", shows the differences: 1.92, 5.32, and -7.24. The sum of these differences is shown to be exactly 0.
