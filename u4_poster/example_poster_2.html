🎯 CONDITIONAL PROBABILITY & INDEPENDENCE

Topics 4.4 | 4.5 | 4.6
When events affect each other — and when they don't
📐 KEY FORMULAS
P(A | B) = P(A and B) / P(B)
Conditional Probability — P(A GIVEN B)
P(A and B) = P(A) · P(B | A)
General Multiplication Rule
P(A and B) = P(A) · P(B) [if independent]
Multiplication Rule for Independent Events
P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)
General Addition Rule
🧪 Independence Test:
If P(A | B) = P(A), then A & B are independent
"Knowing B doesn't change probability of A"

✏️ WORKED EXAMPLE
Problem: A survey of 200 students: 120 play sports, 90 play an instrument, 50 do both.
Find: (a) P(Instrument | Sports)   (b) P(Sports or Instrument)   (c) Are they independent?
(a) P(Inst | Sports)
P(I | S) = P(I and S) / P(S)
= 50/120
= 0.417
41.7% of athletes play an instrument
(b) P(Sports or Inst)
P(S or I) = P(S) + P(I) − P(S and I)
= 120/200 + 90/200 − 50/200
= 160/200 = 0.80
80% do at least one activity
(c) Independence Test
P(I) = 90/200 = 0.45
P(I | S) = 0.417

0.45 ≠ 0.417
❌ NOT independent!
⚠️ WATCH OUT!
  • P(A | B) ≠ P(B | A)! Order matters completely!
  • Don't forget to subtract in addition rule when events overlap
  • "Or" usually bigger than "And" — sanity check your answers
  • Don't ASSUME independence — you must TEST it!
  • Conditional from table: Denominator is row/column total, NOT 200
  • Mutually exclusive ≠ Independent — totally different concepts!
