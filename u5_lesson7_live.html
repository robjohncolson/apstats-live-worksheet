Topic 5.7: Sampling Distributions for Sample Means

From Proportions to Means — Parameters, Shape, & Probability
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • UNC-3.Q: Determine parameters for a sampling distribution for sample means.
  • UNC-3.R: Determine whether a sampling distribution of a sample mean can be described as approximately normal.
  • UNC-3.S: Interpret probabilities and parameters for a sampling distribution for a sample mean.
Essential Knowledge:
  • The sampling distribution of x has mean μ and standard deviation σ/√n.
  • The sampling distribution is approximately normal when the population is normal OR when n ≥ 30 (CLT).

Key Vocabulary

Sample Mean (x) The average of all values in a sample; a statistic used to estimate the population mean μ
Sampling Distribution The distribution of a statistic (here, x) across all possible random samples of a given size from a population
Central Limit Theorem For large enough sample sizes (n ≥ 30), the sampling distribution of x is approximately normal regardless of the population shape
10% Condition The sample size must be less than 10% of the population size for the standard deviation formula to be valid
Unbiased Estimator A statistic whose sampling distribution is centered at the true parameter — x is an unbiased estimator of μ

The Big Idea: Individuals vs. Averages

16. [4:51] A single lemon weighing 4.5 oz is not surprising — it's only 1 SD above the mean of the population. But an average of 6 lemons at 4.5+ oz is very surprising. This is because averages are much variable than individual values: the SD drops from 0.5 oz (individual) to 0.204 oz (sample mean of 6).
