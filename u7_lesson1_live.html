AP Statistics Unit 7 Inference for Means

Topic 7.1: Introducing Statistics: Should I Worry About Error?

The Logic of Significance Testing for a Difference in Means
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Identify the evidence in a study comparing two group means.
  • Describe the two possible explanations for an observed difference in sample means.
  • Use a rerandomization simulation to estimate how likely an observed result is by chance alone.
  • Decide whether the data provide convincing evidence for a claim about a difference in means.
Essential Knowledge:
  • If a treatment makes no difference, the expected difference in sample means is 0.
  • An observed difference can be explained by chance variation in random assignment or by a real treatment effect.
  • A simulation under the "no difference" assumption estimates how unusual the observed statistic would be by chance alone.
  • If the observed result is very unlikely under chance alone, the chance explanation can be ruled out and the treatment explanation is supported.
  • Random assignment allows a causal conclusion about students like the ones in the study.

Key Vocabulary

Claim A statement being evaluated with data, such as whether one wording causes more spending than another.
Evidence The observed result from the study that supports or fails to support the claim.
Difference in Means The difference between two sample averages, used here to compare spending in the two groups.
Chance Variation Natural random differences that can appear just because subjects were randomly assigned to groups.
Random Assignment Assigning subjects to treatments by chance so the groups are comparable.
Rerandomization Shuffling subjects into groups again and again under the assumption that the treatment makes no difference.
Simulation A repeated random process used to estimate how likely a result would be if chance alone were operating.
Convincing Evidence Evidence strong enough that the chance-alone explanation is not very believable.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. Evidence for a claim comes from the observed statistic, such as the difference in sample means.
  2. If there is no treatment effect, the expected difference in means is 0.
  3. A rerandomization simulation estimates how likely the observed statistic would be by chance alone.
  4. If that probability is very small, the chance explanation is not convincing and the treatment explanation is supported.
