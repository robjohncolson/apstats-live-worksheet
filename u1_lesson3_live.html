AP Statistics Unit 1 Categorical Data

Topic 1.3: Topic 1.3

Representing a Categorical Variable with Frequency and Relative Frequency Tables
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Identify the individuals and the variable in a categorical data set.
  • Recognize when a variable is categorical because its values are category labels.
  • Represent categorical data with a frequency table.
  • Represent categorical data with a relative frequency table.
  • Convert counts to proportions or percentages by dividing by the total number of individuals.
  • Use counts and relative frequencies to describe a distribution of categorical data.
  • Decide whether statements about a categorical distribution are supported by a table.
Essential Knowledge:
  • A categorical variable takes values that are category names or labels.
  • A frequency table gives the number of individuals, or cases, in each category.
  • A relative frequency table gives the proportion or percent of individuals in each category.
  • Relative frequencies are found by dividing each category count by the total number of individuals.
  • Percentages and proportions make it easier to compare categories and evaluate statements such as majority, nearly half, or over one-third.
  • Descriptions of categorical data should be justified with numerical evidence from the table and written in context.

Key Vocabulary

Categorical VariableA variable whose values are category names or labels rather than numerical measurements.
IndividualsThe people or objects described by the data.
CasesAnother word for individuals in a data set.
CategoryOne possible label or response for a categorical variable.
DistributionWhat values a variable takes and how often each value occurs.
FrequencyThe count of individuals in a category.
Frequency TableA table that lists each category and its count.
Relative FrequencyThe proportion or percent of individuals in a category.
Relative Frequency TableA table that lists each category and its proportion or percent.
ProportionA part-to-whole comparison, often written as a decimal or fraction.
PercentA proportion out of 100.
MajorityAt least 50% of the individuals in the data set.

Exit Ticket

A random sample of 40 students was asked which school lunch change they would most like to see. The responses were: more choices = 14, shorter lines = 10, lower prices = 8, healthier meals = 5, and bigger portions = 3.

(a) Identify the individuals and the variable, and state whether the variable is categorical or quantitative.

(b) Create or clearly describe a frequency table and give the relative frequency or percent for each category.

(c) Is the statement "A majority of students chose either more choices or shorter lines" supported? Justify with numbers.

(d) Write one other valid statement that describes the distribution in context using a count, proportion, or percent.
