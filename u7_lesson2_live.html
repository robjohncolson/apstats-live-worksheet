AP Statistics Unit 7 Inference for Means

Topic 7.2: Constructing a Confidence Interval for a Population Mean

Procedure, Conditions, Critical Values, and Margin of Error
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Identify the correct confidence interval procedure for estimating a population mean.
  • Verify the randomization, 10% condition, and shape condition for a one-sample t-interval.
  • Calculate margin of error using a t critical value and the standard error s/√n.
  • Construct and interpret a confidence interval for μ in context.
Essential Knowledge:
  • Confidence intervals estimate a population parameter with an interval to account for sampling variability.
  • When estimating a single population mean, use a one-sample t-interval for a population mean.
  • If n < 30, the sample data should be free of strong skewness and outliers.
  • The margin of error is t* times the standard error of the statistic.
  • For a mean, the confidence interval form is x̄ ± t*s/√n with degrees of freedom n − 1.

Key Vocabulary

One-Sample t-Interval The confidence interval procedure used to estimate the mean of a single population.
Critical Value (t*) The multiplier from a t distribution that gives the desired confidence level.
Standard Error An estimate of the standard deviation of a statistic's sampling distribution; for x̄, it is s/√n.
Margin of Error The amount added to and subtracted from the point estimate when building a confidence interval.
Degrees of Freedom For a one-sample t-interval, the value n − 1 that determines which t distribution to use.
10% Condition When sampling without replacement, the sample size should be less than or equal to 10% of the population.
Shape Condition The sample size is at least 30, or if smaller than 30, the sample data show no strong skewness or outliers.
Confidence Level The long-run capture rate for intervals built from repeated random samples of the same size.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. Use a one-sample t-interval when estimating the mean of a single population.
  2. Check randomization, the 10% condition, and the shape condition before calculating the interval.
  3. For a population mean, margin of error is t* times s/√n, where df = n − 1.
  4. Build the interval with x̄ ± margin of error and interpret it in context of the population mean.
