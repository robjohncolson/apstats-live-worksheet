AP Statistics Unit 7 Inference for Means

Topic 7.3: Justifying a Claim About a Population Mean Based on a Confidence Interval

Interpretation, Confidence Level, Margin of Error, and Claims
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Interpret a confidence interval for a population mean in context.
  • Use a confidence interval to justify whether a claim about a population mean is plausible.
  • Interpret the confidence level of a confidence interval using repeated random samples.
  • Describe how sample size and confidence level affect margin of error for a one-sample t-interval.
  • Carry out the full process for constructing and interpreting a confidence interval for μ.
Essential Knowledge:
  • An interval interpretation should identify the confidence level, the numerical interval, and the population mean in context.
  • If a claimed mean is inside a confidence interval, that value is plausible and there is not convincing evidence against the claim.
  • If every value in the interval is above or below a comparison value, the interval can support a directional claim about the population mean.
  • The confidence level describes the long-run capture rate of intervals from repeated random samples, not the probability for one completed interval.
  • For a population mean, a larger sample size or a lower confidence level will reduce the margin of error and narrow the interval.
  • A complete inference response should define the parameter, choose the procedure, verify conditions, calculate the interval, and interpret it in context.

Key Vocabulary

Plausible Value A parameter value that lies inside a confidence interval and is therefore consistent with the sample data.
Confidence Level The long-run proportion of intervals from repeated random samples that capture the true population parameter.
Repeated Random Sampling The process used to interpret confidence level by imagining many samples of the same size from the same population.
Capture When a confidence interval contains the true value of the population parameter.
Margin of Error The amount added to and subtracted from the point estimate to create the confidence interval.
Interval Width The total distance from the lower bound to the upper bound; it is twice the margin of error.
Directional Claim A claim that the population mean is greater than or less than a specific value.
Context The real-world description of the population and variable that must be included in statistical interpretations.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. Interpret a confidence interval by naming the confidence level, the interval bounds, and the population mean in context.
  2. Use interval location to justify claims: inside means plausible, all above supports a greater-than claim, and all below supports a less-than claim.
  3. Interpret confidence level with repeated random sampling, not as the probability for one completed interval.
  4. Increase sample size or decrease confidence level to reduce margin of error; then complete the full State-Plan-Do-Conclude process in context.
