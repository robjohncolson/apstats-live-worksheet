Topic 5.6: Sampling Distributions for Differences in Sample Proportions

Comparing Two Populations — Parameters, Shape, & Probability
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • UNC-3.N: Determine parameters of a sampling distribution for a difference in sample proportions.
  • UNC-3.O: Determine whether a sampling distribution for a difference of sample proportions can be described as approximately normal.
  • UNC-3.P: Interpret probabilities and parameters for a sampling distribution for a difference in proportions.
Essential Knowledge:
  • The sampling distribution of p1 − p2 has mean p1 − p2 and a standard deviation that combines the variability from both populations.
  • Approximate normality requires the Large Counts condition to hold for both populations.

Key Vocabulary

Difference in Sample Proportions The statistic p̂1 − p̂2, computed from independent random samples drawn from two different populations
Sampling Distribution The distribution of a statistic (here, the difference in proportions) across all possible pairs of random samples of given sizes
Large Counts Condition For approximate normality, all four products n1p1, n1(1−p1), n2p2, and n2(1−p2) must be ≥ 10
10% Condition Each sample size must be less than 10% of its population size for the standard deviation formula to be valid
Unbiased Estimator A statistic whose sampling distribution is centered at the true parameter value — on average, it hits the target
z-score The number of standard deviations a value lies from the mean: z = (value − mean) / SD. Used to calculate probabilities from the normal distribution

Part 3: Shape — When Is the Distribution Approximately Normal?

[7:04 – 9:48]
Large Counts Condition (for two proportions): [7:35]
The sampling distribution of p̂1 − p̂2 is approximately normal when all four of these are ≥ 10:
   n1p1 ≥ 10     n1(1 − p1) ≥ 10     n2p2 ≥ 10     n2(1 − p2) ≥ 10
9. [7:35] For a single proportion (Topic 5.5), we checked two products: np ≥ 10 and n(1 − p) ≥ 10. For the difference in two proportions, we must check products because we need both individual sampling distributions to be approximately normal.
