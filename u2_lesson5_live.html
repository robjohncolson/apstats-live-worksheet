AP Statistics Unit 2 Exploring Two-Variable Data

Topic 2.5: Required Course Content

Correlation
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Determine the correlation, r, for a linear relationship between two quantitative variables.
  • Interpret the sign and magnitude of correlation in context.
  • Explain that correlation is unit-free and always between -1 and 1, inclusive.
  • Recognize that technology is commonly used to calculate correlation.
  • Explain the limits of correlation, including that correlation does not imply causation.
Essential Knowledge:
  • The correlation, r, gives the direction and quantifies the strength of the linear association between two quantitative variables.
  • The correlation coefficient can be calculated with a formula, but the most common way to determine r is by using technology.
  • Correlation is unit-free and always between -1 and 1, inclusive.
  • A value of r = 0 indicates no linear association, while r = 1 or r = -1 indicates a perfect linear association.
  • A correlation coefficient close to 1 or -1 does not, by itself, prove that a linear model is appropriate.
  • A real or perceived relationship between two variables does not necessarily mean that changes in one variable cause changes in the other.

Key Vocabulary

CorrelationA numerical measure of the direction and strength of a linear relationship between two quantitative variables.
Correlation CoefficientThe value r that summarizes the direction and strength of a linear association.
Positive CorrelationAs x-values increase, y-values tend to increase.
Negative CorrelationAs x-values increase, y-values tend to decrease.
MagnitudeThe distance of r from 0; it is used to describe strength.
Unit-freeHaving no measurement units attached to the numerical value.
Linear AssociationA relationship that follows an approximately straight-line pattern.
Strong CorrelationA correlation with magnitude close to 1.
Weak CorrelationA correlation with magnitude close to 0.
Perfect Linear AssociationA relationship with r = 1 or r = -1.
No Linear AssociationA relationship with r = 0.
CausationA cause-and-effect relationship in which a change in one variable produces a change in another.
