Turn the claim into a question. Big letters, top of the paper.

The claim, word for wordWriter

"Nobody who chose Telepathy wants to be Famous: 0 of 68. Since the two never happen together, Telepathy and Famous are independent. One has nothing to do with the other." · Data: Super Status, 433 students.

✓1 The joint probabilityTable Keeper Visual

P(Telepathy and Famous) = 0/433 = 0.

This tells us no student in this table chose Telepathy and wants to be Famous, so the two events are mutually exclusive.

Table Keeper writes the probability. Visual shades the Telepathy row and the Famous column on the big table; the cell where they cross is this joint.

✓2 The independence testTable Keeper

P(Famous) = 15/433 = 3.5%

P(Famous | Telepathy) = 0/68 = 0%

These are not equal, so the events are not independent.

✓3 Verdict with BOTH termsWriter

Telepathy and Famous are mutually exclusive and are not independent: knowing a student chose Telepathy changes the chance they want to be Famous, from 3.5% to 0%.

✓4 The proof (2 or 3 sentences)Writer

Independence says P(A and B) must equal P(A) · P(B).

Mutually exclusive says P(A and B) = 0.

Suppose A and B can each happen, so P(A) and P(B) are both more than 0. Can those two statements both be true? cannot, because P(A) · P(B) is more than 0, so it cannot also equal 0.
