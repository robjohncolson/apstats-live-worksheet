Topic 7.6: Confidence Intervals for the Difference of Two Means

Choosing the Procedure, Checking Conditions, and Calculating the Interval
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Identify when a 2-sample t-interval is an appropriate confidence interval procedure for comparing two population means.
  • Verify the independence, 10% condition, and normality or large-sample conditions for a confidence interval for μ1 − μ2.
  • Use the general confidence interval structure statistic ± (critical value)(standard error of statistic).
  • Calculate the point estimate, margin of error, and confidence interval for a difference in population means.
  • Use technology to find the degrees of freedom and construct a 2-SampTInt with pooled set to no.
  • Interpret a confidence interval for a difference in means in context and connect the sign of the interval to which group tends to be larger.
Essential Knowledge:
  • When comparing two independent groups with quantitative data and estimating a difference in means, the appropriate procedure is a 2-sample t-interval.
  • Independence requires two independent random samples or a randomized experiment; when sampling without replacement, each sample should be no more than 10% of its population.
  • If both sample sizes are greater than 30, the sampling distribution of x̄1 − x̄2 is approximately normal; otherwise, both sample distributions should be free from extreme skewness and outliers.
  • The two-sample confidence interval has the form (x̄1 − x̄2) ± t*√((s12/n1) + (s22/n2)).
  • The point estimate is the difference in sample means, and the margin of error is the critical value times the standard error.
  • For a two-sample t-interval, degrees of freedom are typically found with technology rather than a simple n − 1 rule.

Key Vocabulary

2-Sample t-Interval A confidence interval procedure used to estimate the difference between two population means from independent samples.
Independent Samples Two samples collected so that knowing the outcomes in one group does not determine the outcomes in the other group.
Difference in Means The parameter μ1 − μ2 or the statistic x̄1 − x̄2.
Point Estimate The sample statistic at the center of the interval; here it is the difference in sample means.
Margin of Error The amount added and subtracted from the point estimate to create the interval.
Critical Value The t* value determined by the confidence level and the degrees of freedom.
Standard Error A measure of how much the difference in sample means typically varies from the difference in population means.
Degrees of Freedom For a two-sample t-interval, a value usually found with technology and used to determine the critical value.
Pooled: No The default calculator setting for a 2-SampTInt because pooling requires a different procedure and extra conditions.
Interpretation A contextual statement about plausible values of the true difference in population means.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. Use a 2-sample t-interval when you have two independent groups, quantitative data, and want to estimate a difference in population means.
  2. Check independence, the 10% condition when sampling without replacement, and either both sample sizes greater than 30 or roughly normal sample distributions with no extreme skewness or outliers.
  3. For two means, the confidence interval is (x̄1 − x̄2) ± t*√((s12/n1) + (s22/n2)).
  4. Degrees of freedom and the final interval are typically found with technology, and the same interval can be written with endpoints or as point estimate ± margin of error.
