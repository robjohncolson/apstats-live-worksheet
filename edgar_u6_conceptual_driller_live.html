AP Statistics Unit 6 Inference for Proportions

From Sample Proportions to Population Proportions

Edgar's Bottom-Up Conceptual Worksheet
Goal: build inference from first principles, not from button pushing
What this worksheet is trying to accomplish:
  • Explain why a sample proportion p̂ can tell us something about a population proportion p.
  • Keep the caveats visible at every step: randomness, independence, the 10% condition, and normal-approximation checks.
  • Separate the four values students most often blur together: p, p̂, p0, and p̂c.
  • Connect confidence intervals, significance tests, Type I / Type II errors, and two-proportion inference into one coherent picture.
Important design choice: The existing Unit 6 study plan starts with confidence intervals and hypothesis tests. This worksheet intentionally starts one layer earlier, with the sampling-distribution idea underneath those procedures, because the formulas in Unit 6 make no sense unless you already understand why repeated samples would make p̂ vary around p.

4. Confidence Intervals for a Population Proportion

[Topics 6.2–6.3]

Core CI Formula

p̂ ± z* √(p̂(1 − p̂) / n)

Interpretation rule: the interval either captures the true population proportion or it does not. The confidence level tells us how often this method succeeds in repeated random sampling.

8. Two-Proportion Significance Tests

[Topics 6.10–6.11]

Core Pooling Idea

p̂c = (n1p̂1 + n2p̂2) / (n1 + n2)

Pooling is used in a test because H0 says the population proportions are equal. Pooling is not used in a CI because a CI does not assume equality.
