AP Statistics Unit 2 Exploring Two-Variable Data

Topic 2.3: Required Course Content

Joint, Marginal, and Conditional Relative Frequencies
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Calculate joint, marginal, and conditional relative frequencies from a two-way table.
  • Use row totals, column totals, and the table total to choose the correct denominator for a percent.
  • Compare distributions of a categorical variable across groups using conditional relative frequencies.
  • Use summary statistics for two categorical variables to decide whether the variables are associated.
  • Connect the conditional relative frequencies in a table to the segmented bar graph from the previous lesson.
Essential Knowledge:
  • Marginal relative frequencies are the row and column totals in a two-way table divided by the total for the entire table.
  • A conditional relative frequency is a relative frequency for a specific part of the contingency table, usually within one row or one column.
  • A joint relative frequency is a cell frequency divided by the total for the entire table.
  • Summary statistics for two categorical variables can be used to compare distributions and determine whether variables are associated.
  • If the conditional distributions are not the same for all groups, that is evidence of an association.

Key Vocabulary

Joint Relative FrequencyA cell count divided by the total number of observations in the table.
Marginal Relative FrequencyA row total or column total divided by the table total.
Conditional Relative FrequencyA relative frequency found within a specific row or column of a two-way table.
Row TotalThe total for one row of a two-way table.
Column TotalThe total for one column of a two-way table.
Table TotalThe grand total of all observations in the two-way table.
DistributionHow the values of a categorical variable are divided among its categories.
AssociationA relationship in which knowing one categorical variable helps predict another.
Two-Way TableA table that summarizes two categorical variables at the same time.
Segmented Bar GraphA graph that shows conditional relative frequencies stacked to 100% for each group.
