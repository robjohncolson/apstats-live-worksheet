AP Statistics Unit 3 Collecting Data

Topic 3.4: Potential Problems with Sampling

How sampling and survey bias can lead to misleading estimates
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Identify potential sources of bias in sampling methods.
  • Recognize undercoverage, nonresponse, and volunteer response bias.
  • Describe how bias can lead to an overestimate or underestimate.
  • Recognize question wording bias and self-reported response bias in surveys.
  • Write a complete AP-style explanation of bias using the population, sample, and direction of the bias.
Essential Knowledge:
  • Bias occurs when certain responses are systematically favored over others.
  • Undercoverage bias occurs when part of the population has a reduced chance of being included in the sample.
  • Nonresponse bias occurs when selected individuals do not respond and may differ from those who do respond.
  • Volunteer response bias occurs when people choose themselves to participate and those volunteers may not represent the population.
  • Problems with survey wording or self-reported answers can also create biased results.
  • Good bias explanations describe how the sample differs from the population and the likely direction of the bias.

Key Vocabulary

BiasA systematic tendency to favor certain responses over others.
Undercoverage BiasBias that occurs when part of the population has a reduced chance of being included in the sample.
Nonresponse BiasBias that occurs when chosen individuals do not respond and may differ from those who do respond.
Volunteer Response BiasBias that occurs when people choose themselves to participate and volunteers differ from nonvolunteers.
PopulationThe entire group the study wants information about.
SampleThe individuals from whom data are actually collected.
OverestimateAn estimate that is systematically too high.
UnderestimateAn estimate that is systematically too low.
Question Wording BiasBias caused by confusing or leading survey questions.
Self-Reported Response BiasBias caused when people inaccurately report their own traits or behaviors.
Leading QuestionA question worded in a way that pushes respondents toward a certain answer.
RepresentativeAccurately reflecting the characteristics of the population.
