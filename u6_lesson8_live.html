Topic 6.8: Confidence Intervals for the Difference of Two Proportions

Choosing the Procedure, Checking Conditions, and Calculating the Interval
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • UNC-4.I: Identify the appropriate confidence interval procedure for comparing two population proportions.
  • UNC-4.J: Verify independence, the 10% condition when appropriate, and the large counts condition for a two-sample z-interval.
  • UNC-4.K / UNC-4.L: Calculate and interpret a confidence interval for the difference between two population proportions.
Essential Knowledge:
  • The correct procedure for comparing two population proportions is a two-sample z-interval for a difference in proportions.
  • To check independence, use two independent random samples or random assignment to two treatment groups; if sampling without replacement, also check the 10% condition.
  • To justify the z-interval, the observed counts of successes and failures in both groups should all be at least 10.
  • Confidence interval = point estimate ± margin of error, and margin of error = critical value times standard error.

Key Vocabulary

Two-Sample z-Interval A confidence interval procedure used to estimate the difference between two population proportions.
Margin of Error The amount a sample statistic is likely to vary from the population parameter for a chosen confidence level.
Critical Value The z* multiplier that captures the middle C% of the standard normal distribution.
Standard Error An estimate of the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of p̂1 - p̂2.
Independent Random Samples Two separate random samples taken from two populations being compared.
Randomized Experiment A study in which subjects are randomly assigned to treatment groups.
Point Estimate The sample difference p̂1 - p̂2, used as the center of the interval.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. Use a two-sample z-interval for a difference in proportions when comparing two population proportions.
  2. Check for two independent random samples or random assignment, apply the 10% condition when sampling without replacement, and verify all success/failure counts are at least 10.
  3. Margin of error = critical value times standard error.
  4. The confidence interval is centered at p̂1 - p̂2 and gives plausible values for the true difference p1 - p2 in context.
