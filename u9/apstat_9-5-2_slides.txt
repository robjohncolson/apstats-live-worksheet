A slide introducing a dataset from 11 Texas Algebra 1 students. It shows a table with two rows: "Percent attendance" and "Questions correct" with 11 data points for each. A scatterplot of the data is shown alongside a computer output table for a least-squares regression analysis, displaying "Coef," "SE Coef," "T," and "P" values for the "Constant" (-7.69) and "Attendance" (0.57).

At [01:36], text appears asking: "Do the data give convincing evidence at the a = 0.01 significance level of a positive, linear relationship between state test score and percent attendance for Texas Algebra 1 students?"

[01:50] — Test Scores and Attendance
The slide retains the scatterplot and regression table. It restates the question from the previous slide and begins building the framework for a hypothesis test: "From previous videos: H0: beta = 0 vs. Ha: beta > 0".

At [02:02], it defines beta: "where beta = the slope of the population regression line for predicting number of questions answered correctly on the state test from the percent of school days attended for Texas Algebra 1 students."

At [02:14], it adds the significance level "Use a = 0.01" and confirms "t-test for slope; Conditions are met."

[02:24] — Interpreting the p-value
The slide recaps findings: "From previous videos: b = 0.57, t = 9.18, and p-value ≈ 0.000". The scatterplot and regression table remain visible.

At [02:38], an explanation appears: "b = 0.57 is evidence against H0: beta = 0 and for Ha: beta > 0 because b = 0.57 > 0."

At [02:54], the general definition of a p-value is shown: "The p-value measures how likely it is to get evidence for Ha as strong as or stronger than the observed evidence by chance alone when H0 is true."

At [03:07], the specific interpretation for this problem appears in blue text: "Assuming that there is no linear relationship between state test score and percent attendance for Texas Algebra 1 students, there is an approximately 0 probability of getting a sample regression line with a slope of 0.57 or greater by chance alone in a random sample of 11 Texas students."

[03:27] — Making a Conclusion
A general instruction slide for conclusions. It outlines the logic: "Small p-values -> test statistic unlikely to occur by random chance" and "Large p-values -> test statistic not unlikely to occur by random chance".

At [03:44], a template for rejecting the null hypothesis appears: "Because the p-value of ___ <= a = ___, we reject H0. There is convincing statistical evidence that [state Ha in context]."

At [03:57], a template for failing to reject the null appears: "Because the p-value of ___ > a = ___, we fail to reject H0. There is not convincing statistical evidence that [state Ha in context]."

[04:09] — Making a Conclusion (Texas Example)
The slide applies the templates to the Texas schools example. It recaps the question, hypotheses, condition check, and calculated values (b, t, and p-value).

At [04:16], the completed conclusion appears: "Because the p-value of approximately 0 < a = 0.01, we reject H0. There is convincing statistical evidence of a positive, linear relationship between state test score and percent attendance for Texas Algebra 1 students."
