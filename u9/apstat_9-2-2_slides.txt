[00:34] — Predicting Old Faithful in 2019
This slide provides context for the example. It includes a picture of the Old Faithful geyser erupting. The text says:
"The Starnes family took its first trip to Yellowstone National Park in July 1995. They only had six hours in the park, but were able to see the Old Faithful geyser erupt.
Mr. and Mrs. Starnes returned to the park in July 2019. They wondered if the model used to predict eruptions of Old Faithful was still the same as in 1995. Earlier, we used simulation to determine that the answer is 'No.'
Can we construct a confidence interval for the slope of the population regression line in 2019?"

[01:15] — Old Faithful: 2019
The slide displays the data for the example.
Text: "The scatterplot displays data on the duration (in minutes) and wait time until the next eruption (in minutes) for a random sample of 25 Old Faithful eruptions in July 2019, along with the sample regression line. Computer output from a least-squares regression analysis is shown below."
A scatterplot shows "Wait Time (min)" on the y-axis (from 88 to 102) versus "Duration (min)" on the x-axis (from 3.5 to 4.5). A positive linear trend line is drawn through the points, with the equation displayed above it: "predicted wait time = 62.95 + 7.79(duration)".
Below the scatterplot is a table of computer output showing columns for Predictor, Coef, SE Coef, T-Value, and P-Value. The rows are "Constant" (Coef: 62.95) and "Duration(min)" (Coef: 7.79). Additional stats are listed below the table: S=4.20970, R-sq=14.01%, R-sq(adj)=10.27%.
