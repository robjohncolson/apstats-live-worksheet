# Video 2 — Slide Descriptions
# Unit 1, Lesson 4

[00:01] — Representing a Categorical Variable with Graphs (Topic 1.4, Video 2)

[00:20] — Learning Objectives
  * Representing multiple sets of data for the same categorical variable in tabular form.
  * Representing multiple sets of data for the same categorical variable graphically.
  * Comparing multiple sets of categorical data using these representations.

[00:46] — Superpower Preferences: Survey Data
  * Data for 80 boys and 125 girls regarding their choice of superpower (Fly, Freeze time, Invisibility, Super strength, or Telepathy).

[01:52] — Skill 2.D: Compare distributions

[02:11] — Practice Question: Frequency Table Comparison
  * A frequency table showing raw counts for male and female superpower preferences.
  * Multiple-choice question asking which statement is supported by the table.

[03:59] — Relative Frequencies
  * Explanation of why relative frequencies are better for comparing groups of different sizes.
  * A relative frequency table showing proportions for each gender.

[04:47] — Learning Objective: Compare multiple sets of categorical data

[04:59] — Side-by-Side Relative Frequency Bar Chart
  * A bar chart comparing male and female preferences using relative frequency.
  * Visual emphasizing the importance of labels, scales, and legends.

[06:04] — Practice Problem: Average Daily Screen Media Use
  * A bar chart comparing "Tweens" (ages 8-12) and "Teens" (ages 13-18).
  * Multiple-choice question to identify the statement supported by the graph.

[08:16] — Key Takeaways
  * Representing categorical data with frequency/relative frequency tables.
  * Representing categorical data with relative frequency bar graphs.
  * Using tables and graphs to compare sets of data and justify claims.
