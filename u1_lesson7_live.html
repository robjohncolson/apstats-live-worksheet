Topic 1.7: Topic 1.7

Summary Statistics for a Quantitative Variable
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Identify summary statistics that describe the center of a quantitative distribution.
  • Find and interpret the mean, median, first quartile, and third quartile.
  • Find and interpret the range, interquartile range, and standard deviation.
  • Use quartiles and the IQR to describe the middle 50% of a distribution.
  • Use two methods to identify possible outliers in a quantitative data set.
  • Distinguish between resistant and nonresistant summary statistics.
  • Choose appropriate measures of center and variability based on the shape of a distribution.
Essential Knowledge:
  • The mean is the average of the values in a data set, and the median is the middle value of an ordered data set.
  • If a data set has an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle values.
  • Q1 is the median of the lower half of the data and Q3 is the median of the upper half, excluding the overall median when the number of values is odd.
  • The range is the maximum minus the minimum and should be reported as a single value.
  • The interquartile range is Q3 minus Q1 and describes the spread of the middle 50% of the data.
  • Standard deviation describes the typical distance of the values from the mean.
  • The square of the standard deviation is the variance.
  • Possible outliers can be checked with the 1.5 IQR rule or by looking for values two or more standard deviations from the mean.
  • The median and IQR are resistant, while the mean, range, and standard deviation are nonresistant.
  • For skewed distributions, the median and IQR are usually best; for symmetric distributions, the mean and standard deviation are usually best.

Key Vocabulary

Summary StatisticsNumerical measures that describe a distribution.
MeanThe average value, found by adding all values and dividing by the number of values.
MedianThe middle value in an ordered data set.
First Quartile (Q1)The median of the lower half of the ordered data.
Third Quartile (Q3)The median of the upper half of the ordered data.
RangeThe difference between the maximum and minimum values.
Interquartile Range (IQR)The difference between Q3 and Q1; it measures the spread of the middle 50%.
Standard DeviationA measure of the typical distance each value is from the mean.
VarianceThe square of the standard deviation.
ResistantNot strongly affected by outliers or skewness.
NonresistantStrongly affected by outliers or skewness.
OutlierA value that is unusually far from the rest of the data.
