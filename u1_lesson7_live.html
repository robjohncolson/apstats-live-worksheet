Topic 1.7: Topic 1.7

Summary Statistics for a Quantitative Variable
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Calculate and interpret mean and median as measures of center for quantitative data.
  • Find and interpret Q1, Q3, range, IQR, and standard deviation for a quantitative distribution.
  • Use the 1.5 IQR rule and the 2 standard deviation rule to identify possible outliers.
  • Choose appropriate summary statistics based on skewness, symmetry, and resistance to outliers.
Essential Knowledge:
  • A statistic is a numerical summary of sample data.
  • The mean is the sum of all data values divided by the number of values, and the median is the middle ordered value.
  • Q1 and Q3 are quartiles that mark the middle 50% of the ordered data set.
  • Range, IQR, and standard deviation are measures of variability, and variance is the square of the standard deviation.
  • Mean, range, and standard deviation are nonresistant to outliers, while median and IQR are resistant.
  • For skewed distributions, median and IQR are preferred; for symmetric distributions, mean and standard deviation are preferred.

Key Vocabulary

StatisticA numerical summary calculated from sample data.
MeanThe average found by adding all values and dividing by the number of values.
MedianThe middle value in an ordered data set.
QuartilesValues that divide ordered data into sections, including Q1 and Q3.
Q1The median of the lower half of the ordered data.
Q3The median of the upper half of the ordered data.
RangeThe maximum minus the minimum.
IQRThe interquartile range, found by Q3 minus Q1.
Standard DeviationA measure of the typical distance each value is from the mean.
VarianceThe square of the standard deviation.
OutlierA value that is unusually far from the rest of the distribution.
ResistantA statistic that is not greatly affected by outliers.
NonresistantA statistic that changes a lot when outliers are present or removed.
