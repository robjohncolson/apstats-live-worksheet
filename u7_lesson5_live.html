AP Statistics Unit 7 Inference for Means

Topic 7.5: Carrying Out a Test for a Population Mean

Test Statistic, P-Value, Interpretation, and Conclusions
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Calculate the standardized test statistic for a one-sample t-test about a population mean.
  • Use degrees of freedom and the t-distribution to find or approximate a p-value.
  • Match the direction of the alternative hypothesis to the correct tail area for the p-value.
  • Interpret a p-value in context by assuming the null hypothesis is true.
  • Compare a p-value to a significance level to reject or fail to reject H0 and state a conclusion in context.
  • Carry out a complete significance test for a mean difference in a matched-pairs setting by analyzing the differences.
Essential Knowledge:
  • After conditions are checked, the standardized test statistic is statistic minus parameter divided by the standard error of the statistic.
  • For a one-sample test about a population mean, t = (x̄ − μ0) / (s / √n) and the degrees of freedom are n − 1.
  • The p-value is the probability, assuming H0 and the probability model are true, of getting a result as extreme or more extreme than the one observed.
  • Two-sided alternatives use probability in both tails, while one-sided alternatives use the tail named by the inequality in Ha.
  • If the p-value is less than or equal to α, reject H0; if the p-value is greater than α, fail to reject H0.
  • Matched-pairs data are analyzed by taking differences and then using a one-sample t-test for the population mean difference μD.

Key Vocabulary

Standardized Test Statistic A measure of how far the sample statistic is from the null value in standard-error units.
Degrees of Freedom For a one-sample t-test, the value n − 1 that determines which t-distribution to use.
p-Value The probability, assuming the null hypothesis is true, of getting evidence as extreme or more extreme than the evidence observed.
Two-Sided p-Value A p-value that adds probability from both tails because the alternative uses ≠.
One-Sided p-Value A p-value from just one tail because the alternative uses < or >.
Significance Level The cutoff value α used to decide whether a result is statistically significant.
Reject H0 The decision made when the p-value is small enough that the sample result would be unlikely if the null hypothesis were true.
Fail to Reject H0 The decision made when the p-value is too large to provide convincing evidence against the null hypothesis.
Matched Pairs A design in which two related measurements are taken together and then compared by their differences.
μD The true mean of the paired differences in a matched-pairs study.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. For a one-sample test about a mean, compute t = (x̄ − μ0) / (s / √n) and use n − 1 degrees of freedom.
  2. The direction of the alternative tells you whether the p-value comes from the left tail, right tail, or both tails of the t-distribution.
  3. Interpret the p-value assuming the null is true, then compare it to α to reject or fail to reject H0.
  4. Matched-pairs data are handled by taking differences and then carrying out a one-sample t-test for μD.
