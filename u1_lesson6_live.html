Describing the Distribution of a Quantitative Variable
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Describe a quantitative distribution using shape, center, variability, and unusual features.
  • Use shape vocabulary such as symmetric, skewed left, skewed right, unimodal, bimodal, and uniform.
  • Identify outliers, gaps, and clusters in a quantitative distribution.
  • Describe a distribution in context rather than using disconnected graph vocabulary.
Essential Knowledge:
  • Descriptions of quantitative distributions should include shape, center, variability (spread), and unusual features.
  • Outliers are unusually small or unusually large values relative to the rest of the data.
  • A distribution is skewed right when the right tail is longer, skewed left when the left tail is longer, and symmetric when both sides are mirror images.
  • A distribution with one main peak is unimodal, with two prominent peaks is bimodal, and with about the same frequency across values is approximately uniform.
  • A gap is a region with no observed data values, and clusters are concentrations of values usually separated by gaps.
  • Descriptive statistics summarize the data set at hand and do not automatically describe a larger population.

Key Vocabulary

DistributionThe overall pattern of values in a quantitative data set.
ShapeThe general form of a distribution, such as skewed or symmetric.
CenterA value that describes the typical response in a distribution.
Variability / SpreadHow closely packed or spread out the data values are.
SymmetricA distribution whose left and right sides are roughly mirror images.
Skewed LeftA distribution with a longer tail on the left side.
Skewed RightA distribution with a longer tail on the right side.
UnimodalA distribution with one main peak.
BimodalA distribution with two prominent peaks.
UniformA distribution where frequencies are about the same across values.
OutlierA value that clearly does not fit with the rest of the data.
GapA region in a distribution where no values appear.
ClusterA concentration of values in one part of a distribution.
ContextThe real-world variable being described, which should be named in the answer.
