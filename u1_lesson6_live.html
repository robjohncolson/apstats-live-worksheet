Topic 1.6: Topic 1.6

Describing the Distribution of a Quantitative Variable
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Identify the four characteristics used to describe the distribution of a quantitative variable.
  • Use vocabulary such as symmetric, skewed left, skewed right, unimodal, bimodal, and uniform to describe shape.
  • Describe center as the typical response in a distribution.
  • Describe variability or spread as how packed together or spread out the values are.
  • Identify unusual features such as outliers, gaps, and clusters.
  • Describe a quantitative distribution in context.
  • Apply distribution vocabulary to the Flint water crisis lead-level data.
Essential Knowledge:
  • A full description of a quantitative distribution should include shape, center, variability, and unusual features.
  • Symmetric distributions have left and right sides that are roughly mirror images.
  • Skewed-left distributions have a longer tail to the left, and skewed-right distributions have a longer tail to the right.
  • Unimodal distributions have one peak, while bimodal distributions have two peaks.
  • Uniform distributions have about the same frequency across values.
  • Center refers to a typical value in the distribution.
  • Variability or spread refers to how tightly packed or spread out the values are.
  • Unusual features may include outliers, gaps, and clusters.
  • Good statistical descriptions include the context of the variable being studied.

Key Vocabulary

DistributionThe overall pattern of values for a variable.
ShapeThe overall visual pattern of a distribution.
SymmetricA distribution whose left and right sides are about mirror images.
Skewed LeftA distribution with a longer tail on the left side.
Skewed RightA distribution with a longer tail on the right side.
UnimodalA distribution with one clear peak.
BimodalA distribution with two clear peaks.
UniformA distribution with about the same frequency across values.
CenterA value that represents the typical response in the distribution.
Variability / SpreadHow close together or spread out the values are.
OutlierA value that does not seem to fit with the rest of the data.
GapAn interval with no values.
ClusterA group of values that are packed closely together.
ContextThe real-world meaning of the variable being described.
