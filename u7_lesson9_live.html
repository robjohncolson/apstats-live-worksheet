Topic 7.9: Carrying Out a Test for the Difference of Two Population Means

Checking Conditions, Computing the Test Statistic, Finding the P-value, and Writing a Conclusion
Video Follow-Along Worksheet
Learning Objectives:
  • Check whether the conditions for a test about the difference of two population means are satisfied.
  • Compute the test statistic for a two-sample t test using sample statistics.
  • Interpret the sign and magnitude of a test statistic in context.
  • Find and interpret a P-value for a test about the difference of two means.
  • Use the P-value to decide whether to reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis.
  • Write a conclusion in context and explain why failing to reject H0 does not prove the means are equal.
Essential Knowledge:
  • After hypotheses are stated, carrying out a test includes checking conditions, calculating the test statistic, finding the P-value, and making a decision.
  • For a difference in two population means, the test statistic compares the observed sample difference to the null value of 0 using the estimated standard error.
  • The P-value is the probability, assuming the null hypothesis is true, of getting a result at least as extreme as the one observed.
  • If the P-value is less than or equal to the significance level, reject H0; otherwise, fail to reject H0.
  • The sign of the test statistic depends on the order of subtraction used for the sample means.
  • A non-significant result does not prove the population means are equal; it only means the sample does not provide convincing evidence of a difference.

Key Vocabulary

Two-Sample t Test A test used to compare two population means when the population standard deviations are unknown.
Conditions The assumptions that must be checked before using the test, such as random sampling, independence, and a large sample or roughly normal distribution.
Standard Error The estimated standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the difference in sample means.
Test Statistic A standardized value that tells how many standard errors the observed difference is from the null value.
Degrees of Freedom A value used with the t distribution when finding the P-value.
P-value The probability, assuming the null is true, of getting a result at least as extreme as the observed one.
Significance Level The cutoff, often 0.05, used to decide whether the evidence against the null is strong enough.
Reject H0 A decision made when the P-value is small enough to show convincing evidence against the null hypothesis.
Fail to Reject H0 A decision made when the sample does not provide convincing evidence against the null hypothesis.
Statistically Significant A description for a result when the P-value is less than or equal to the chosen significance level.
Conclusion in Context A final statement that refers to the population means and the original question, not just the sample values.

Key Takeaways

[Summary]
  1. To carry out a test for the difference of two population means, check conditions, calculate the test statistic, find the P-value, and make a decision.
  2. The test statistic compares the observed difference in sample means to the null value of 0 using the estimated standard error.
  3. The P-value is calculated under the assumption that the null hypothesis is true.
  4. If the P-value is greater than the significance level, fail to reject H0 and state that there is not convincing evidence of a difference.
  5. Failing to reject the null does not prove the population means are equal.
