You've already started

You're coming out of Probability & Statistics, so you're not starting from zero. A good chunk of our probability work (Unit 4) and some of how we describe data (Unit 1) will feel familiar.

What's new is the frame. AP Statistics is built around one question that every topic comes back to: what can we actually conclude from data, and how sure are we allowed to be? That shift — from computing answers to defending claims — is the whole course. The summer path below lets you move quickly through what you've seen and spend your energy where it's genuinely new.

  • My job is to keep the path clear and keep it open. That's the promise. The rest of this page is me holding up my end.

What I'll ask of you

Short version: do the work honestly, and don't move on while something's broken. Concretely —

  • Do the follow-along while you watch. Fill it in, don't just play the video. The watching isn't the learning; the doing is.
  • Predict, then retrieve. We bookend videos with quick Blooket rounds — a guess going in, a recall coming out. Both matter more than they look.
  • Don't just finish a quiz — understand it. A wrong answer is the most useful thing on the page. Dig into why until you could explain it to someone else.
  • Treat a weak quiz as information, not a verdict. A gap is just a signal — go back, shore it up, keep going. It still counts toward your grade; nothing is wasted.
  • Own your calculator. TI-84 fluency is a skill you build, not something you look up mid-test. The trainer turns it into muscle memory.
  • Show up for the Progress Check. It's in class, paced like the real exam. You do need to take it — skip it once it's due and your grade is capped — but walk in with the work done and it's upside, not a threat: it can lift your grade, never drag it down.

How your grade actually works

Make no mistake — this is for a grade. Everything you do here is tracked, and it all counts. There is no "busywork that doesn't matter" in this class.

All of Units 1–9 are open to you from day one, and you work at your own pace. The work has a destination, not a nightly deadline.

Your grade is built from two tracks, and it takes whichever one you're stronger at:

① Your work — your everyday graded pieces: each lesson's worksheet and quiz, your unit posters, and each lesson's Blooket (a small slice, scored on how many questions you answer and how accurate you are — answer them all correctly and it's full marks; the in-game points you race for are just for fun). Steady effort, banked as you go.

② The Progress Checks — one per unit, in class, AP-exam-style, and retakable until the quarter closes. Pure mastery: prove you know the material and this alone can carry you.

Your grade is the higher of the two. Lead with your strength — ace the AP checks or put in the steady work, and either path goes all the way to 100%.

One catch, and it's a low bar: you have to at least show up for both sides — about 40% on each (roughly D-level; an honest attempt clears it). Blow off a whole side once it's in play — never take a Progress Check that's due, or stop doing the work — and your grade is capped at 70% until you put in that minimum. It isn't a punishment for struggling; it's so no one can skip half the class and still claim full marks. Make a real attempt at both and the cap is gone — you keep the higher of your two scores.

So the move is simple: do the work at your pace, and take the Progress Checks when they come. Work ahead — over the summer, even — and that work stands on its own until a unit's Progress Check actually arrives in class; then the check is your shot to top it off. The ↑ number on each quarter is your ceiling — the best grade you can still reach if you ace what's left. As long as it's above where you are now, the door is open.
