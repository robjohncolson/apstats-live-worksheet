Welcome to AP Statistics

You're joining my class in the fall. Here's how it works, what I promise you, and what I'll ask of you in return — plus an optional head start if you want one.

You've already started

You're coming out of Probability & Statistics, so you're not starting from zero. A good chunk of our probability work (Unit 4) and some of how we describe data (Unit 1) will feel familiar.

What's new is the frame. AP Statistics is built around one question that every topic comes back to: what can we actually conclude from data, and how sure are we allowed to be? That shift — from computing answers to defending claims — is the whole course. The summer path below lets you move quickly through what you've seen and spend your energy where it's genuinely new.

My promise to you

  • A grade should tell the truth about what you know. One rough test day should not outweigh everything you can actually do. I've been the student with the real understanding and the disappointing grade, and it followed me further than it should have. I am not running that class.
  • This class is mastery-based, and the door back is always open. Your grade reflects what you've shown you can do — and there is no single moment that decides your fate. Fall behind, have a bad week, bomb a check: none of it is final.
  • Anyone here can earn full marks. Not by luck, not by retake roulette — by finishing the loop: learn it, show it, and where it's shaky, fix it and show it again. As many times as it takes. "Caring enough" isn't a personality trait here; it's a process, and the process is open to everyone.
  • My job is to keep the path clear and keep it open. That's the promise. The rest of this page is me holding up my end.

What I'll ask of you

Short version: do the work honestly, and don't move on while something's broken. Concretely —

  • Do the follow-along while you watch. Fill it in, don't just play the video. The watching isn't the learning; the doing is.
  • Predict, then retrieve. We bookend videos with quick Blooket rounds — a guess going in, a recall coming out. Both matter more than they look.
  • Don't just finish a quiz — understand it. A wrong answer is the most useful thing on the page. Dig into why until you could explain it to someone else.
  • Treat a weak quiz as information, not a verdict. A gap is just a signal — go back, shore it up, keep going. It still counts toward your grade; nothing is wasted.
  • Own your calculator. TI-84 fluency is a skill you build, not something you look up mid-test. The trainer turns it into muscle memory.
  • Show up for the Progress Check. It's in class, paced like the real exam — and it can only raise your unit grade, never lower it. Do the work first and it's upside, not a threat.

The rhythm

The year has a heartbeat. Once you feel it there are no surprises — every lesson is the same four beats:

Warm up
yesterday's Blooket · ungraded → Learn
video + worksheet · GRADED → Retrieve
today's Blooket · ungraded → Prove it
the lesson quiz · GRADED

Two pieces count toward your grade — the worksheet and the quiz. The Blookets bookend them as no-stakes practice: a prediction going in, a recall coming out. We move about 3–4 lessons a week, and every lesson opens by reviewing the last one — spaced repetition built into the schedule so you never have to engineer it yourself.

When a unit wraps, it closes with the Progress Check:

Progress Check
in class · two periods · AP-exam-style timing

It's paced like the real May exam — eased in gently early in the year, then held to full exam pace once you're strong. Practice for the thing that actually counts, run under the conditions that actually count.

How your grade actually works

Make no mistake — this is for a grade. Everything you do here is tracked, and it all counts. There is no "busywork that doesn't matter" in this class.

All of Units 1–9 are open to you from day one, and you work at your own pace. The work has a destination, not a nightly deadline.

Your unit grade is built up from the work itself — each lesson's worksheet and its AP-question quiz — accumulating as you complete them. Do the work and the grade is already there: finish a unit and you've earned its grade. You're not waiting on anything.

Then the unit's one Progress Check can only raise that grade — never lower it. Walk in with the work done and it's pure upside: a shot to push the number higher, with nothing to lose.

So the move is simple: do the work, at your pace. Do it ahead — over the summer, even — and you carry a real grade for that unit into the year before it starts, with the Progress Check waiting as a bonus, not a cliff.

★Your summer on-ramp — optional

There's no summer deadline, and skipping it now costs you nothing. But be clear on what this is: it's the graded work itself, just done early. Finish a unit over the summer and you've banked a real grade for it before day one — and its Progress Check becomes pure upside instead of a cliff.

How to do it without overthinking it:

  1. Open the Roadmap (the Desk). It lays out the lessons in order and tells you what's next, so you never have to plan anything.
  2. For each lesson tile: watch the video, fill in the worksheet, then take the quiz — the two graded pieces, both linked right from the tile. (Working solo over the summer, the worksheet's "class answers" panel stays quiet — that's expected; it wakes up in the fall.)
  3. Anytime you want: ten minutes on the TI-84 Trainer. Low effort, high payoff, works on your phone.

Where to start: Roadmap → Unit 1 → first lesson. That's the entire decision.
