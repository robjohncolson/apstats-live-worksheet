[03:31] — Interpreting the Confidence Level
A new title slide with just the title at the top.

[03:47] — Interpreting the Confidence Level (General Definition)
Text appears on the screen:
"In general, here is how to interpret a confidence level for a population parameter:"
"In repeated random sampling with the same sample size, approximately C% of those C% confidence intervals created will capture the population mean."

[04:05] — Interpreting the Confidence Level (Applied to Example)
The generic definition is replaced with an interpretation specific to the body temperature example:
"If we take many random samples of 10 people from the population of all humans and use each sample to construct a 95% confidence interval for the mean body temperature of all people, about 95% of those intervals would capture the population mean."

[04:33] — Interpreting the Confidence Level (Context Reminder)
A blue box appears with the text: "Remember to write in context. Always."
Certain phrases in the interpretation text are highlighted in blue to indicate the context: "10 people from the population of all humans", "for the mean body temperature of all people", and "about 95%".

[04:45] — Interpreting the Confidence Level (Common Mistake)
The text changes to explain a common misconception:
"The confidence level describes what happens in repeated sampling. It does NOT give the probability that a particular interval captures the population proportion." (Note: The speaker says "population mean", but the slide text says "proportion").
