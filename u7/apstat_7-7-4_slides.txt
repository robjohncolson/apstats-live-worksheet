[01:02] — 2009 Question 4
This slide presents a practice problem from the 2009 AP Exam.
The text reads:
"One of the two fire stations in a certain town responds to calls in the northern half of the town, and the other fire station responds to calls in the southern half of the town. One of the town council members believes that the two fire stations have different mean response times. Response time is measured by the difference between the time an emergency call comes into the fire station and the time the first fire truck arrives at the scene of the fire.
Data were collected to investigate whether the council member's belief is correct. A random sample of 50 calls selected from the northern fire station had a mean response time of 4.3 minutes with a standard deviation of 3.7 minutes. A random sample of 50 calls selected from the southern fire station had a mean response time of 5.3 minutes with a standard deviation of 3.2 minutes.
(a) Construct and interpret a 95 percent confidence interval for the difference in mean response times between the two fire stations.
(b) Does the confidence interval in part (a) support the council member's belief that the two fire stations have different mean response times? Explain."
As the presenter speaks, key phrases are highlighted with yellow and green boxes: "two fire stations", "northern half", "southern half", "believes that the two fire stations have different mean response times", "random sample of 50 calls", "mean response time of 4.3 minutes with a standard deviation of 3.7 minutes", "random sample of 50 calls", and "mean response time of 5.3 minutes with a standard deviation of 3.2 minutes".

[04:17] — 2009 Question 4, Part (a) - Interpretation
This slide provides the final written interpretation of the confidence interval calculated on the previous slide.
"Interpretation:
I am 95% confident that the difference in the population mean response times for the two fire stations (northern - southern) is between -2.37 minutes and 0.37 minutes."

[04:34] — 2009 Question 4, Part (b)
This slide moves to part (b) of the question: "(b) Does the confidence interval in part (a) support the council member's belief that the two fire stations have different mean response times? Explain."
It uses the interval found in part (a) to answer the question:
"From part (a), the 95% CI is (-2.37, 0.37).
Since 0 is within the interval, it is a plausible value for the difference in the mean response times.
Therefore, this confidence interval does not support the council member's belief that there is a difference in mean response times for the two fire stations."
A warning is displayed in bold red text at the bottom: "NOTE: Do not say the council member is wrong!"

[05:46] — 2009 Question 4, What If ...?
This slide poses an additional, hypothetical question related to the problem: "Sometimes you are asked to interpret the confidence LEVEL instead of the confidence interval."
It proposes a "part (c)": "(c) Interpret the meaning of the 95% confidence level for this interval."
The provided interpretation is:
"If all possible random samples of 50 calls from each fire station were selected and a 95% confidence interval was constructed from each pair of samples, then 95% of these intervals would succeed in capturing the difference (northern - southern) in the mean response times for all calls from the two fire stations."
