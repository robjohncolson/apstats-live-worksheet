[00:23] — Example: They just won't go away!
Text describes the scenario: "The large black and yellow Argiope aurantia spiders are found in most of the contiguous United States. These spiders often display sexual dimorphism, meaning that the male and female spiders differ physically. To investigate if there is a difference in the mean body length of male and female Argiope spiders, a researcher measured the length of a random sample of 15 female spiders and a random sample of 17 male spiders."

[00:35] — Example: They just won't go away!

Female: n = 15, x̄ = 25.267, sx = 3.535

Male: n = 17, x̄ = 15.224, sx = 2.193
Below the table is the prompt: "Calculate and interpret a 95% confidence interval for the difference in the population mean body lengths of female and male spiders."

[00:46] — Interpret a Confidence Interval
A bullet point states: "In general, here is how to interpret a confidence interval for a population parameter." Below it is a boxed template with fill-in-the-blanks:
"We are ___C___% confident that the interval from ___lower bound___ to ___upper bound___ captures the ___value to be estimated___."

[01:05] — Example: They just won't go away!
The slide summarizes previous calculations: "From Topic 7.6 Video 2, the 95% confidence interval for the Argiope spiders example was 7.956 mm to 12.129 mm."
The standard interpretation is provided: "We are 95% confident that the interval from 7.956 to 12.129 mm captures the difference (female – male) in the true mean body lengths of all female and male Argiope spiders."
A warning box appears: "Include the direction that you subtracted!"

[01:45] — Example: They just won't go away!
The slide updates to show an "Alternate Interpretation": "I am 95% confident that the true mean body lengths of all female Argiope spiders is between 7.956 and 12.129 mm larger than the mean body length of all male Argiope spiders."

[02:02] — Example: They just won't go away!
The slide addresses subtracting in the reverse order: "If we estimate the difference in the mean body lengths by subtracting in the opposite order (Male - Female) the 95% confidence interval would be (-12.129, -7.956)." A text box notes: "This is not incorrect!"

[02:29] — Example: They just won't go away!
The corresponding interpretation for the reverse subtraction is added: "I am 95% confident that the true mean body length of all male Argiope spiders is between 7.956 and 12.129 mm smaller than the mean body length of all female Argiope spiders."

[02:40] — Incorrect Interpretations
The first incorrect interpretation is shown: "We are 95% confident that the interval from 7.956 to 12.129 mm captures the difference (female - male) in the mean body lengths of these Argiope spiders." A red 'X' appears, and an arrow points to the word "these." A box explains: "This interpretation refers to the samples instead of the parameter. We don't need to estimate the difference in sample means! We know everything about the samples. The parameter is what we estimate."

[03:22] — Incorrect Interpretations
A second incorrect interpretation is shown: "There is a 95% chance that the interval from 7.956 to 12.129 mm captures the difference (female - male) in the true mean body lengths of female and male Argiope spiders." A red 'X' appears, and an arrow points to the phrase "95% chance." A box explains: "The confidence level of 95% is not a probability of capturing the population parameter. Any confidence interval either includes the parameter of interest or does not include the parameter."

[03:54] — Justify a Claim
The text describes a follow-up study: "The researchers in this study hypothesize that male spiders tend to be smaller than female spiders so that they can maneuver around the webs and habitats faster against the effects of gravity. This makes finding a mate easier. To further study this phenomenon, they wish to identify various spider species where the females are larger than the males on average. Is there convincing evidence that Argiope spiders should be included in this follow-up study?"

[04:30] — Justify a Claim
A box appears with the conclusion: "If the female and male Argiope spiders were equal in size, then the difference in their mean body lengths would be 0. Because 0 is not on our 95% confidence interval (7.956, 12.129), 0 is not a plausible value for the difference in the population mean body lengths of the female and male spiders. Therefore, we DO have convincing evidence that the female Argiope spiders have significantly larger body lengths than males, on average. Argiope spiders should be included in the follow-up study."
