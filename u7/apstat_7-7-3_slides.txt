[01:00] — Let's Practice!
The full text for the 2009 AP Exam Question 4 is displayed:
"A council member of a large city was concerned about the time it took for fire engines to respond to calls sent to the single fire station in the city. The city council used a 95 percent confidence interval to estimate the mean response time, in minutes, for a random sample of fire calls. As a result of this interval, the city council decided to build a second fire station in the city. The two fire stations are located on opposite sides of the city—one in the northern half and the other in the southern half.

The council member wants to know if the two fire stations have different mean response times. A random sample of 50 calls is selected from the northern fire station, and the time it takes a fire engine to respond to each call is recorded. The mean and standard deviation of these 50 response times are 4.3 minutes and 3.7 minutes, respectively. A random sample of 50 calls is also selected from the southern fire station, and the time it takes a fire engine to respond to each call is recorded. The mean and standard deviation of these 50 response times are 5.3 minutes and 3.2 minutes, respectively.

(a) Construct and interpret a 95 percent confidence interval for the difference in mean response times between the two fire stations.
(b) Does the confidence interval in part (a) support the council member's belief that the two fire stations have different mean response times? Explain."

[01:54] — Part (a)

[02:35] — Conditions
Three condition checks are listed, each receiving a green checkmark as the speaker verifies them:

"Random samples of 50 calls from the northern fire station and 50 calls from the southern fire station."

"50 calls ≤ 10% of all calls from the northern fire station AND 50 calls ≤ 10% of all calls from the southern fire station."

"Both samples are large enough (n_N = 50 > 30, n_S = 50 > 30) that the sampling distribution of the difference in sample means is approximately normal according to the Central Limit Theorem."
